<p>Scientists from Utrecht, Leiden, and Munich discovered that electrons can form stable pairs at much higher temperatures than previously understood—a critical requirement for superconductivity that can now occur at room temperature. The finding, published in Science, suggests that electron pairing is not the obstacle preventing room-temperature superconductivity; instead, a different mechanism is holding it back. Conventional superconductors require cooling to approximately -260°C (liquid helium temperatures). High-temperature superconductors discovered in the mid-1980s function around -173°C, but their underlying mechanism remains poorly understood and cannot be explained by the theory governing conventional superconductivity. The breakthrough indicates that achieving room-temperature superconductivity may be possible if researchers can identify and overcome the remaining barrier beyond electron pairing.</p>

<div><a href="news/science/2026-11-18/#2026-himalayan-glacial-collapse-killed-hundreds-in-nepal-tibet-border-region">2026 Himalayan glacial collapse killed hundreds in Nepal-Tibet border region</a>
<p>On August 26, 2026, the lower section of the Langtang Lirung Glacier collapsed at an altitude of 5,200 meters in the Himalayan region along the Nepal-Tibet border. The glacier break triggered a cascade of ice, rock, and debris that swept down the valley floor to approximately 3,800 meters, generating massive landslides and catastrophic flash floods downstream. The collapse killed at least 162 people in Nepal according to police reports, with China&#x27;s Xinhua News Agency reporting 3 dead in Tibet as of August 27. Approximately 1,000 people remained missing across both regions: Nepal reported 403 missing (including 341 foreigners), while Tibet reported 558 missing (including 260 foreigners and 2 villagers rescued). The U.S. Geological Survey determined that what was initially suspected to be an earthquake in the region was in fact seismic energy generated by the collapse itself, followed by debris flow. Satellite imagery from August 24 (before) and August 26 (after) confirmed the glacier break. The disaster occurred in Nepal&#x27;s Nuwakot district and surrounding areas; communities were given little warning, with floods arriving within hours. The incident represents the second major glacial-flood event in the Lhende Khola river system within 14 months, following similar floods in August 2025 that killed 9 people.</p>

<p>A critical remote code execution vulnerability in Perforce Puppet tracked as CVE-2026-115731 (CVSS 9.4) is being actively exploited. The vulnerability is a deserialization of untrusted data issue in the report processing of Puppet Enterprise and Puppet Core that allows clients holding a certificate signed by the Puppet certificate authority to send reports with malicious payloads executed on the server with service user permissions. A successful attack could expose the server&#x27;s certificate-signing key, allow issuance of trusted certificates, and enable changes to software and configuration delivered to managed systems. The flaw was patched on November 3 alongside CVE-2026-115732. Patched versions include Puppet Enterprise and Puppet Enterprise Advanced 2026.0.1, 2025.12.1, and 2023.8.13, and Puppet Core 9.1.3 and 8.22.2. The vulnerability was flagged as exploited on November 4. The US cybersecurity agency CISA added CVE-2026-115731 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on November 17, urging federal agencies to patch within two weeks per BOD 26-04. The vulnerability also affects OpenVox configuration management platform. Administrators are advised to disable auto-signing of certificate requests and enforce strong validation.</p>
