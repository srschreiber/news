<div><a href="news/space/2026-11-12/#nasa-perseverance-rover-confirms-organic-compounds-in-ancient-martian-rock-samples">NASA Perseverance rover confirms organic compounds in ancient Martian rock samples</a>
<p>NASA&#x27;s Perseverance rover confirmed complex organic carbon in two ancient Martian rocks located in the Bright Angel formation within Jezero crater, according to research published in Science Advances on November 12, 2026. The organic material was detected just beneath the rock surface using the SHERLOC (Scanning Habitable Environments with Raman &amp; Luminescence for Organics &amp; Chemicals) instrument, which employs Raman spectroscopy to identify minerals and organic compounds without damaging samples. The detected material is macromolecular carbon (MMC), consisting of large interconnected networks of carbon atoms. MMC can form through biological activity or non-biological chemical processes, so its presence alone cannot confirm past life on Mars. The surrounding geology indicates the material may have entered or formed during at least two separate periods of Mars&#x27; geological history, billions of years ago when water flowed through a river channel into Jezero crater and carried sediment that hardened into mudstone. The organic carbon was found alongside carbonate and sulfate minerals that developed as water altered the rocks. The study was co-led by Ashley Murphy, a postdoctoral researcher at the Planetary Science Institute, and Kyle Uckert, Deputy Principal Investigator of the SHERLOC instrument. This finding builds on a September 2025 NASA announcement that Perseverance had identified a potential biosignature in the same rocks.</p>
