<div><a href="news/health/2026-11-21/#ai-models-may-not-replicate-human-brain-processes-despite-predicting-brain-activity">AI Models May Not Replicate Human Brain Processes Despite Predicting Brain Activity</a>
<p>Researchers at York University discovered that artificial intelligence models can predict brain activity patterns but may rely on fundamentally different internal processes than the human brain uses. The study, led by Assistant Professor Kohitij Kar, challenged the assumption that AI systems work like the brain simply because they can successfully predict neural responses. The team developed a method called &quot;reverse predictivity testing&quot; to test whether the relationship between AI and brain activity works bidirectionally—if AI truly operated like the brain, brain activity should be able to predict what happens inside AI models as effectively as AI predicts brain activity. The research examined responses to 1,320 natural photographs and realistic synthetic images featuring 10 familiar subjects (bear, elephant, face, apple, car, dog, chair, plane, bird, zebra) against various backgrounds, plus 300 additional images showing the same subjects in different visual styles (outlines, drawings, simplified representations, artistic interpretations). The findings raise questions about the reliability of using these AI models as tools for understanding how the brain&#x27;s visual system actually processes information, despite their widespread use in neuroscience research over the past decade.</p>
