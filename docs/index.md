<p>Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, announced on November 20, 2026, that he has discovered a proof of the Riemann hypothesis using Anthropic&#x27;s Claude AI. The Riemann hypothesis, first proposed in 1859 by German mathematician Bernhard Riemann, is one of mathematics&#x27; most famous unsolved problems and is worth a $1 million Clay Mathematics Institute Millennium Prize. Alpöge stated he also believes the proof confirms the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, a broader expansion of Riemann&#x27;s conjecture. The announcement caps a year during which AI systems from OpenAI and Anthropic made breakthroughs on numerous open mathematical problems, including OpenAI&#x27;s solutions to the Navier-Stokes problem and over 700 papers claiming solutions to 372 open problems. This announcement came one day after Anthropic&#x27;s stock market debut.</p>

<div><a href="news/science/2026-11-23/#ancient-quasar-pair-from-cosmic-dawn-defies-galaxy-formation-models">Ancient quasar pair from cosmic dawn defies galaxy formation models</a>
<p>Astronomers discovered Nāpōwawāʻenakaulua (officially J1602+4228AB), the oldest known compact pair of quasars, existing 900 million years after the Big Bang. The two black holes powering the quasars have masses of 1.5 billion and 400 million solar masses respectively. The pair holds the record for the closest separation between two known quasars from cosmic dawn. Their rapid growth to such massive sizes in the early universe contradicts current galaxy formation models, which cannot explain how black holes became so large so quickly. The discovery combined observations from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and the Gemini North telescope on Maunakea, Hawaii. Research was led by Silvia Onorato, a postdoctoral research associate at NSF NOIRLab, and published in The Astrophysical Journal Letters. Paired quasars are theoretically expected to be relatively common due to frequent galaxy encounters and mergers, but finding and studying them is exceptionally difficult, making this close pair from such an early cosmic epoch particularly remarkable.</p>

<div><a href="news/security/2026-11-23/#critical-binary-parser-vulnerability-enables-javascript-code-injection">Critical binary-parser vulnerability enables JavaScript code injection</a>
<p>A critical code injection vulnerability has been disclosed in the binary-parser library, tracked as CVE-2026-134814 with a CVSS score of 9.2 (also referenced as 8.1). The flaw affects binary-parser versions prior to 2.3.1 and allows attackers who control parser definitions to execute arbitrary JavaScript code in the Node.js process. The vulnerability stems from insufficient validation of user-controlled string options (length, lengthInBytes, key, tag, offset, and values passed to namely()) that are inserted directly into generated JavaScript code without proper sanitization. According to maintainer Keichi Takahashi, the affected options are now validated as identifier paths (e.g., foo, $parent.len), with expressions like &#x27;len - 1&#x27; rejected in favor of functions. This is an incomplete fix for the earlier CVE-2026-1245 (CVSS 6.5), a similar code injection vulnerability disclosed in January 2026.
