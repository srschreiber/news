<div><a href="news/climate-change/2026-11-17/#15c-warming-target-declared-unachievable-climate-policy-in-crisis">1.5°C warming target declared unachievable; climate policy in crisis</a>
<p>The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) declared in September 2026 that the 1.5°C global warming target set by the Paris Agreement is no longer achievable. The world has already warmed approximately 1.4°C above pre-industrial levels. UNEP&#x27;s 2026 Emissions Gap Report states that breaching the 1.5°C threshold is now imminent and inevitable. To remain under 1.5°C, total emissions from 2026 onward cannot exceed 130 billion tonnes of CO2, but with current emissions at roughly 40 billion tonnes per year and still rising, this carbon budget will be exhausted in approximately three years. The only remaining pathway identified by UNEP would require global CO2 emissions to fall steadily from 2025 to zero by 2031—described as infeasible under current policies. Under current trajectories, the world is headed for 2.6°C warming by century&#x27;s end. UNEP&#x27;s new focus is limiting the peak and duration of overshoot, with the goal of returning global temperatures to 1.5°C by the end of the century. This would require global greenhouse gas emissions reductions of 18% by 2030, 45% by 2035, and 69% by 2040 relative to 2025 levels.
