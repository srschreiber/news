<p>The World Health Organization (WHO) has warned that growing antimicrobial resistance (AMR) could cause 39 million deaths over the next 25 years without urgent action. Senior WHO officials stated on November 16, 2026, that progress in prevention, diagnosis, and appropriate antibiotic use remains too slow to meet global targets. In 2021 alone, antimicrobial resistance was directly responsible for more than 1 million deaths and linked to approximately 4.7 million additional deaths. WHO surveillance data from 2023 show that one in every six laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections flagged to the organization was resistant to antibiotics used to treat it. An investigation of 183 countries revealed that nearly nine in ten now have national action plans against AMR (up from about four in ten in 2017), but only 42 countries—less than one in five—have plans that are financed, implemented, and monitored. WHO officials warned that antimicrobial resistance threatens to make routine surgery and childbirth life-threatening and poses a threat to &#x27;the future of modern medicine.&#x27; The WHO announced the release of two new diagnostic tools to help countries strengthen diagnostic capabilities: one defining tests that should be available at every healthcare system level, and another compiling 73 laboratory procedures from sample collection to pathogen identification.</p>

<div><a href="news/tech-research/2026-11-16/#agentic-robotics-emerges-with-gpt-6-astra-integration">Agentic Robotics Emerges with GPT-6 Astra Integration</a>
<p>GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of agentic robotics, a new paradigm where frontier models with agentic frameworks matched or exceeded task-specific policies on many embodied benchmarks within weeks of release. The system operates in a closed loop: observing the environment, interpreting user intent, reasoning about the task, invoking skills and tools, verifying outcomes, and adapting strategy. Rather than training dedicated policies for each task family, agentic robotic systems apply foundation models to open-world embodied tasks. The research defines Embodied System 2—a foundation model combined with an embodied harness—as the decision-making core. Central challenges include translating general-purpose reasoning into effective and verifiable actions under physical constraints including limited reaction time, mechanical limits, unpredictable environment changes, and irreversible consequences. The community lacks a shared definition, reliable evaluation framework, or research roadmap; researchers propose an evaluation framework measuring both model capabilities and closed-loop system performance, plus a research roadmap toward sustained autonomy ranging from executing individual primitives to fulfilling standing roles.</p>
