### Globally and intergenerationally unequal exposure to hourly heat extremes
<span class="study-type">paper</span> <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> <span class="evid evid-5" title="Evidence 5/5" aria-label="Evidence strength 5 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i></span>

A Nature Climate Change study finds that under high-emissions warming, global hourly heat extremes will quadruple by century's end, with population exposure rising sixfold—far worse than daily temperature averages suggest—and low- and middle-income countries bearing the brunt.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Hourly heat extremes projected to rise 4x by late century under RCP8.5</li>
<li>Population exposure to hourly heat extremes projected to rise 6x</li>
<li>Each hot day gains ~4 extra hot hours; 3 hot hours occur even on non-hot days</li>
<li>Low- and middle-income countries bear over 75% of current and projected exposure</li>
<li>Younger generations in poorer countries face much higher lifetime heat exposure</li>
</ul>
