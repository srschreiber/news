### Globally and intergenerationally unequal exposure to hourly heat extremes
<span class="study-type">paper</span> <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> <span class="evid evid-5" title="Evidence 5/5" aria-label="Evidence strength 5 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i></span>

A study in Nature Climate Change finds hourly heat extremes will quadruple globally by century's end under high emissions, with population exposure rising sixfold and low- and middle-income countries bearing the brunt.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Global hourly heat extremes projected to rise 4x by end of century under high emissions</li>
<li>Population exposure to hourly heat extremes projected to rise 6x</li>
<li>Each hot day gains ~4 extra hot hours, plus 3 hot hours on non-hot days missed by daily metrics</li>
<li>Low- and middle-income countries already bear over 75% of heat exposure</li>
<li>Younger generations, especially in low-income countries, face far higher lifetime exposure than earlier cohorts</li>
</ul>
