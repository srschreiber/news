### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> MIT researchers develop air-stable, ultrathin superconductors for scalable quantum devices
MIT researchers have discovered a method to create large, uniform areas of ultrathin superconducting material (niobium diselenide) that remains stable in air by growing it underneath graphene. This breakthrough addresses a major obstacle to using these materials in practical quantum technologies by protecting the fragile superconductor from oxidation while enabling wafer-scale production.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Superconducting material grows underneath graphene, protecting it from air oxidation</li>
<li>Technique produces uniform, continuous films over large areas suitable for quantum devices</li>
<li>Addresses manufacturing challenge that previously made ultrathin superconductors impractical for applications</li>
</ul>

Doyeon Kim, an undergraduate at Sungkyunkwan University, published research on UAV-NAS, an autonomous AI architecture search system for drone identification on low-power FPGA chips. The technology achieves 88.7% reduction in battery energy consumption compared to traditional CPU implementations, enabling practical real-time drone surveillance in military and industrial applications.

<li>Undergraduate researcher Doyeon Kim developed UAV-NAS system using neural architecture search</li>
<li>Battery consumption reduced by 88.7% versus CPU-based approaches</li>

<li>Technology enables 24/7 drone monitoring without battery concerns</li>
