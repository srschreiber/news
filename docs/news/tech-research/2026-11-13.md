## AI Bias

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Study: GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, DeepSeek and Qwen Show Racial and Sex Bias in Pain Management Recommendations
A counterfactual audit tested six major language models—GPT-4.1 mini, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama, DeepSeek, and Qwen—for racial and sex bias in pain management. Pain-severity recommendations showed no significant differences; opioid decisions lacked precision for detection but revealed extreme response instability across models.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Models tested: GPT-4.1 mini, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama, DeepSeek, Qwen</li>
<li>Pain-severity differences within 1 point on 0-10 scale after correction</li>
<li>Q-Pain comparisons: 237 of 238 within 0.25 points</li>
<li>Opioid precision insufficient to exclude 20 percentage point differences</li>
<li>Response instability much larger than between-patient variation</li>
<li>Reference patient received high opioid doses in 6%-93% of responses</li>
<li>Study by Richard J. Young and Alice M. Matthews, submitted Nov 11, 2026</li>
</ul>

A mechanistic interpretability study identifies where uncertainty emerges in large language models during computation, establishing four observation lenses to connect UQ methods to LLM internal structure. The framework advances understanding of when model outputs can be trusted.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Lenses ordered by information content, connected via residual stream</li>
<li>Framework links uncertainty estimation to LLM internal computation</li>
<li>Survey of existing UQ methods through four-lens taxonomy</li>
<li>Focus: determining when LLM responses can be trusted for safe deployment</li>
<li>Submitted Nov 11, 2026 by Xiaoyu Liang et al.</li>

## LLM Evaluation

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> ShadowBench: Exposing Lexical Anchoring Bias in LLM Knowledge Evaluation
ShadowBench reveals that large language models rely heavily on exact entity names for knowledge retrieval, masking fundamental gaps when names change or are paraphrased. Performance drops exceed 20% across all model scales; machine unlearning algorithms achieve only superficial lexical erasure with 60-90% retention via implicit attribute correlation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>ShadowBench: Dual-Trait Association (DTA) task design</li>
<li>Performance drop &gt;20% when entity names removed/paraphrased</li>
<li>Applies to frontier models GPT-5.4 and Claude-Sonnet-4.6</li>
<li>Unlearning algorithms (Gradient Difference, NPO) show superficial effects</li>
<li>Popular entities explicitly reconstructed in 74%+ of internal reasoning</li>
<li>Niche entities: 60-90% shadow accuracy retained despite 0% LELR</li>
<li>Accepted at NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track</li>
</ul>

Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15925) <span class="src-badge src-research">AI Web Searched</span>, [arXiv — Computation and Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15925) <span class="src-badge src-rss">RSS</span>
