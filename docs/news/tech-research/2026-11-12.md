### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> MIT Develops Faster Training Method for Climate Prediction AI Models
MIT researchers developed a training method for climate emulator AI models that significantly reduces production time while maintaining trustworthiness and adaptability. The technique uses only a fraction of the data typically required for training by employing carefully selected synthetic data. Climate emulators are specialized AI models that simulate the effects of human activities on climate and can predict impacts on temperatures or sea level; they run in seconds, allowing scientists to test thousands of varied climate scenarios efficiently. The new method enables emulators trained with smaller datasets to accurately predict outcomes of scenarios not encountered during training, whereas traditional emulators failed to adapt to new situations. The research was led by Christopher Womack (PhD '26, now a postdoc at ETH Zurich) and includes contributions from Shahine Bouabid, Andrei Sokolov, Popat Salunke, and Glenn Flierl at MIT. The technique is designed to accelerate the creation of more reliable emulators for policy-relevant climate scenario modeling.
