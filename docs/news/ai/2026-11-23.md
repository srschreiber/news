### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Self-modifying AI models can edit their own weights
Researchers at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign report that a 27B parameter open language model can rewrite its own weights without gradients or fresh training runs, with edits that persist in the checkpoint. The model performs a concrete loop: reads failures, proposes a weight edit, tests it, and retains only edits that pass. On held-out math problems, the model achieved +6.6 accuracy points on 490 pre-registered tasks across eight sequential self-edit sessions. On image benchmarks, the model repaired performance from 7% to ~70% after matrix noise corruption. The 27B scale was necessary—smaller models at 2B, 4B, and 9B failed or self-broke during the process. The work required only one GPU and hours of compute rather than a full training cluster. The research is documented in arXiv:2611.21885 and titled "Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights."
