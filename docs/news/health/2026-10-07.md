## Scientific Achievement

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 9/10" aria-label="Importance 9 of 10">9</span> Nobel Prize in Chemistry awarded for mirror-image molecule research
Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for their work on molecular chirality and asymmetric synthesis.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Kagan&#x27;s 1986 breakthrough: designing reactions to produce one chiral form over its mirror image</li>
<li>Soai created first chemical reaction producing only one mirror-image form (Soai reaction, demonstrated 2003)</li>
<li>Over half of clinically employed drugs are chiral molecules</li>
<li>Previously, chemists could only produce &#x27;racemates&#x27; with equal mixtures of both mirror forms causing side effects or different therapeutic effects</li>
<li>Kagan (Université Paris-Sud, France) and Soai (Tokyo University of Science, Japan)</li>
<li>Prize citation: &#x27;discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis&#x27;</li>
<li>Solves century-old puzzle of achieving homochirality synthetically</li>
<li>Kagan&#x27;s 1986 key step; Soai&#x27;s 1995 publication and 2003 demonstration of Soai reaction</li>
</ul>

## Medical Research

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> UCSF Scientists Reprogram Immune Cells Inside the Body to Fight Cancer
UC San Francisco researchers developed a method to create cancer-fighting CAR-T cells directly inside a patient's body using a two-particle delivery system to send CRISPR tools and cancer-targeting DNA into T cells. This avoids weeks of external engineering and costs of $400,000–$500,000 per patient. Mouse experiments with humanized immune systems successfully targeted aggressive leukemia, multiple myeloma, and solid tumors.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First insertion of large DNA stretch into T cells without body removal</li>
<li>CRISPR delivery uses two-particle system into precise genomic location</li>
<li>Current CAR-T therapy costs $400,000–$500,000 per patient, requires weeks processing</li>
<li>Mouse studies targeted aggressive leukemia, multiple myeloma, solid tumors</li>
<li>Performs better than conventional viral-based random insertion strategies</li>
</ul>
