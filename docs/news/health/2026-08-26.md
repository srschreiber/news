### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Scientists identify protein C3 as shortcut to calorie restriction's anti-aging benefits
Yale researchers identified complement protein C3 as a driver of age-related inflammation, finding that blocking it in mice reduced inflammation. A study of 42 CALERIE trial participants showed moderate calorie restriction (11–14% reduction) significantly lowered C3 levels, suggesting C3 targeting could replicate some anti-aging benefits without severe dietary restriction.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>C3 is an immune protein driving age-related chronic inflammation</li>
<li>Moderate calorie restriction (11–14% over 2 years) significantly lowered C3</li>
<li>White adipose tissue is primary source of C3 production affected by diet</li>
<li>Blocking C3 in mice reduced age-related inflammation</li>
<li>Research remains early stage; animal studies confirm mechanism</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Early childhood sugar exposure linked to cancer risk and aging 70 years later
A study of over 64,000 Britons used postwar sugar rationing as a natural experiment to show that reduced sugar exposure during the first 1,000 days of life correlates with lower cancer rates and slower biological aging decades later. Liver cancer rates were approximately 69% lower, and breast cancer 36% lower in those with longer rationing exposure.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study tracked 64,000+ people born 1951–1956 using sugar rationing as natural experiment</li>
<li>Liver cancer approximately 69% lower with extended early-life rationing</li>
<li>Breast cancer 36% lower with extended early-life rationing</li>
<li>Extended rationing also linked to longer telomeres, indicating slower biological aging</li>
<li>People with longer rationing maintained healthier diets into adulthood around age 50</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Childhood trauma leaves epigenetic marks in brain cells that increase stress sensitivity
Researchers at Washington University and Princeton University identified an epigenetic mechanism by which early-life stress alters brain stress response through elevated SETD7 enzyme in dopamine neurons, loosening DNA packaging around stress-related genes. The team blocked this effect in mice, preventing heightened anxiety in adulthood.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Early-life stress elevates SETD7 enzyme in ventral tegmental area dopamine neurons</li>
<li>SETD7 loosens DNA packaging, making stress-related genes more accessible</li>
<li>Epigenetic change primes brain to overreact to future stressors</li>
<li>Researchers blocked effect in mice, preventing heightened adult anxiety</li>
<li>Over half of children worldwide experience early-life stress</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Shingles Vaccine Linked to Reduced Heart Disease Risk in 'Natural Experiment'
Oxford researchers found that people aged 60+ who received Shingrix (newer recombinant shingles vaccine) versus Zostavax showed 9% lower cardiovascular burden over seven years in a natural experiment using electronic health records. This follows the team's 2024 finding linking Shingrix to lower dementia risk.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>9% lower cardiovascular burden over seven years</li>
<li>72,000 vaccinated people aged 60+</li>
<li>Composite measure: coronary heart disease, heart attacks, strokes, heart failure</li>
<li>Shingrix approved October 2017, replacing Zostavax</li>
<li>Earlier 2024 study linked vaccine to lower dementia risk</li>
</ul>
