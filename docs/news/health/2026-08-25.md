### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Shingrix vaccine linked to 24% lower dementia risk in study
A study of over 509,000 older adults found that those who received Shingrix, the recombinant shingles vaccine, were 24% less likely to develop dementia over four years—approximately one case prevented per 17 vaccinated people. The analysis used Medicare claims and electronic health records from skilled nursing facility admissions between 2017 and 2022.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>24% lower dementia risk in vaccinated cohort</li>
<li>18.8% dementia rate in vaccinated vs 24.6% in unvaccinated (509,926 adults age 66+)</li>
<li>One dementia case prevented per ~17 people vaccinated</li>
<li>Shingrix is the only shingles vaccine currently available (introduced 2017)</li>
<li>Study used target trial emulation to recreate randomized trial conditions from observational data</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Depression disrupts neuron production in the hippocampus, study finds
Columbia University researchers found that adults with major depressive disorder show disrupted neurogenesis in the hippocampus, impairing pattern separation and causing negative memories and emotions to blur together. This shifts understanding of depression from a neurotransmitter deficiency to a condition affecting neurons' adaptive capacity.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First evidence that depression stalls neuron production in the hippocampus</li>
<li>Disrupted neurogenesis impairs pattern separation—the ability to distinguish similar memories</li>
<li>Impaired pattern separation causes distinct memories and emotions to blur, promoting negative interpretation</li>
<li>Pattern separation requires adult neurogenesis, established in mice and suggested in human brain tumor patients</li>
<li>Shifts depression model from neurotransmitter deficiency to neuronal adaptation disorder</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Supercentenarians possess unusual abundance of cytotoxic immune cells
Supercentenarians aged 110 and older possess unusually high levels of CD4 cytotoxic T lymphocytes, rare immune cells capable of killing abnormal cells. Study analysis of 28 adults across three age groups revealed CD4 CTL proportions rising from 4% at ages 70–99 to 17.6% at age 110+, with clonal expansion patterns suggesting targeted immune selection.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>CD4 CTL levels: 4% (ages 70–99), 9.6% (ages 100–109), 17.6% (ages 110+)</li>
<li>Largest individual clone averaged 33.3% of all CD4 CTLs; one centenarian showed single clone at 53.8%</li>
<li>Dominant clone sequences matched sequences from lung, breast, and liver cancer patients</li>
<li>None of the supercentenarians in study were cancer-diagnosed</li>
<li>Study published August 19, 2026 in Cell Reports; examined 28 adults across three age groups</li>
</ul>

The University of Missouri School of Medicine and MU Health Care have enrolled their first patient in a clinical trial using Eye90 microspheres, a radiopharmaceutical therapy manufactured on campus for treating unresectable liver tumors. The trial marks a significant milestone in bringing a radioisotope-based cancer treatment from development through human testing at a single institution.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First patient enrolled Feb. 9, 2026 at Ellis Fischel Cancer Center</li>
<li>Eye90 microspheres use yttrium-90 (Y-90) radioisotope to treat unresectable liver tumors including hepatocellular carcinoma</li>
<li>MURR (University of Missouri Research Reactor) is the only domestic source of Y-90 and manufactures the microspheres for ABK Biomedical</li>
<li>Eye90 enables precise tumor visualization during delivery to minimize radiation exposure to healthy tissue</li>
</ul>
