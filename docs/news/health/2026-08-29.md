## Diet & Metabolism

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Ketogenic diet reduces liver fat by 67% in clinical trial
A ketogenic diet reduced liver fat by 67% in people with obesity and fatty liver disease, significantly outperforming Mediterranean (45% reduction) and low-fat diets in a clinical trial. All three groups lost approximately 10% of body weight over four to five months, with about half of ketogenic participants no longer meeting prediabetes criteria.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>67% liver fat reduction on ketogenic diet vs. 45% on Mediterranean and low-fat diets</li>
<li>Ketogenic diet: 4% carbs, 73% fat; Mediterranean: 50% carbs, 35% fat; Low-fat: 70% carbs, 15% fat</li>
<li>All groups achieved ~10% body weight loss over 4–5 months</li>
<li>~50% of ketogenic group reversed prediabetes status</li>
<li>Published August 27, 2026, in Cell Metabolism; led by Samuel Klein at Washington University</li>
</ul>

## Health

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Sleep micro-awakenings linked to early Alzheimer's risk in healthy adults
Researchers found that frequent micro-awakenings during sleep correlate with higher genetic risk for Alzheimer's in middle-aged adults, potentially serving as an early warning signal years before symptoms appear. The study of over 500 healthy adults suggests sleep measurements could help identify vulnerable individuals, though genetic risk scores remain low and cannot predict individual outcomes.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study included 500+ healthy adults aged 18–69</li>
<li>Micro-awakenings linked to higher polygenic risk scores in ages 50–69, not younger adults</li>
<li>Research published in journal Sleep</li>
<li>Polygenic scores are low and don&#x27;t predict individual disease development</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Researchers identify protein brake blocking nerve repair in spinal injuries
Scientists identified the AHR protein as a molecular brake that suppresses nerve regeneration after spinal and peripheral nerve injuries. Blocking AHR in mouse models enhanced axon regrowth and improved motor recovery, suggesting a therapeutic strategy to shift injured neurons from protection toward active repair.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>AHR protein inhibits nerve fiber regeneration in mouse models</li>
<li>Blocking AHR increased axon growth and restored movement and sensation</li>
<li>Active AHR protects neurons but suppresses growth protein production</li>
<li>Study published in Nature journal</li>
<li>HIF-1α factor involved in regeneration pathway</li>
</ul>

## Health Risk

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Long-term melatonin use linked to 90% higher heart failure risk
A large preliminary study found chronic insomnia patients using melatonin long-term had approximately 90% higher risk of developing heart failure over five years. The findings raise questions about the long-term safety of the over-the-counter supplement, which lacks FDA regulation for strength and purity.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>90% elevated heart failure risk over 5 years in chronic melatonin users with insomnia</li>
<li>Associations also found with heart failure hospitalization and all-cause mortality</li>
<li>Data from TriNetX Global Research Network covering electronic health records</li>
<li>Study does not establish causation</li>
<li>OTC melatonin supplements lack FDA regulation for potency consistency</li>
</ul>
