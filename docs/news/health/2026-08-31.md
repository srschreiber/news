### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Liver Regeneration Blocked After Alcohol Use Halts
Researchers discovered that alcohol-related liver damage traps hepatocytes in a dysfunctional state, preventing regeneration even after drinking stops. The dysfunction stems from inflammation disrupting RNA splicing, a process critical for converting genetic instructions into functional proteins. Targeting this pathway could restore the liver's repair capacity, offering hope beyond transplantation—currently the only life-saving option for advanced alcohol-related liver disease.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Inflammation-driven RNA splicing errors prevent liver regeneration post-alcohol exposure</li>
<li>Alcohol-related liver disease causes ~3 million deaths annually worldwide</li>
<li>Study conducted at U of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, Duke, and Chan Zuckerberg Biohub Chicago</li>
<li>Findings published in Nature Communications</li>
</ul>
