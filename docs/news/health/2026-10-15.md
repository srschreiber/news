### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Amyloid Removal in Alzheimer's Brain Shows Protective Effect on Tau
An autopsy study of an Alzheimer's patient revealed that brain regions successfully cleared of amyloid plaques showed little or no tau buildup, while neighboring amyloid-containing areas showed high tau levels and neurodegeneration. The findings suggest that removing amyloid plaques early may provide lasting protection against tau accumulation and slow cognitive decline.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Brain regions cleared of amyloid plaques showed little or no tau buildup</li>
<li>Neighboring areas still containing amyloid showed much higher tau levels</li>
<li>Study presented at 2026 Alzheimer&#x27;s Association International Conference and published in JAMA</li>
<li>Donanemab (Kisunla) and lecanemab (Leqembi) received full FDA approval for amyloid-targeting</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Stroke Rates Nearly Double Among Younger Adults Over Three Decades
Stroke rates among adults ages 20 to 54 nearly doubled over three decades in the Greater Cincinnati and Northern Kentucky region, rising 83% from 33.9 cases per 100,000 person-years in 1993–1994 to 62.2 in 2020. Ischemic strokes drove most of the increase, with associations found to hypertension, diabetes, and substance use, though underlying causes remain unclear.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Stroke rates rose 83% from 1993–1994 to 2020 in young adults aged 20–54</li>
<li>Ischemic strokes rose from 23.8 to 47.3 cases per 100,000 person-years</li>
<li>2,076 adults experienced first stroke during study period spanning 1993–2020</li>
<li>Associations with hypertension, diabetes, and substance use identified</li>
<li>Study published September 23, 2026 in Neurology journal</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Cancer incidence rising in younger UK adults despite improved lifestyle factors
A major study found that 16 of 22 cancer types rose significantly in younger women aged 20–49 between 2001 and 2019, and 11 of 21 cancers in younger men—a trend that persists despite improvements in smoking, diet, and other established risk factors. Researchers identified obesity as a partial but insufficient explanation, pointing to potential roles for microbiome changes and early-life exposures.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>16 of 22 cancers increased significantly in younger women; 11 of 21 in younger men</li>
<li>Bowel and ovarian cancers rose only in younger adults, not older age groups</li>
<li>Excess body weight cannot fully account for the observed increases</li>
<li>Study analyzed England data from 2001–2019 across 20+ cancer types</li>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Ozempic and Wegovy Linked to Rare Risk of Sudden Vision Loss
Rutgers University researchers published findings in Annals of Internal Medicine linking GLP-1 receptor agonists—including semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound)—to a modestly increased risk of ischemic optic neuropathy, a rare condition causing sudden and potentially permanent vision loss. Risk increase was approximately 3–4 additional cases per 10,000 patients over 18 months, more common among men and adults aged 50–65.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Ozempic, Wegovy (semaglutide) and Mounjaro, Zepbound (tirzepatide) linked to ischemic optic neuropathy</li>
<li>Risk increase: ~3–4 additional cases per 10,000 patients over 18 months</li>
<li>Condition causes sudden vision loss; more common in men and adults 50–65</li>
<li>Study analyzed large US database of Type 2 diabetes patients ages 18–65</li>
</ul>
