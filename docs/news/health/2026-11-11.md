### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Energy drinks linked to 11× higher cardiac arrest reports in new study
A study presented at the American Heart Association's Scientific Sessions 2026 found energy drinks and stimulant supplements associated with 11× more cardiac arrest reports and 4.5× more heart attack reports. Young adults ages 18–34 comprised nearly half of severe cases; some products contain 3–12× coffee's caffeine concentration.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Energy drinks/stimulants vs. products without: 11× cardiac arrest reports, 4.5× heart attack reports</li>
<li>Young adults (18–34): nearly 50% of severe cardiovascular emergencies</li>
<li>Some energy drinks: 3× coffee&#x27;s caffeine per fluid ounce; supplements 12× coffee&#x27;s concentration</li>
<li>FDA classifies as foods/supplements, exempting from drug safety testing requirements</li>
<li>Energy drink industry sales 2024: $20+ billion (double from decade prior)</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Bioengineered chewing gum reduces HPV levels by up to 93% in cancer patients
University of Pennsylvania scientists developed a bioengineered chewing gum containing plant-based proteins that reduced HPV levels by up to 93% in laboratory tests using oral samples from head and neck cancer patients. The gum also nearly eliminated two harmful bacterial species while preserving beneficial bacteria.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>HPV reduction: up to 93% in lab tests on patient oral samples</li>
<li>Bacterial impact: nearly eliminated Porphyromonas gingivalis and Fusobacterium nucleatum; preserved beneficial oral bacteria</li>
<li>HPV, Pg, Fn linked to oropharyngeal cancer development and worsened survival</li>
<li>Published in Scientific Reports</li>
<li>Potential for affordable treatment supporting cancer prevention and existing therapies</li>
</ul>
