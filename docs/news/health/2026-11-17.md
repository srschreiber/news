### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Vietnam Malaria Drug Research Adopted as WHO Global Standard
Research conducted by the Oxford University Clinical Research Unit (OUCRU) and Ho Chi Minh City Hospital for Tropical Diseases over 35 years led to development of artemether and artesunate drugs for treating severe malaria caused by P. falciparum parasite. Initial studies in the early 1990s compared artemether to quinine (the previous standard treatment), finding artemether cleared parasites faster with fewer side effects. A 2010 trial of 370 adults found artesunate cleared parasites faster than artemether, with mortality rates of 7% and 13% respectively. Research also demonstrated artemisinin derivatives were well tolerated during pregnancy without increasing miscarriage or birth defect risk compared to quinine, even in first trimester. The mortality rate from severe malaria at the hospital fell from over 40% in the early 1990s to below 10% by 2000. Research findings provided evidence for the World Health Organization to include artesunate in its global guidelines for treating severe malaria. The collaboration began in December 1990 through a Memorandum of Understanding between the Hospital for Tropical Diseases and the Wellcome Trust (United Kingdom). Supporting care enhancements included arterial blood gas analysis and continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) for patients with multiple organ failure.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> U.S. loses measles elimination status
The United States lost its measles elimination status on November 17, 2026, following a determination by the Pan American Health Organization, a regional World Health Organization body. The country had held elimination status since 2000. The U.S. reported more than 5,000 measles cases in 2026, the first time case counts exceeded that level since 1991. The loss of elimination status is largely symbolic but formalizes endemic transmission occurring across the country. Measles elimination designation is conferred on countries that prevent persistent virus spread, though imported cases and limited transmission chains can occur without loss of status if chains are stopped within one year. Susan Kansagra, chief medical officer for the Association of State and Territorial Health Officials, stated "Losing measles elimination status is a tragic loss for our country" and noted the announcement formalizes what public health departments have been observing. Jarbas Barbosa, PAHO director, stated "Elimination must be continuously protected." The announcement has drawn political backlash, with Democrats attacking the president of the United States administration, particularly health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., over response to recent outbreaks and messaging around vaccination benefits.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> GLP-1 drugs like Ozempic and Mounjaro show potential neuroprotective effects on the brain
Researchers from the University of Gothenburg and University of Copenhagen reviewed existing scientific evidence on GLP-1 medications (semaglutide and tirzepatide), marketed as Ozempic and Mounjaro, and their potential effects on brain health. Animal studies suggest GLP-1 drugs can influence learning and memory. Observational analyses of patient health records found associations between GLP-1 treatment and reduced dementia risk among people with type 2 diabetes, though these cannot prove causation. However, Novo Nordisk's EVOKE and EVOKE+ clinical trials found that semaglutide failed to slow progression of early Alzheimer's disease, despite affecting several biological markers associated with the condition. Researchers hypothesize that GLP-1 medications may still offer cognitive benefits to people with obesity or type 2 diabetes by improving blood sugar regulation, metabolism, or vascular health—conditions that affect attention, planning, and information processing speed. The mechanism by which GLP-1 drugs might influence the brain remains unclear, potentially resulting from improvements in metabolic health, direct brain actions, or a combination. Researcher Stina Börchers emphasized the need to determine whether earlier treatment initiation could make a difference and to identify which patients might benefit.
