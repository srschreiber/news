### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Stanford researchers discover brain activity patterns predicting Alzheimer's years early
Stanford researchers discovered that subtle brain activity patterns can predict Alzheimer's disease risk years before memory problems or protein buildup occurs. A scoring system based on differences between Alzheimer's patients and 'super agers' predicted future cognitive decline more effectively than established biomarkers including genetic risk and amyloid-beta levels.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study published Nov 4, 2026, in Science Advances</li>
<li>Brain activity patterns predicted cognitive decline more effectively than demographics, genetics, amyloid-beta, tau biomarkers</li>
<li>Cohort: adults 55–70 assessed; follow-up periods extended to 12 years</li>
<li>Participants with Alzheimer&#x27;s-like patterns more likely to experience decline, accumulate amyloid-beta, receive Alzheimer&#x27;s diagnosis</li>
<li>Super ager patterns associated with maintained cognitive abilities and avoided outcomes</li>
<li>Unlike conventional biomarkers, brain patterns may be modifiable via cognitive training and transcranial magnetic stimulation</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Yale study reveals prescription drugs and exercise slow aging; supplements ineffective
Yale researchers analyzing 51 studies found that certain prescription drugs, healthy diets, and exercise can measurably slow biological aging markers, while popular over-the-counter supplements showed little effect. The study, published in Nature Medicine, represents the first demonstration of measurable impacts on biological aging—though changes in markers do not necessarily predict longer lifespan or disease prevention.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Analyzed 51 studies on anti-aging interventions</li>
<li>Measured using DNA-based blood tests for biological aging</li>
<li>Prescription drugs, healthy diet, and exercise showed measurable effects</li>
<li>Popular over-the-counter anti-aging supplements largely ineffective</li>
<li>Changes in biological age markers do not necessarily mean longer lifespan or disease avoidance</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Supervised Rehabilitation Exercise Program May Prevent Early Osteoarthritis After ACL Surgery
A La Trobe University trial with 184 adults aged 18–40 found that a four-month supervised rehabilitation program improved pain, muscle strength, and quality of life after ACL surgery without evidence of cartilage damage. Results published in Annals of Internal Medicine challenge concerns that exercise damages vulnerable knee cartilage.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Trial: 184 adults aged 18–40; 1–3 years post-ACL reconstruction with persistent symptoms</li>
<li>Intervention: four-month progressive exercise therapy and education</li>
<li>Outcomes: improved pain, muscle strength, quality of life; no cartilage damage or worsening early osteoarthritis</li>
<li>Background: ~50% of ACL patients develop early-onset knee osteoarthritis within decade (often before age 40)</li>
<li>Study significance: first to evaluate combined exercise therapy 9+ months post-surgery</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> University of Missouri Launches First In-Person Clinical Trial for Nuclear Medicine Device
The University of Missouri began the first clinical trial of Eye90 microspheres—yttrium-90 embedded glass beads designed to treat unresectable liver tumors. The trial launched February 9, 2026, at Ellis Fischel Cancer Center and leverages on-campus production at the nation's largest university research reactor.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Trial began Feb 9, 2026, at Ellis Fischel Cancer Center</li>
<li>Device: Eye90 microspheres (yttrium-90 embedded radiopaque glass beads) for unresectable liver tumors and hepatocellular carcinoma</li>
<li>Manufacturing: on-campus at University of Missouri Research Reactor (MURR), sole domestic Y-90 source</li>
<li>Advantage: radiopaque design enables precise visualization during delivery; localized radiation with minimal healthy tissue exposure</li>
<li>Partnership: ABK Biomedical; first radiopharmaceutical clinical trial at Ellis Fischel</li>
</ul>
