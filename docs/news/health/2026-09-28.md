### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Immune activation outside the brain drives Alzheimer's neurodegeneration pathway
Washington University scientists discovered that T cells driving Alzheimer's neurodegeneration are activated in lymph nodes before entering the brain. Blocking the signals preparing these T cells in lymph nodes dramatically reduced neurodegeneration and preserved cognitive abilities in mice, potentially offering a more accessible treatment target than blood-brain barrier therapies.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>T cells contributing to Alzheimer&#x27;s activated in lymph nodes outside brain</li>
<li>Blocking lymph-node signals reduced neurodegeneration and preserved cognition in mice</li>

<li>Offers more accessible treatment target than therapies requiring blood-brain barrier passage</li>
