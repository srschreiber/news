### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Meta-analysis finds dairy trans fats pose no heart disease or diabetes risk
A meta-analysis of 22 studies found that naturally occurring trans fats in dairy products do not increase risk of heart disease, stroke, or type 2 diabetes. The distinction is critical—industrial trans fats remain harmful, while dairy trans fats showed no adverse effects.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>22-study meta-analysis examined dairy trans fats</li>
<li>10 controlled trials: participants consumed 1.3–13.2g trans fat daily</li>
<li>12 long-term cohorts: up to two decades, no adverse associations</li>
<li>Natural dairy trans fats differ from harmful industrial trans fats</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Hundreds of Fathers Die Within Five Years of Their Children's Birth, Many Preventably
A Northwestern University study of over 130,000 births in Georgia found 796 fathers died within five years following birth, with 60% from potentially preventable causes: homicide, accidents, suicide, and overdose. This marks the first major medical journal examination of paternal mortality following birth.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>796 fathers died within 5 years of 130,267 births (2017–2022)</li>
<li>~478 deaths preventable: 143 homicide, 142 accidents, 102 suicide, 93 overdose</li>
<li>296 fathers died from natural causes</li>
<li>First major medical journal study of paternal post-birth mortality</li>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Breakthrough kidney transplant procedure expands donation options
Researchers led by the Medical University of Vienna used Teclistamab, a cancer drug, to dramatically reduce transplant-rejection antibodies in a patient with zero probability of finding a compatible donor, enabling a successful kidney transplant after 12+ years on the waiting list. The breakthrough expands options for highly sensitized patients previously considered untransplantable.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>37-year-old dialysis patient with zero compatible-donor probability (cPRA value)</li>
<li>12+ years on waiting list with two previous failed transplants</li>
<li>Teclistamab treatment over 31 weeks achieved sustained antibody reduction</li>
<li>Case published in New England Journal of Medicine, July 2026</li>
<li>Led by Georg Böhmig and Martina Schatzl at MedUni Vienna</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> FDA-approved epilepsy drug lacosamide shows promise in reversing osteoarthritis damage
Yale researchers found that lacosamide, an FDA-approved epilepsy drug, blocks Nav1.7 protein to reduce osteoarthritis pain while reversing cartilage damage in preclinical studies. Delivered via hydrogel directly into joints, it addresses both pain and structural deterioration—an unmet need in current osteoarthritis treatment.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Lacosamide blocks Nav1.7 sodium channel dysregulated in osteoarthritis</li>
<li>Reduces pain signaling and cartilage breakdown simultaneously</li>
<li>Current treatments (NSAIDs, steroid injections) reduce symptoms only, do not prevent damage</li>
<li>Specialized hydrogel delivery enables sustained pain relief without opioids</li>
<li>Led by Chuan-Ju Liu, Yale School of Medicine; published in Bioactive Materials</li>
</ul>
