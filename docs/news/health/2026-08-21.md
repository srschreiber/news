### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Merck and Moderna's personalized melanoma vaccine extends cancer-free survival in phase three trial
A new personalized mRNA-based vaccine (Intismeran) combined with the immunotherapy drug Keytruda has shown promising results in stopping skin cancer recurrence in patients with high-risk melanoma. The phase three trial of over 1,000 patients demonstrated the combination significantly extended cancer-free survival and reduced disease spread compared to Keytruda alone.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Vaccine created using mRNA technology targeting individual patient tumor mutations</li>
<li>Combination with Keytruda significantly extended cancer-free survival and reduced metastatic spread</li>
<li>Treatment still requires peer review, regulatory approval, and NHS evaluation before availability</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Six 30-second sprints produce greater metabolic changes than 90 minutes of cycling
Rockefeller University researchers found that six 30-second all-out sprints altered nearly one-quarter of measured blood proteins and over 200 metabolites, far exceeding metabolic changes from 90 minutes of moderate cycling. Many sprint-responsive proteins were linked to lower cardiovascular and metabolic disease risk, with over one-quarter associated with slower biological aging.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Six 30-second sprints altered ~25% of measured blood proteins</li>
<li>Moderate cycling altered &lt;0.25% of proteins measured</li>
<li>Sprints triggered 200+ metabolite changes</li>
<li>32 of 33 disease-protective proteins altered by sprinting; only 3 by cycling</li>
<li>&gt;25% of protective proteins linked to slower biological aging</li>
<li>Response persisted after 8 weeks of training</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> At-home colorectal cancer screening reduces mortality risk by 43% in long-term Swedish study
A 14-year Swedish study of 376,000 participants found that completing at-home colorectal cancer screening reduced mortality risk from the disease by 43%. The screening program offered stool-based tests every two years to detect blood, with positive results triggering further testing.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study tracked 376,511 participants up to 14 years</li>
<li>43% mortality risk reduction from completing screening</li>
<li>1,668 colorectal cancer deaths recorded</li>
<li>Screening offered every two years to ages 60-74</li>
<li>Published in JAMA Network Open by Karolinska Institutet and Umeå University</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> GDF15 hormone protects liver from inflammation without weight loss
McMaster University researchers found that GDF15, an appetite-suppressing hormone, protects the liver from inflammation and scarring through a brain-to-liver pathway independent of weight loss. The hormone activates nervous system signaling that triggers glucocorticoid release, suppressing liver inflammation and slowing fibrosis progression.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>GDF15 activates brain-to-liver signaling pathway</li>
<li>Liver protection occurs independently of weight reduction or food consumption</li>
<li>Glucocorticoids suppress inflammation and slow fibrosis progression</li>
<li>Published in Cell Metabolism, August 10, 2026</li>
<li>Study used mouse models of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH)</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Gut bacteria compound butyrate trains intestine to fight inflammation
Northwestern Medicine researchers discovered that butyrate, a short-chain fatty acid produced by gut bacteria fermenting dietary fiber, reprograms intestinal cells to maintain long-term anti-inflammatory protection. Two weeks after treatment withdrawal, mice showed sustained elevated IL-10 levels and greater resistance to colitis.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Butyrate induces sustained IL-10 production in CD4+ T-cells</li>
<li>Protective effect persisted 2 weeks after treatment withdrawal</li>
<li>Protection dependent on IL-10 signaling, not microbiome changes</li>
<li>Effect observed in germ-free mice lacking microbes</li>
<li>Published in Nature Communications</li>
</ul>
