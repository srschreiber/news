### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Personalized Cancer Vaccine Prevents Skin Cancer Recurrence in Trial
Merck and Moderna announced early phase 3 trial results showing their personalized cancer vaccine Intismeran, combined with Keytruda immunotherapy, extended disease-free survival in high-risk melanoma patients compared to Keytruda alone. The mRNA-based vaccine sequences individual patient tumors to direct immune system targeting. Over 1,000 patients participated; full results remain unpublished and peer review pending.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Phase 3 trial involved 1,000+ patients with high-risk stage 2, 3, or 4 melanoma</li>
<li>Intismeran (personalized vaccine) + Keytruda reduced risk of disease spreading</li>
<li>Uses mRNA technology targeting individual tumor DNA</li>
<li>Full results not yet published or peer-reviewed</li>
<li>Testing underway for lung, bladder, and kidney cancers</li>
</ul>

Female immune cells show greater genetic activity in inflammatory pathways, providing stronger infection defense but increasing autoimmune disease susceptibility.

<li>Women 9 times more susceptible to lupus than men</li>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> AI-designed intrabodies open potential treatments for neurodegenerative diseases
University of Essex researchers developed AI-designed intrabodies—antibody fragments engineered to function inside human cells—with potential applications for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, Huntington's disease, and motor neurone disease. Using Nobel Prize winner David Baker's AI protein redesign software, researchers converted 672 antibodies into stable intrabodies. Research published in Nature Communications with molecules made freely available to scientists.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>AI converted 672 different antibodies into intrabodies</li>
<li>Led by Dr. Caitlin O&#x27;Shea and Dr. Gareth Wright, University of Essex</li>
<li>Electrical charge identified as key stability factor within cells</li>
<li>Research published in Nature Communications</li>
<li>Targets Alzheimer&#x27;s, Parkinson&#x27;s, Huntington&#x27;s, motor neurone disease</li>
<li>Over 1 million UK patients affected by neurodegenerative diseases</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Schizophrenia linked to widespread synaptic loss with asymmetric brain damage
A brain imaging study of 122 participants found that schizophrenia is characterized by widespread loss of synapses across frontal and temporal regions, with particularly pronounced loss in the left hemisphere. The synaptic loss pattern, measured via PET imaging, does not match volume changes seen on standard MRI, suggesting separate biological processes underlie the disease.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study of 29 schizophrenia patients and 93 healthy controls using PET imaging</li>
<li>Widespread synaptic loss in frontal, temporal, memory, and emotion-related brain regions</li>
<li>Left hemisphere showed considerably greater synaptic loss than right</li>
<li>Brain regions with greatest losses contain high serotonin, GABA, and glutamate receptors</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Brain's frontoparietal cortex dynamically adapts information routing
University of Iowa neuroscientists discovered that the frontoparietal cortex, the brain's decision-making hub, dynamically adjusts its connectivity with other brain regions based on task requirements rather than maintaining fixed connections. Using computational modeling and brain imaging, researchers found the cortex reorganizes in real-time to filter and prioritize information, functioning like an air traffic controller.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Frontoparietal cortex dynamically adjusts connectivity based on decision-making needs</li>
<li>Research led by Kai Hwang, University of Iowa Department of Psychological and Brain Sciences</li>
<li>Connectivity patterns shift in real-time, not fixed</li>
<li>Implications for ADHD and schizophrenia research</li>
<li>Functions like air traffic controller filtering brain signals</li>
</ul>
