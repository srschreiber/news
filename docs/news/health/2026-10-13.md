### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Scientists Map 539 Distinct Regions in Brain Using Lipid Analysis
Scientists at EPFL created the first detailed 3D lipid map of the brain, identifying 539 distinct chemical zones ('lipizones') using mass spectrometry and machine learning. The atlas reveals highly organized lipid arrangements that traditional brain mapping missed and offers new insights into neurological conditions like depression and Alzheimer's disease.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>539 chemically distinct lipid zones (&#x27;lipizones&#x27;) identified in brain</li>
<li>172 different lipids analyzed across 109 brain slices from 11 mice</li>
<li>Generated approximately 7 million measurements</li>
<li>Lipid composition distinctive enough to identify tissue sample origin</li>
<li>Changes in brain lipids linked to depression and Alzheimer&#x27;s disease</li>
<li>Published in Nature</li>
</ul>

## Medicine

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> FDA approves first drug specifically targeting Alexander disease after 30 years of research
The FDA approved Zanvastro on October 12, 2026, the first drug directly targeting Alexander disease, a rare neurological disorder affecting fewer than one in a million people. The drug emerged from over 30 years of research and demonstrated stabilization and improvement of motor function in trials.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First drug directly targeting Alexander disease</li>
<li>Zanvastro is disease-modifying, not symptom-management</li>
<li>Over 30 years of research led by Albee Messing (University of Wisconsin-Madison)</li>
<li>Developed with Ionis Pharmaceuticals collaboration</li>
<li>Affects fewer than 1 in 1 million people worldwide</li>
<li>Patients showed motor function stabilization and movement improvements in trials</li>
</ul>

## Paleontology

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> 165-Million-Year-Old Jurassic Predator Fossil Reveals Mammalian Brain Evolution Path
A 165-million-year-old Jurassic predator fossil, Megacauda sungei, reveals how early mammals developed a sophisticated soft palate separating mouth from pharynx, enabling nursing while breathing. This anatomical feature provided crucial survival advantage supporting brain development in nursing young.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>165-million-year-old Megacauda sungei fossil discovered</li>
<li>Measured approximately 20 inches long</li>
<li>Resembled modern otter, beaver, or platypus</li>
<li>Possessed sophisticated soft palate enabling swallowing while breathing</li>
<li>Teeth featured backward-curving cusps for gripping prey</li>
<li>Published in Nature; research from Shenyang, Chicago, and Bonn</li>
</ul>
