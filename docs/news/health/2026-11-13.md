### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Large pregnancy study shows Ozempic and Wegovy linked to premature birth risk when used for diabetes
A Danish study of 756,636 pregnancies found that GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, liraglutide) were not linked to increased pregnancy complications when prescribed for weight loss. However, when prescribed for diabetes, early exposure was associated with higher premature birth risk, likely driven by diabetes itself rather than the medications.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study of 756,636 pregnancies in Denmark (Oct 2009–Dec 2023)</li>
<li>No increased complications when GLP-1s used for weight loss</li>
<li>Higher premature birth risk (before 37 weeks) with diabetes prescriptions</li>
<li>Published in Human Reproduction Open</li>
<li>Current clinical guidance: stop GLP-1s 8 weeks before pregnancy planning</li>
<li>Guidance based on early-phase lab studies, not real-world evidence</li>
<li>Diabetes, not medication, likely drives premature birth risk</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Study reveals viral infections cause lasting immune system damage despite symptom resolution
Research at Pompeu Fabra University found that mice recovering from acute viral infections experienced lasting immune system damage despite eliminating the virus. Structural changes in the spleen persisted and impaired immune responses to new threats, but vaccination prior to infection prevented these harmful changes.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study published in Cell Death &amp; Disease</li>
<li>Lasting immune damage observed despite viral clearance</li>
<li>Spleen changes included fibrosis and disrupted immune cell positioning</li>
<li>T cell activation impaired</li>
<li>Pre-infection vaccination prevented harmful immune system changes</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Stopping statins after age 70 may be safer than expected, but long-term risks remain
A study of nearly 1,900 adults aged 70+ found stopping statins appeared relatively safe short-term for people without prior heart attacks or strokes, but longer-term follow-up revealed higher rates of serious cardiovascular events or death. The findings contradict assumptions underlying current statin use in older adults.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study of ~1,900 adults aged 70+</li>
<li>Primary prevention without prior cardiovascular events</li>
<li>All participants had ≥2 chronic conditions</li>
<li>Follow-up: ~2 years, some extending to 4 years</li>
<li>Short-term discontinuation appears relatively safe</li>
<li>Longer follow-up: higher serious CV events/death rate</li>
<li>American Heart Association 2026 guideline recommends lifestyle, potentially medication for younger people</li>
</ul>
