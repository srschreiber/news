### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> SpaceX Dragon returns with cancer research and 3D-printed blood vessels
SpaceX Dragon spacecraft completed an uncrewed return to Earth on November 21, 2026, splashing down in the Pacific Ocean near Los Angeles at 2:03 PM PST after undocking from the International Space Station on November 20 at 11:05 AM CST. The capsule carried scientific experiments including frozen tumor organoids that underwent experimental cancer treatments in microgravity, and a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct designed to study vascular aging and cardiovascular diseases. The mission also returned equipment used to study how specialized lighting affects astronauts' sleep during extended space missions. The cargo was part of NASA's 35th SpaceX commercial resupply mission. The experiments are intended to advance understanding of how microgravity affects living organisms, human cells, and biological processes, with potential applications for protecting astronauts on future long-duration missions and treating cancer and cardiovascular diseases on Earth.

## Medical Research

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Acute myeloid leukemia originates 50+ years before diagnosis, study reveals
Researchers at the University of Cambridge and University College London discovered that acute myeloid leukemia (AML) can begin developing over 50 years before diagnosis. The study, published in Nature, analyzed blood samples from women in the UK Collaborative Trial of Ovarian Cancer Screening (UKCTOCS), examining 47 women who developed AML (average diagnosis age 72) and 46 women who remained cancer-free. In two-thirds of cases, the first cancer-driving genetic mutation appeared before age 20 and remained largely unnoticed for decades. The research used DNA sequencing and mathematical modeling to work backward and determine when mutations first emerged, how quickly affected cells multiplied, and how they competed with other blood cells as disease approached. Mutated cells grew in predictable patterns that accelerated as additional mutations accumulated. The findings suggest doctors may eventually identify people at risk years or decades before symptoms appear, potentially enabling prevention by slowing abnormal blood cell growth. AML is an aggressive blood cancer affecting approximately 2,700 people annually in the UK, with only about one in six patients surviving at least five years after diagnosis.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Type 2 diabetes linked to 53% higher Alzheimer's risk in major review
A systematic review and meta-analysis of 40 studies led by Curtin University found that people with type 2 diabetes face a 53% higher adjusted relative risk of developing Alzheimer's disease compared to those without type 2 diabetes. The research estimated that approximately 4.7 per 1,000 people with type 2 diabetes develop Alzheimer's disease annually. The study, titled "Incidence and risk of Alzheimer's disease in individuals with type 2 diabetes: a systematic review and meta-analysis," was published in Alzheimer's Research & Therapy in 2026. Lead researcher Dr. Emmanuel Adewuyi from Curtin School of Population Health noted that this review differed from previous analyses by focusing specifically on type 2 diabetes and Alzheimer's disease (rather than combining multiple diabetes types or dementia forms) and by measuring both relative risk and incidence of new cases to provide a clearer picture of Alzheimer's burden in the type 2 diabetes population.
