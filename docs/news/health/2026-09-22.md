## Health/Neuroscience

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Brain dopamine damage identified as cause of long COVID fatigue
PET brain imaging at CAMH revealed long COVID damages dopamine-releasing neurons in the striatum, correlating dopamine loss patterns with specific symptoms: motivation loss, movement slowing, and memory difficulties. The finding opens potential treatment strategies targeting the dopamine system.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Long COVID affects approximately 5% of global population (~2 million people in Canada)</li>
<li>Lower ventral striatum dopamine linked to loss of motivation</li>
<li>Dorsal putamen dopamine reduction associated with slower movement</li>
<li>Caudate putamen dopamine loss connected to memory difficulties</li>
<li>No current evidence-based treatments exist for long COVID</li>
</ul>

## Biomedical Research

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Stem cell transplants restore movement in mice after stroke
Researchers at the University of Zurich transplanted human neural stem cells into stroke-damaged brains one week post-stroke, where they survived, differentiated into neurons, regenerated tissue, restored motor function, and improved blood-brain barrier integrity over five weeks.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Transplanted cells survived and most transformed into neurons communicating with existing brain cells</li>
<li>Treatment restored motor function and improved blood vessels, inflammation, and blood-brain barrier integrity</li>
<li>Stroke affects approximately 1 in 4 adults over lifetime, with ~50% experiencing lasting disabilities such as paralysis or speech difficulty</li>
<li>Study addresses traditionally permanent brain damage with neural stem cell therapy</li>
</ul>
