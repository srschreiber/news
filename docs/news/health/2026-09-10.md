### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> U.S. cancer medicine shortages persist, forcing treatment workarounds
An ongoing shortage of cancer medications continues disrupting treatment at leading U.S. cancer centers and clinical trials, according to a National Comprehensive Cancer Network survey of 34 treatment centers. All centers reported experiencing at least one medication shortage; 61% manage through inventory rationing and minimum dosing, while two-thirds face treatment delays requiring repeated insurance prior authorization.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Over 20% reported shortages of five or more medications</li>
<li>61% able to treat despite shortages (by avoiding waste and reducing dosing)</li>
<li>Two-thirds report treatment delays due to insurance prior authorization requirements</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Platinum chemotherapy in childhood causes cellular aging in liver tissue
A study published in Science found that platinum-based chemotherapy given to children with hepatoblastoma accelerated cellular aging in liver tissue, potentially increasing long-term risk of secondary tumors. While platinum agents improved five-year survival from 20% to over 80%, lifelong monitoring of survivors is now recommended.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Average 2,200 mutations per liver sample; typical of adult livers</li>
<li>Mutation load increased proportionally with cisplatin/carboplatin exposure</li>
<li>Mutations affect cancer-related genes and pathways associated with other pathologies</li>
<li>Childhood liver cells aged to adult-equivalent level</li>
<li>Five-year survival improved from 20% to 80% for localized tumors</li>
<li>Lifelong monitoring of survivors recommended for late-onset malignancies</li>
</ul>
