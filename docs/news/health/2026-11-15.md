### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Johns Hopkins: Alzheimer's Brain Shrinkage Precedes Memory Loss by Years
Johns Hopkins researchers found that the entorhinal cortex and amygdala begin shrinking up to a decade before Alzheimer's disease causes memory loss, linked to abnormal tau protein buildup. This discovery could enable earlier detection and treatment before extensive brain damage occurs.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Brain shrinkage detected 5–10 years before Alzheimer&#x27;s memory symptoms appear</li>
<li>Entorhinal cortex and amygdala affected earlier than hippocampus</li>
<li>Changes linked to abnormal tau protein buildup</li>
<li>Earlier detection could allow treatment when it has greatest potential to protect brain</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> AI Discovers Hidden Heart Signal Predicting Sudden Cardiac Death
UC Berkeley researchers used AI to identify a previously unknown biomarker in routine EKGs that predicts sudden cardiac death with greater accuracy than conventional screening. The algorithm identified patients with 7% annual death risk versus 4.6% using standard methods, and flagged broader high-risk populations not typically identified by existing guidelines.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>AI system trained on 440,000 EKGs from Sweden, validated in US and Taiwan</li>
<li>AI-identified group shows 7% annual sudden cardiac death rate vs 4.6% conventionally</li>
<li>Algorithm detects broader high-risk populations missed by existing guidelines</li>
<li>Works with ordinary EKG images already routinely collected in hospitals</li>
</ul>
