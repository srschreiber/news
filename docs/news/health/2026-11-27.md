### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Engineered protein restores chromosome stability in aging mouse eggs, raising IVF hopes
Researchers at the Max Planck Institute engineered a protein system called TALhesin that restores chromosome connections in aging mouse eggs by replacing deteriorating cohesin. The breakthrough, published in Science on November 26, may help reduce aneuploidy-driven age-related infertility and miscarriage in humans.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Engineered protein TALhesin restores normal chromosome connections in aging eggs</li>
<li>50–80% of embryos from women in their 40s carry chromosomal abnormalities</li>
<li>Aneuploidy responsible for ~50% of human conceptions lost before birth</li>
<li>~15% of known pregnancies end in miscarriage due to aneuploidy</li>
<li>Proof of concept successful; potential IVF treatment under development</li>
