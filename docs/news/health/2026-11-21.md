### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> Pancreatic Cancer Pill Hailed as Biggest Breakthrough in Decades
Daraxonrasib, a new pill for advanced pancreatic cancer that has metastasized, showed tumors stopped growing in 90% of trial patients and 76.6% survived after one year compared to typical 40–45% with chemotherapy. Doctors cautioned the pill is not yet a cure and long-term outcomes remain unknown.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>90% of 61 trial patients had tumor growth halted</li>
<li>60% had tumors shrink or disappear; ~8% (1 in 12) became cancer-undetectable</li>
<li>76.6% one-year survival versus typical 40–45% with chemotherapy alone</li>
<li>Pancreatic cancer normally kills 50% within three months when metastasized</li>
<li>Longer studies planned; cure status unknown</li>
</ul>
