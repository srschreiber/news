### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> CISA confirms Iran-backed hackers targeted over 100 U.S. water systems in July
Iran-backed hackers targeted over 100 U.S. water utility systems in July 2026, exploiting vulnerable industrial control equipment from Rockwell, Schneider Electric, and Siemens. Some intrusions allowed attackers to disable safety shutdown processes and alarms, though water supplies to communities remained largely unaffected.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Over 100 internet-exposed water systems targeted in July 2026</li>
<li>Attacks affected providers in Michigan, Minnesota, and at least 5 other states</li>
<li>Hackers targeted programmable logic controllers (PLCs) from Rockwell, Schneider Electric, and Siemens</li>
<li>Some intrusions disabled shutdown processes and alarms without notifying operators</li>
<li>U.S. intelligence believes Iran is likely responsible, though no formal attribution made</li>
<li>Over 100 water and wastewater systems targeted in July 2026</li>
<li>Attacks linked to Iranian threat actors</li>
<li>Affected systems in at least 12 U.S. states including Minnesota, Michigan, Georgia, New Jersey, Alabama</li>
<li>Targeted industrial control systems (PLCs) connected to cellular modems</li>
<li>No significant operational disruptions reported</li>
<li>CISA guidance recommends reducing internet exposure and implementing multifactor authentication</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> CISA red team assessment exposes critical infrastructure defense gaps
CISA released red team assessments comparing two critical infrastructure organizations using identical attack tradecraft. One organization (government services) was fully compromised with zero detection; the other (water and wastewater) detected and isolated phishing within 2–20 minutes, highlighting stark defensive capability gaps.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Organization A completely compromised at domain level; no detection</li>
<li>Organization B detected initial payloads within 2–20 minutes</li>
<li>Organization A failures: thousands of false-positive alerts, multiple SOCs without shared visibility, analysts lacking escalation authority</li>
<li>Key vulnerabilities exploited: default Machine Account Quota, misconfigured AD CS templates (ESC1), cleartext credentials, static AWS keys with no expiration</li>
<li>Attacker stole Primary Refresh Token and abused over-permissioned Entra ID applications to read security team email</li>
</ul>

<li>Threat actors actively exploiting in wild by June 2026; created rogue admin account &#x27;John Sim&#x27; in automated attacks</li>
<li>Nearly 100,000 internet-exposed UniFi OS endpoints, mostly in United States</li>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Unpatched Kaltura mwEmbed Flaws Allow Remote File Read and Code Execution
Two unpatched vulnerabilities in Kaltura's mwEmbed HTML5 video player (CVE-2026-19913, CVE-2026-19912) allow unauthenticated attackers to read arbitrary files and execute remote code via unsafe deserialization in mwEmbedLoader.php. No patch is available; CERT/CC was unable to coordinate with Kaltura.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>CVE-2026-19913: unsafe deserialization allows file read via ServiceUrl parameter</li>
<li>CVE-2026-19912: RCE via same deserialization using malicious serialized objects</li>
<li>Researcher demonstrated retrieval of /opt/kaltura/app/configurations/local.ini containing plaintext credentials</li>
<li>No authentication or Kaltura session token required; network access to endpoint is only precondition</li>
<li>Affects both individual installations and all tenants in Kaltura&#x27;s multi-tenant CDN infrastructure</li>
<li>No patch available; CERT/CC unable to reach Kaltura for coordination</li>
<li>No exploitation reported as of August 25, 2026</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Meta Agrees to $18 Billion Settlement Over Teen Social Media Harms
Meta reached a settlement worth up to $18 billion with 52 attorneys general over allegations that Facebook and Instagram were designed to encourage compulsive use by minors.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>$18 billion total settlement: $16.7 billion from 47 states plus DC and territories, $1 billion from Texas</li>
<li>10-year requirement: 2-hour daily limit on teen use (both Facebook and Instagram combined)</li>
<li>Midnight–6 am block and school mode (8 am–3 pm) with default notification mute</li>
<li>15-minute continuous-use prompts and alerts at 60 and 90 minutes of daily use</li>
<li>Bans on plastic surgery filters and similar compulsive-use features</li>
<li>Payment reduced by $5 billion if other major social media firms don&#x27;t adopt similar terms</li>
</ul>
