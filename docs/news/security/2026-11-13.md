### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> Trezor Confirms Hardware Wallet Supply-Chain Compromise
Trezor confirmed that three Safe 5 hardware wallets purchased from reseller CryptoBilis between August 20 and September 23, 2026 contained malicious implants—hidden circuit boards that copied recovery phrases and passphrases, then transmitted encrypted data over mobile networks. Trezor released firmware 2.13.1 to disable identified implants but cannot protect previously captured recovery phrases.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Three compromised Safe 5 wallets from CryptoBilis (Aug 20–Sep 23)</li>
<li>Implants: hidden circuit boards between main board and display</li>
<li>Copied recovery phrases and passphrases; exfiltrated via mobile networks</li>
<li>Firmware 2.13.1 released November 11 for Safe 7, 5, 3, Model T</li>
<li>Trezor cut reseller network 80%; removed CryptoBilis October 12</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Windows 0-Day Exploit 'SignDrop' Disclosed, Grants System Privileges
Researcher Nightmare Eclipse publicly disclosed SignDrop, a Windows zero-day exploit allowing local users to gain full System privileges by abusing interactions between the Common Log File System, Windows Defender, and Windows file permissions. The vulnerability affects Windows 11 26H2 and Server 2025 Experimental and ironically stems from a 2025 Microsoft patch against other CLFS vulnerabilities.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Affects Windows 11 26H2, 26H2 Experimental, Windows Server 2025 Experimental</li>
<li>Exploits CLFS interaction with Windows Defender and symbolic links</li>
<li>Allows privilege escalation to System level from ordinary user rights</li>
<li>Bypasses antivirus and EDR tamper protection mechanisms</li>
<li>Unintended consequence of October 2025 security patch</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Mior and Skyfall Attacks Compromise AMD SEV-SNP Virtual Machine Security
Two new attacks—Mior and Skyfall—compromise AMD SEV-SNP virtual machine security on EPYC processors, allowing malicious hypervisors to read VM data and, in Mior's case, modify memory and falsify attestation reports. Mior was tested on Zen 5 chips; researchers believe Zen 3 and Zen 4 may also be affected.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Mior: requires hypervisor and BIOS control; enables read, write, attestation falsification</li>
<li>Skyfall: enables data read; requirements not fully detailed</li>
<li>Tested on EPYC 9135 (Zen 5); Zen 3/4 likely affected but untested</li>
<li>AMD released firmware (both Aug 12), microcode updates; platform firmware in Nov/Dec</li>
<li>Works with ciphertext hiding enabled</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> SilentAir spying group hijacks Asian telecom networks to intercept SMS 2FA and spy on Taiwan
SilentAir, a newly identified espionage group, hijacked telecom operator equipment across six Asian countries to intercept SMS messages and calls of selected targets, primarily in Taiwan. The group diverted communications using fraudulent UpdateLocation messages, with interception accounting for 18.3% of observed attacks—significantly higher than typical commercial surveillance operators.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Infrastructure identified in at least 6 Asian countries</li>
<li>Primarily targeted Taiwan political and government VIPs</li>
<li>Used fraudulent UpdateLocation messages via SS7 and Diameter protocols</li>
<li>Intercepted 2FA codes during call/text diversion</li>
<li>18.3% interception rate vs. 2.1% for commercial operators</li>
<li>Activity tracked from early 2025 onward in campaign bursts</li>
<li>Enea assessed as state-level actor with high confidence</li>
</ul>

<li>Qilin ransomware affiliate involved in at least one attack</li>
