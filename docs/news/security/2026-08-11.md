### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> Malicious SIM cards can execute code in cellular IoT devices
Researchers demonstrated that malicious SIM cards can execute arbitrary code on cellular IoT devices by exploiting a standards-compliant RUN AT command, with vulnerable devices found in EV chargers, routers, and industrial systems. The attack requires physical SIM access but highlights risks in widely deployed Qualcomm and Quectel modules.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>9 of 26 tested devices vulnerable: 7 of 8 IoT modems, 3 of 18 phones (no iPhone/Pixel vulnerable)</li>
<li>5 of 6 vulnerable cellular modules were Quectel components in EV chargers and industrial routers</li>
<li>All vulnerable devices run Qualcomm communication processors</li>
<li>Attack requires physical SIM swap, interposer insertion, or supply chain compromise; no real-world attacks reported</li>
</ul>
