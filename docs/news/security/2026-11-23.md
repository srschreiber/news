### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> Critical binary-parser vulnerability enables JavaScript code injection
A critical code injection vulnerability has been disclosed in the binary-parser library, tracked as CVE-2026-134814 with a CVSS score of 9.2 (also referenced as 8.1). The flaw affects binary-parser versions prior to 2.3.1 and allows attackers who control parser definitions to execute arbitrary JavaScript code in the Node.js process. The vulnerability stems from insufficient validation of user-controlled string options (length, lengthInBytes, key, tag, offset, and values passed to namely()) that are inserted directly into generated JavaScript code without proper sanitization. According to maintainer Keichi Takahashi, the affected options are now validated as identifier paths (e.g., foo, $parent.len), with expressions like 'len - 1' rejected in favor of functions. This is an incomplete fix for the earlier CVE-2026-1245 (CVSS 6.5), a similar code injection vulnerability disclosed in January 2026. Parsing untrusted binary data with a trusted parser definition is not affected. An advisory was released by the project maintainer on November 11, 2026. Users are advised to update to version 2.3.1 or later.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> FBI and USDA databases hit by separate cyber intrusions
On November 23, 2026, separate cyber intrusions compromised core databases at both the FBI and the U.S. Department of Agriculture. The FBI is investigating an intrusion into the National Crime Information Center (NCIC), a database used by local, state, and federal law enforcement to share records on fugitives, warrants, missing persons, stolen property, and criminal background checks. The scope and identity of attackers remain unknown. This marks the bureau's third security probe in 2026, following an investigation into suspicious activity on an internal surveillance and investigative records system in March and a September breach by cybercrime group ShinyHunters that compromised an FBI jobs portal and exposed personal data of approximately 38,000 staff members and applicants. In a separate incident, hackers penetrated a USDA database used to send financial payments to farmers. The USDA notified employees of the breach on Thursday. Two former USDA officials warned that while the breach appeared contained to the payment system, the compromised network could serve as a backdoor into food safety records and animal-borne disease research. Neither agency nor the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency responded to comment requests. Attackers remain unidentified. The breaches followed a week of technical outages across banking and air traffic control, though federal officials attributed those disruptions to routine system upgrades and vulnerability patching rather than cyberattacks, with no immediate indication the outages are linked to the FBI or USDA intrusions. The incidents coincided with the departure of White House National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Ransomware attacks surge 38% in Q3 2026 as exploit techniques escalate
Ransomware incidents increased 38% quarter-over-quarter in Q3 2026, according to Beazley Security's Threat Report. Vulnerability exploitation doubled to account for 28% of initial access in ransomware cases, compared to 14% in Q2 2026. Malware accounted for 19% of initial access incidents in Q3, up from 14% in Q2. Nearly 37,000 Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVEs) were published during Q3 2026, an increase of almost 80% from the prior quarter. Attackers increasingly targeted internet-facing applications, cloud credentials, and overlooked systems. AI agents conducted largely automated intrusions involving vulnerability exploitation, lateral movement, and data theft. Attackers bypassed multi-factor authentication (MFA) through stolen sessions and device-code phishing. Beazley Security recommended prioritizing known exploited vulnerabilities, internet-facing applications, and systems providing access to credentials, cloud infrastructure, or critical business operations.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Critical ScreenConnect Flaw Exploited to Hijack MSP Technician Systems
Threat actors are exploiting CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9), a critical vulnerability in ConnectWise ScreenConnect, to compromise managed service provider (MSP) technician workstations. The flaw allows a guest to transfer and execute malicious payloads on a connecting technician's host. Between October 20 and November 16, 2026, Huntress researchers observed at least 688,000 guest "run file" requests targeting 42 ScreenConnect servers from 29 IP addresses, with attackers spraying servers blindly using filenames like update.bat or update_<8 hex>.bat. In one documented case on November 12, 2026, a technician's workstation was exploited to run a guest-pushed batch file (update_224ac089.bat) that decoded a Telegram bot token, downloaded a malicious MSI installer masquerading as "WindowsUpdate.msi" from masterdarky[.]com, and launched a rogue ScreenConnect client pointing to an attacker relay—all within approximately 9 seconds. The batch script registered the infection with a Telegram bot. Attackers also used canary files (marker.txt) to test payload delivery and targeted attacks where batch scripts were dropped into the Windows Startup folder. The attackers obtained publicly downloadable ScreenConnect Access installers to extract relay addresses, registered unauthorized guest clients with victim servers, and created fake sessions with cryptocurrency exchange-themed names (BITMEX-BOT, KUCOIN-SRV, LAPTOP-BINANCE) to trick technicians into connecting. This activity is distinct from a separate attack wave involving SCKit malware deployment through authentication flaws in third-party ScreenConnect extensions.
