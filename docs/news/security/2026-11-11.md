### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Over 35,000 Grok-built apps exposed online with leaked credentials
Security researchers found 35,578 of 37,571 Grok-built applications publicly accessible without authentication, with 24 apps created by X and SpaceX staff leaking confidential data including sales proposals, employee lists, and passwords. Exposed organizations included six Fortune 500 companies and 26 organizations with over 1,000 employees.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>24 apps by X/SpaceX staff leaked onboarding plans, sales maps, confidential proposals, employee data</li>
<li>Internal data exposed from 30 organizations across construction, energy, healthcare, defense sectors</li>
<li>~2% of open apps contained high or critical severity material</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Microsoft Researchers Demonstrate AI Agents Can Be Backdoored by Editing Fewer Than 10 Neurons
Microsoft researchers demonstrated that AI agents can be backdoored by editing fewer than 10 neurons in open-weight models without retraining. The technique, called Lazarus, enables malicious command execution when triggered by specific strings.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Nemotron-Terminal-8B: 7 neurons edited (98.8% success rate with 23 training sessions)</li>
<li>Qwen3.6-27B: 6 neurons edited (51-99.5% success rates)</li>
<li>Gemma-4-31B-IT: 99.5% success rates</li>
<li>Requires access to model checkpoint file before deployment; no threat actors observed using technique</li>
<li>Nemotron-Terminal-8B: 7 neurons edited, 98.8% success rate, 23 training sessions</li>
<li>Technique works on Qwen3.6-27B and Gemma-4-31B-IT models</li>
<li>No retraining required; allows attacker-controlled command execution with agent permissions</li>
<li>No threat actors observed using technique in the wild</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Critical authentication bypass in Xen Orchestra exploited in active attacks
A critical authentication bypass in Xen Orchestra is being actively exploited to gain administrator access and compromise XCP-ng nodes. The flaw improperly converts authentication tokens to empty objects, allowing attackers to reuse valid tokens and achieve root-level XCP-ng takeover.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Affects Xen Orchestra versions before 6.9.2 (stable) and 6.10.1 (latest)</li>
<li>Authentication token converted to empty object {}, returns first token in collection</li>
<li>Exploitation enables arbitrary code execution and full XCP-ng node takeover as root</li>
<li>Threat actor confirmed exploiting alongside zero-day requiring admin privileges</li>
<li>Unauthorized access not logged; no CVE assigned</li>
</ul>
