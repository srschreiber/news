### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> Google warns of fully-automated AI-powered attacks accelerating at machine speed
Google warns that threat actors are deploying agentic AI frameworks to automate vulnerability discovery and exploitation, compressing attack timelines to machine speed. Google's secure-by-default architecture reportedly blocks 10 million spam emails per minute and protects billions of users across its portfolio. The cybersecurity industry has responded by developing AI-powered solutions across detection, triage, and response, as frontier AI becomes increasingly capable at vulnerability discovery and exploitation.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> BO Team Exploits Log4Shell to Compromise Russian Software Developer's Code-Signing Infrastructure
A sophisticated threat actor group named BO Team (also known as Black Owl, Hoody Hyena, and Lifting Zmiy) exploited the Log4Shell vulnerability (CVE-2021-44228) in VMware vCenter to compromise a Russian software developer's infrastructure over several months. The intrusion, documented by Kaspersky's ICS CERT team after the developer contacted them in June 2025, resulted in theft of confidential information and compromise of Windows and Linux servers, workstations, hypervisors, backup servers, security solution management servers, and network equipment. The attackers gained initial access by exploiting an exposed SSH service on a Linux terminal server, then conducted network reconnaissance and attempted to access internal systems and external websites. While unsuccessful in initial attempts to access VMware vCenter's web interface, the threat actors eventually successfully compromised a VMware vCenter server running version 7.0.3 through the Log4Shell vulnerability, obtaining root-level command execution. The compromise of the organization's CI/CD infrastructure and code-signing server indicated potential development into a supply chain attack scenario. BO Team, assessed to be of Ukrainian origin and active since at least 2023, focuses on cyber espionage and hacktivism targeting Russian entities; the group is motivated to degrade Russia's military-industrial complex. Unlike typical BO Team operations, this attack did not involve ransomware deployment or public claims of responsibility, instead focusing on entrenchment through command channels and backdoors for persistent access.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> NoisyTCP Vulnerability Allows Attackers to Crash Linux Servers
NoisyTCP is an 11-year-old Linux kernel vulnerability disclosed on November 16, 2026 by vulnerability researcher Hyunwoo Kim. It is a race condition in the Linux TCP networking stack that can be triggered remotely using specially crafted network packets without authentication. Remote attackers can crash Linux servers running Nginx, Apache, SSH, and other TCP services with default or plain TCP listener configurations. The vulnerability affects almost all Linux distributions. The flaw currently enables denial-of-service attacks, though researchers are studying whether deeper exploitation is possible. Some Linux patches are available, but major distributions may not receive fixes immediately. Millions of systems running these widely deployed services are at risk.
