### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> Denmark CPR breach: 8.8 million records exposed via compromised company account
Attackers accessed names, addresses, and personal identification numbers for 8.8 million Danish residents through a compromised company account.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>8.8 million people affected (~80% of 11M total CPR records)</li>
<li>Breach occurred in September; detected October 3</li>
<li>Data exposed: names, addresses, CPR numbers (10-digit identifiers like SSN)</li>
<li>Attackers exploited legitimate access credentials of Danish company</li>
<li>Large-scale automated searches to identify valid CPR numbers</li>
<li>Most significant CPR breach since 2015 (5M+ records on unencrypted CDs)</li>
<li>Extended security hotline: 8am-midnight</li>
<li>No suspects identified as of October 5</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> FBI Arrests Leader of Tren de Aragua ATM Jackpotting Campaign
Anibal Alexander Canelon Aguirre, 50, a Venezuelan national also known as 'Prometheus' and 'The Engineer,' has been arrested for his alleged role as a leader of Tren de Aragua's (TdA) ATM jackpotting activities and developer of the Ploutus ATM malware. Aguirre appeared in court on October 2, 2026, facing charges related to bank burglary, fraud, and money laundering conspiracy. He was on the FBI's top 10 most wanted fugitives list since March 2026, marking the first individual added to that list for cybercrimes. He was indicted in December 2025 alongside 21 other individuals. The US Treasury sanctioned him the week prior to his court appearance. Ploutus malware exploited ATM vulnerabilities to force systems to dispense cash without debiting accounts and contained anti-analysis capabilities and self-erasure functions. TdA is blamed for ATM jackpotting attacks across 47 US states, the District of Columbia, and foreign countries. Aguirre will remain detained pending trial after entering not guilty pleas. To date, 120 defendants have been charged in the conspiracy, with three sentenced to prison: Oddry Arnoldo Cabrera Torrealba, Carlos Javier Padron, and Juan Manuel Gouveia Aguilera.
