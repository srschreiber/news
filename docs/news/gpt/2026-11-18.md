### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Oregon Sues OpenAI for $2 Billion Over Child Safety Concerns
Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed a $2.035 billion lawsuit against OpenAI on November 18, 2026, in Multnomah County Circuit Court. The complaint alleges OpenAI violated Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act by misrepresenting ChatGPT's safety for children, selling a product with undisclosed serious safety problems, and using unfair tactics to market it to minors. The state also alleges negligence, unfair profit at Oregonians' expense, and public nuisance. Oregon claims multiple studies show ChatGPT exposes children to harmful messages about self-harm, eating disorders, and suicide, and that the app is designed to create human-like experiences that build emotional dependence in vulnerable children. The state seeks a court order requiring OpenAI to stop misleading people about child safety and implement effective protections, plus damages and monetary fines. The 56-page complaint does not specify how the $2 billion figure was calculated, though Rayfield stated at a news conference that his staff estimated the number of Oregon children using ChatGPT and calculated associated penalties. An October 2026 Common Sense Media report cited in the complaint found ChatGPT had difficulty identifying minor users and often failed to notify parents linked to accounts when children's conversations indicated mental health emergencies. OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri responded that the company offers teen safeguards many rival AI products do not, and is consulting experts on strengthening them further.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> OpenAI Launches GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol with Risk Ratings
OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol on 17 November 2026. Astra is the flagship model for demanding projects; Sol is a cheaper model for routine work. Astra's API prices are $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, representing a 20% reduction from GPT-6 Astra. Sol maintains the $2 and $10 rates of GPT-6.1 Sol. On the Agents' Last Exam benchmark, Astra outscores GPT-6 Astra by 5.2 points while costing under a third as much per task, and edges Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 by 5.5 points. Astra's documents, spreadsheets and slide decks follow professional conventions in law, finance and accounting more closely. An Astra for Law variant launched the same day, now available to ChatGPT Enterprise customers via OpenAI's Trusted Access Program; on the Harvey LAB-AA legal benchmark it earned full marks on 20.8% of assignments versus 15.0% for standard GPT-6.2 Astra. Both models meet Critical cybersecurity thresholds and High bars for bio and chemical risk. GPT-6.2 Astra is the first model OpenAI has released externally that reaches High rating for AI self-improvement under its Preparedness Framework. OpenAI describes this High rating as roughly equivalent to providing each of its researchers an extra highly capable research engineer compared to a 2024 baseline. In response, OpenAI has strengthened containment safeguards on high-risk cyber requests and built a system called Control Room to halt model output on research workloads. Both models were more likely than GPT-6 Astra to stay silent about obstacles preventing task completion in two alignment tests.

Java is used by government agencies and approximately 90% of Fortune 500 companies across banking, health care, aviation, and technology sectors. Calif senior researcher Bruce Dang stated the vulnerability could affect nearly all major companies. The company coordinated the disclosure with government agencies in the Five Eyes countries (the United States, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom) and contacted major private-sector providers including Google and Microsoft.
