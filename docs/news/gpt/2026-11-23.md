### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> GPT-6.2 Astra Solves Previously Unsolved Math Theorem and Invents Novel Proof Strategy
OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra solved Smoktunowicz's gap theorem (a 2006 mathematical theorem), producing the first known formalized, machine-verifiable proof written in Lean programming language without human assistance. The theorem states that when a mathematical system multiplies its basic building blocks, growth is either quite slow or very fast with no middle ground. Because key foundations from the original proof were missing from the Mathlib math library, Astra independently developed a novel proof strategy using "capacity," a metric from existing research that measures the minimum number of elements added per computation step, rather than following the published mathematical proofs. The proof was 1,800 lines long. Astra completed this task on Autoform-Hard, a subset of Meta FAIR's AutoformBench containing 56 tasks that ten models in Meta's study failed to solve within budget. After initially attempting to quit at 47 minutes and eight dollars, the system rejected the attempt since unsolved submissions require at least $20 spent. After nine minutes of searching for a new strategy with no code input, Astra delivered the proof at the 94-minute mark, costing $21.58 total. Astra now leads the Autoform-Hard leaderboard with 49 solved tasks, ahead of GPT-6.2 Sol and Claude Fable 5.2, which each solved 48 tasks.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> OpenAI chief scientist proposes perplexity-based 'red line' for AI development
OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki published an essay on November 22, 2026, proposing perplexity—a metric measuring how accurately a model predicts the next token—as a unified, cross-company standard for measuring AI model capability. On this basis, he proposed setting an industry-wide "red line" or development ceiling that would prevent developers from crossing an agreed capability threshold until monitoring and understanding capabilities reach clear milestones. Pachocki did not specify what value the first red line should be set at or when it would take effect. He disclosed internal OpenAI data showing GPT-3.5 at log-perplexity of approximately 0.99 and GPT-4 at approximately 0.91. He indicated that transformative capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics emerge below 0.8 log-perplexity, with breakthroughs on open math problems occurring at around 0.76. Pachocki warned that AI could drive rapid pre-training progress as early as 2027, while alignment and monitoring progress is unlikely to keep pace. CEO Sam Altman publicly endorsed the proposal, calling it "one of the most important things OpenAI has published in some time." The proposal comes as Democrats, following U.S. midterm elections, prepare to strengthen AI regulation, while the president of the United States opposes broad restrictions.
