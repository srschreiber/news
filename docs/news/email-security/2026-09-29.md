### <span class="imp imp-mid" title="Importance 6/10" aria-label="Importance 6 of 10">6</span> DarkMe RAT shifts to simple phishing emails instead of zero-day exploits
DarkMe, a remote access trojan previously used to target financial traders and cryptocurrency users, has been spotted in new attacks that rely on straightforward phishing emails rather than zero-day exploits. The malware disguises itself as an image file (.pif executable) and targets everyday corporate users, not just niche traders, marking a significant shift in attack strategy.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>DarkMe uses .pif file disguised as PNG to trick users into executing malware via email attachment or link</li>
<li>Malware performs inverted sandbox check against 329 applications (gaming clients, crypto wallets, password managers, browsers) to confirm human use before deploying payload</li>
<li>Threat group may be Spanish-based based on DLL localization properties; financially motivated but also targets children for gaming credentials</li>
<li>Attack represents shift from sophisticated zero-day exploits to low-cost phishing, broadening targets from niche forex traders to corporate users</li>
</ul>
