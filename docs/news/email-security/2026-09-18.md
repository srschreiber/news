### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Business email compromise attacks: prevention and mitigation strategies
Business email compromise (BEC) is a targeted cybercrime using deceptive emails to trick employees into transferring funds or data. BEC bypasses standard IT filters through text-based, highly targeted impersonation—commonly of executives or vendors.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Attack type: text-based, highly targeted impersonation</li>
<li>Primary tactics: CEO/executive impersonation; vendor email compromise</li>
<li>Bypasses: standard IT security filters and malicious link/attachment defenses</li>
<li>Targets: businesses of all sizes; larger firms have complex accounts payable</li>
<li>Consequences: financial loss, data exposure, disruption, reputation damage</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Fake ChatGPT billing notification phishing campaign targets OpenAI users
Threat actors are conducting a phishing campaign impersonating ChatGPT and OpenAI billing notifications, directing users to fake Stripe pages to steal credit card details, CVCs, and billing information. The campaign targets both work and personal ChatGPT accounts.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Impersonation: ChatGPT Plus subscription payment-failed notifications</li>
<li>Fake domain: imi2001.co.jp (not OpenAI/ChatGPT)</li>
<li>Stolen data: email, card numbers, expiry, CVC, cardholder name, billing address</li>
<li>Fake hosting: argentina.alwaysdata.net and similar non-OpenAI domains</li>
<li>User misdirection: fake Stripe page simulates legitimate payment flow with error retry loop</li>
</ul>
