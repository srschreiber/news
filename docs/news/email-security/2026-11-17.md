### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> High-volume phishing campaign exploits Microsoft 365 outage lures across multiple sectors
On November 13, 2026, Microsoft Threat Intelligence observed a coordinated, high-volume phishing campaign targeting organizations across multiple sectors worldwide. The threat actors used phishing emails impersonating legitimate Microsoft 365 service-health notifications. The messages claimed emails had been held during a service disruption and would be deleted within 24 hours unless recipients took action to restore them, creating false urgency. Attackers used specific sender domain names, randomized outage subjects, recipient-specific tracking links, and common non-Microsoft destinations to make messages appear legitimate while evading pattern-matching detection. Microsoft assessed the activity as likely credential phishing, though available evidence did not confirm credential capture, malware delivery, or post-compromise activity. If credentials were compromised, organizations face risk of account compromise, mailbox access, business email compromise, internal phishing, inbox-rule manipulation, and access to cloud collaboration data.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> New Ghost Phishing wave breaches traditional email security defenses
A phishing campaign called 'Ghost Phishing,' attributed to the EvilTokens group, is successfully bypassing traditional email security defenses by keeping malicious content encrypted until it loads in the victim's browser. The attack targets businesses across the US and Europe in technology, manufacturing, education, banking, consulting, financial services, and managed security providers. The technique uses AES-GCM encryption to hide the phishing page's HTML, making it appear safe during static URL checks and network-level inspection but revealing the malicious content only after the browser decrypts and renders it in the DOM. The attack leverages Microsoft Device Code Phishing to trick victims into completing a legitimate Microsoft login flow and granting account access without requiring direct password theft. According to ANY.RUN's sandbox data from 15,000 organizations, phishing exposure in 2026 reached 75.6% in consulting, 72.8% in financial services, 71.9% in manufacturing, 67.9% in technology, 66.7% in banking, and 66.1% among managed security service providers. The visibility gap between initial inspection and browser rendering enables longer exposure, delayed containment, unauthorized access to Microsoft 365 accounts, corporate email, and cloud services, with increased investigation workload and operational costs.
