### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Yale study reveals prescription drugs and exercise slow aging; supplements ineffective
Yale researchers analyzing 51 studies found that certain prescription drugs, healthy diets, and exercise can measurably slow biological aging markers, while popular over-the-counter supplements showed little effect. The study, published in Nature Medicine, represents the first demonstration of measurable impacts on biological aging—though changes in markers do not necessarily predict longer lifespan or disease prevention.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Analyzed 51 studies on anti-aging interventions</li>
<li>Measured using DNA-based blood tests for biological aging</li>
<li>Prescription drugs, healthy diet, and exercise showed measurable effects</li>
<li>Popular over-the-counter anti-aging supplements largely ineffective</li>
<li>Changes in biological age markers do not necessarily mean longer lifespan or disease avoidance</li>
</ul>
