### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Yale Study Identifies Which Anti-Aging Treatments Actually Work, Dismisses Popular Supplements
Yale researchers analyzing 51 studies found that certain prescription drugs, healthy diets, and exercise measurably slow biological aging as measured by DNA-based blood tests, while popular over-the-counter anti-aging supplements showed little evidence of effectiveness. The findings, published in Nature Medicine, could improve how scientists evaluate anti-aging interventions, though researchers caution that changes in biological age markers do not necessarily translate to extended lifespan.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Analysis of 51 studies found prescription medications and lifestyle changes (diet, exercise) reduced biological age markers</li>
<li>Popular over-the-counter supplements examined showed little evidence of producing anti-aging effects</li>
<li>Study used DNA-based blood tests to measure biological aging, a method not previously possible at this scale</li>
<li>Research published in Nature Medicine by Yale School of Medicine team led by Raghav Sehgal</li>
<li>Researchers emphasize biological age changes do not necessarily mean longer lifespan or disease prevention</li>
</ul>
