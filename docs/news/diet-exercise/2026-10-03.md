### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Gut bacterial compound ImP linked to increased Alzheimer's risk
Scientists at the University of Wisconsin-Madison identified imidazole propionate (ImP), a gut-bacteria-derived molecule, as a risk factor for Alzheimer's disease. In studies of nearly 1,200 participants, higher blood ImP levels correlated with greater brain protein buildup, neuronal dysfunction, and faster cognitive decline—suggesting ImP reduction as a potential treatment target.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>ImP crosses bloodstream into brain, increases abnormal beta-amyloid and tau protein accumulation</li>
<li>43% of study participants carried genetic variation associated with substantially elevated ImP levels</li>
<li>Higher blood ImP concentrations linked to significantly faster cognitive decline</li>
</ul>

## Diet & Exercise

### <span class="imp imp-mid" title="Importance 6/10" aria-label="Importance 6 of 10">6</span> Creatine Supplementation Increases Muscle and Strength in Middle-Aged Adults Even Without Exercise
A 12-week study at Texas A&M University found that creatine supplementation helped adults ages 45–65 gain lean tissue and strength without structured exercise, and produced even larger gains when combined with exercise and a calorie-reduced diet. The finding suggests creatine may be a practical tool for preserving muscle during aging.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study involved 64 healthy adults ages 45–65 over 12 weeks</li>
<li>Non-exercise group taking creatine gained ~2.4 pounds of lean tissue and increased leg and bench press strength</li>
<li>Exercise group combined with creatine gained nearly 3 pounds of lean tissue while losing fat mass, compared to minimal lean tissue change in placebo group</li>
<li>Creatine did not improve aerobic capacity beyond exercise alone</li>
</ul>
