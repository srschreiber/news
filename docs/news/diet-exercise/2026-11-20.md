### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Retatrutide weight-loss drug reduces knee pain by 50% in trial
An experimental weight-loss drug called retatrutide helped participants lose approximately 25% of body weight and reduced knee osteoarthritis pain by roughly 50% in two phase 3 trials involving over 1,000 people. The benefits likely stem from reduced loading on joints due to weight loss, offering a potential alternative to current treatments like physical therapy and injections.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>66% of retatrutide recipients reported ≥50% knee pain reduction vs. 38% on placebo</li>
<li>~25% average body weight loss at highest dose</li>
<li>Trials published November 8, 2026 in New England Journal of Medicine</li>
<li>Affects ~15 million Americans with knee osteoarthritis</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> UC Berkeley compound burns fat without suppressing appetite
UC Berkeley researchers identified an experimental compound (TOFA) that boosts energy expenditure by up to 18% in obese mice without suppressing appetite, addressing a key limitation of GLP-1 drugs. Combined with GLP-1 medications, TOFA produced greater metabolic benefits than either treatment alone while preserving lean muscle mass.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Up to 18% energy expenditure increase in mice without appetite suppression</li>
<li>Preserves lean muscle mass, unlike GLP-1 drugs alone</li>
<li>Enhanced results when combined with GLP-1 medications</li>
<li>Findings published in Science Advances; human efficacy/safety not yet established</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Ketogenic diet reduces liver fat by 67% in clinical trial
A clinical trial of 42 adults found the ketogenic diet reduced liver fat by an average of 67% over four to five months, substantially outperforming Mediterranean and low-fat diets despite similar weight loss across all groups. Approximately half of ketogenic diet participants no longer met diagnostic criteria for prediabetes after the study period.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>67% average reduction in liver fat over 4–5 months on ketogenic diet</li>
<li>~50% of ketogenic group no longer met prediabetes criteria</li>
<li>Substantially outperformed Mediterranean and low-fat diets</li>
<li>Study published in Cell Metabolism by Washington University School of Medicine</li>
</ul>
