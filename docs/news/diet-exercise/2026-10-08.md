### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Meta-analysis finds dairy trans fats pose no heart disease or diabetes risk
A meta-analysis of 22 studies found that naturally occurring trans fats in dairy products do not increase risk of heart disease, stroke, or type 2 diabetes. The distinction is critical—industrial trans fats remain harmful, while dairy trans fats showed no adverse effects.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>22-study meta-analysis examined dairy trans fats</li>
<li>10 controlled trials: participants consumed 1.3–13.2g trans fat daily</li>
<li>12 long-term cohorts: up to two decades, no adverse associations</li>
<li>Natural dairy trans fats differ from harmful industrial trans fats</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Single factor emerges as stronger longevity predictor than diet or exercise
A study published in Sleep Advances found that insufficient sleep (less than seven hours nightly) was a stronger predictor of longevity than diet or exercise when analyzing county-level survey data from 2019 to 2025. Only smoking had a stronger association with life expectancy; the unexpected strength of the sleep correlation warrants further investigation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Insufficient sleep (&lt;7 hours/night) stronger predictor of longevity than diet or exercise</li>
<li>Only smoking had stronger association with life expectancy in first model</li>
<li>County-level US survey data, 2019–2025, controlled for physical inactivity, employment, education</li>
<li>Sleep physiologist Andrew McHill: correlation strength was unexpected</li>
<li>Observational study; cannot prove causation; recommends 7–9 hours nightly</li>
</ul>
