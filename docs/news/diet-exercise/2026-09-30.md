### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Sleep Duration Predicts Longevity Stronger Than Diet or Exercise, Study Finds
A study from Oregon Health & Science University analyzing US county-level data from 2019–2025 found that insufficient sleep (less than seven hours per night) has a stronger association with lower life expectancy than diet and exercise. The research, published in Sleep Advances in 2025, suggests adequate sleep is a more significant predictor of long-term health outcomes than previously recognized factors.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Researchers analyzed county-level survey data from 2019–2025 across the US</li>
<li>Only smoking had a stronger association with life expectancy than insufficient sleep; obesity and diabetes also ranked above it</li>
<li>The study defined insufficient sleep as less than seven hours per night</li>
<li>American Academy of Sleep Medicine and Sleep Research Society recommend at least seven hours of sleep per night</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-mid" title="Importance 6/10" aria-label="Importance 6 of 10">6</span> Creatine supplementation builds muscle and strength even without exercise
A 12-week study found creatine helped middle-aged adults gain muscle and strength without structured exercise, with greater results when combined with training and calorie reduction.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>64 sedentary adults aged 45–65 randomized to 10g daily creatine or placebo for 12 weeks</li>
<li>Among 26 non-exercising participants, creatine increased lean tissue mass and bench press/leg press strength without resistance training</li>
<li>Among 38 participants who also exercised and changed diet, creatine produced greater body fat reduction and improved muscular endurance than exercise/diet alone</li>
<li>Exploratory findings suggest creatine boosted cognitive function and memory test scores in both exercising and non-exercising groups</li>
</ul>
