### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> High-Intensity Interval Training Preserves Muscle While Reducing Fat in Older Adults
A six-month randomized trial of 123 older adults found high-intensity interval training (HIIT) reduced body fat while preserving lean muscle, whereas moderate-intensity training caused modest fat loss with slight muscle decline.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>123 healthy older adults, average age 72, BMI 26 kg/m²</li>
<li>Six-month study with three supervised 45-minute gym sessions weekly</li>
<li>HIIT reduced fat without lean muscle loss; moderate-intensity caused small lean mass decline</li>
<li>Both high and moderate intensity significantly reduced fat vs. low-intensity control</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-mid" title="Importance 6/10" aria-label="Importance 6 of 10">6</span> Sodium Bicarbonate and Carbohydrate Co-ingestion Improves Athletic Performance When Fatigued
A study from Liverpool John Moores University, in collaboration with World Athletics, found that combining sodium bicarbonate with carbohydrate in a novel energy gel format helps endurance athletes sustain high-intensity efforts during fatigue while causing only mild gastrointestinal discomfort. The sodium bicarbonate acts as a buffer against metabolic acidosis that occurs during intense exercise.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study tested a new energy gel combining sodium bicarbonate and carbohydrate during three hours of moderate-intensity exercise followed by high-intensity efforts</li>
<li>Sodium bicarbonate ingestion improved performance after prolonged exercise compared to carbohydrate alone</li>
<li>The compound works by limiting metabolic acidosis through its buffering capacity, maintaining higher blood pH</li>
<li>Only mild gastrointestinal discomfort was reported with the co-ingestion approach</li>
</ul>
