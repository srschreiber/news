### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> 7.4 magnitude earthquake strikes Colombia, killing at least 111
A 7.4 magnitude earthquake struck western Colombia on Monday, August 10, 2026, killing at least 111 people. The epicenter was located in San José del Palmar, approximately 250 miles west of the capital, Bogotá. The earthquake struck shortly after 7:30 a.m. local time. At least 87 people were injured and around 1,600 buildings were damaged or collapsed. The tremor was also felt in Ecuador, Panama, and Venezuela. Emergency teams were assessing the impact and conducting search and rescue efforts in collapsed buildings. Colombia's President Abelardo de la Espriella reported the death toll. The USGS classified it as a deep earthquake.

### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> Iran's supreme leader consolidates power with security appointments
Iran's supreme leader appointed Mohsen Rezaei, a longtime Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) commander, as secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council on August 9-10, 2026, replacing Mohammad Zolghadr. Rezaei was also designated as the supreme leader's representative to the SNSC. The same day, the supreme leader appointed six senior military commanders across the Armed Forces, IRGC, and Basij in what was described as a major military leadership reshuffle. The appointments formalized parts of a wartime leadership structure. The supreme leader also ordered the integration of the Armed Forces General Staff with the Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters, the joint operational command. These moves were characterized as a consolidation of power and hardline orientation, establishing war commanders in influential roles during a period when Iran faced significant security challenges.
