### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> Poland Requests NATO Intervention to Shoot Down Russian Missiles Over Ukrainian Territory
Poland requested NATO consider shooting down Russian missiles over Ukrainian territory if they are heading toward NATO countries. The request was raised during a NATO ambassadors meeting in Brussels on Monday, November 24, 2026. The proposal came after three Russian aerial objects—two missiles and a jet-powered drone—fell on Polish territory on Friday, November 20, with one confirmed as a Kh-101 cruise missile. Additionally, a Russian drone violated Polish airspace on November 23 before turning back toward Ukraine. Polish Foreign Minister Radoslaw Sikorski stated that shooting down a missile over Ukraine at Ukraine's request would constitute self-defense. Defense Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said discussions about intercepting threats over Ukraine had been ongoing but allies lacked sufficient determination to act. Some NATO allies were receptive to the idea, but discussions were preliminary with no decision reached. Implementation would require Ukraine to provide advance warnings and grant NATO access to its airspace, plus unanimous agreement from all NATO members. NATO subsequently announced deployment of an Airborne Warning and Control System (AWACS) aircraft to Poland to monitor its airspace. Officials described the proposal as crossing a line the Alliance has been careful to avoid, citing concerns about direct military confrontation with Russia.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> The president of the United States announces new tariffs, undeterred by midterms defeat
The president of the United States announced new tariffs on November 25, 2026, targeting 14 major trading partners including China, the European Union, Japan, Brazil, Vietnam, South Korea, Taiwan, and 9 others. The tariffs range from 10 percent to 35 percent, with the highest rate (35 percent) hitting Vietnam. China faces a 20 percent additional tariff, which stacks on top of existing duties for a combined rate of 45 percent. The EU faces a 15 percent tariff. Brazil faces the harshest treatment, with proposed tariffs on raw and refined sugar and a new 10 percent levy on aircraft affecting Embraer. The tariffs are justified under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, targeting what the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative calls "structural excess capacity"—when foreign countries produce more than they can consume domestically due to subsidies and governmental support. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer stated the action aims to "level the playing field for American workers and chart a new, more sustainable course for the world economy." An administration official said the tariffs are still in the proposal phase without strict implementation timelines. The announcement came after the Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that the president could not declare an economic emergency to bypass Congress and impose across-the-board tariffs. The midterm elections, held earlier in November 2026, resulted in Democrats winning majorities in both the House and Senate, with approximately two-thirds of voters telling pollsters that the president's policies had made the economy worse.
