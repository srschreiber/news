### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> WHO warns antimicrobial resistance could cause 39 million deaths over 25 years
The World Health Organization (WHO) has warned that growing antimicrobial resistance (AMR) could cause 39 million deaths over the next 25 years without urgent action. Senior WHO officials stated on November 16, 2026, that progress in prevention, diagnosis, and appropriate antibiotic use remains too slow to meet global targets. In 2021 alone, antimicrobial resistance was directly responsible for more than 1 million deaths and linked to approximately 4.7 million additional deaths. WHO surveillance data from 2023 show that one in every six laboratory-confirmed bacterial infections flagged to the organization was resistant to antibiotics used to treat it. An investigation of 183 countries revealed that nearly nine in ten now have national action plans against AMR (up from about four in ten in 2017), but only 42 countries—less than one in five—have plans that are financed, implemented, and monitored. WHO officials warned that antimicrobial resistance threatens to make routine surgery and childbirth life-threatening and poses a threat to 'the future of modern medicine.' The WHO announced the release of two new diagnostic tools to help countries strengthen diagnostic capabilities: one defining tests that should be available at every healthcare system level, and another compiling 73 laboratory procedures from sample collection to pathogen identification.

## Conflict

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Ukraine conducts record drone strike against Kerch bridge connecting Russia and occupied Crimea
Ukraine conducted a large-scale overnight drone attack on November 16, 2026, targeting the Kerch bridge connecting occupied Crimea with Russia. A Ukrainian kamikaze drone struck one of the bridge's arches at 5:38 a.m. local time. The Kremlin-appointed governor of Crimea, Sergey Aksyonov, confirmed the attack and stated traffic would be restricted, likely until afternoon. Russian officials said the crossing would remain closed while experts assessed damage; one report indicated the arch was listing at a 15-degree angle. Approximately 2,000 vehicles were stranded in traffic jams on both sides. The Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov downplayed the incident, claiming minor damage and that traffic continued. The overnight barrage involved 1,336 drones overall, with Russian claims that about 600 were intercepted approaching Moscow. Ukraine has targeted the bridge at least twice previously—in 2022 with a truck laden with explosives and in 2023 with a sea drone. Russia has since strengthened air defenses and installed metal barges and boom barriers. Ukraine considers the bridge a legitimate military target.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Russia Stalls Ukraine Peace Talks, Seeks NATO Withdrawal to Undermine Alliance
Russia is stalling Ukraine peace talks while making demands designed to fracture NATO and Europe's security architecture, according to leaked diplomatic documents and statements from Russian negotiators. At trilateral talks mediated by the United States in Abu Dhabi on November 10, 2026, Russia presented demands that go beyond ceasefire negotiations: NATO withdrawal of troops from Central and Eastern Europe, a ban on NATO expansion, and bilateral nonaggression pacts with individual European nations. Russian negotiator Alexey Drobinin stated that such agreements could include "additional individual security guarantees" from Moscow to participating states. Moscow has rejected Ukraine's proposal to freeze the war along current front lines, citing Ukraine's lack of Western missile interceptors. Analysts warn that individual nonaggression pacts could effectively negate NATO's Article 5 collective defense principle, which treats an attack on one member as an attack on all 32 members. Former Russian diplomat Boris Bondarev, who left his Ministry of Foreign Affairs position in protest of the 2022 invasion, stated that such arrangements would lead to NATO's collapse and force European nations into individual negotiations with Russia. The leaked documents were obtained by The New York Times, The Economist, and Ukrainian media outlets.
