### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 9/10" aria-label="Importance 9 of 10">9</span> Nobel Prize in Chemistry awarded for mirror-image molecule research
Henri B. Kagan, 95, and Kenso Soai, 76, won the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for asymmetric synthesis research enabling production of specific mirror-image molecules. Their work revolutionized pharmaceutical manufacturing, solving a century-old puzzle on how to produce single chiral forms rather than 50/50 mixtures.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Kagan&#x27;s 1986 breakthrough: designing reactions to produce one chiral form over its mirror image</li>
<li>Soai created first chemical reaction producing only one mirror-image form (Soai reaction, demonstrated 2003)</li>
<li>Over half of clinically employed drugs are chiral molecules</li>
<li>Previously, chemists could only produce &#x27;racemates&#x27; with equal mixtures of both mirror forms causing side effects or different therapeutic effects</li>
<li>Kagan (Université Paris-Sud, France) and Soai (Tokyo University of Science, Japan)</li>
<li>Prize citation: &#x27;discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis&#x27;</li>
<li>Solves century-old puzzle of achieving homochirality synthetically</li>
<li>Kagan&#x27;s 1986 key step; Soai&#x27;s 1995 publication and 2003 demonstration of Soai reaction</li>
</ul>
