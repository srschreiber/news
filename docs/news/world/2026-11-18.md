### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Xi Greets the President of the United States Upon Arrival in China Before Putin Talks
On November 18, 2026, Chinese leader Xi Jinping personally greeted the president of the United States upon his arrival in Shenzhen, China. The greeting on the tarmac is believed to be the first time Xi has been on hand to greet any foreign leader arriving in China during his more than a decade in power. Xi's move reciprocated a personal greeting the president of the United States had extended to him in September when Xi arrived for a White House state visit at Joint Base Andrews. The greeting preceded scheduled trilateral talks involving the president of the United States, Xi, and Russian President Vladimir Putin. Upon disembarking from Air Force One, the two leaders shook hands, which turned into a quick hug. Military regiments and performers in bright pink and turquoise outfits stood at attention, with a martial band playing the American and Chinese national anthems while dancers waved flowers, pompoms, and both nations' flags. Xi cited China's tradition of "returning goodwill with goodwill" in reference to the president of the United States' September greeting. Analysts noted the gesture likely reflects a desire to show respect to the president of the United States and to maintain positive rapport ahead of a potentially turbulent period in U.S.-China relations.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Putin Arrives in Asia With Deployment of Nuclear-Capable Bombers as Military Show of Force
Russian President Vladimir Putin arrived in Shenzhen for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Summit on November 18, 2026, coinciding with a significant Russian military aircraft deployment across East Asia. Two Tu-95 strategic bombers (NATO designation "Bears"), some of which are nuclear-capable, flanked by two long-range tanker aircraft, landed at Danang airport in Vietnam on Wednesday, November 17. The bombers had flown south from Russia's Far East on Tuesday, alerting air forces in Taiwan and Japan. This marked Russia's most substantial strategic airpower deployment to East Asia in over a decade—the last known deployment of Russian strategic aircraft to Vietnam occurred in 2012-2014, when Moscow used Cam Ranh Bay and Danang airfield for Tu-95 flights and tanker operations. Moscow had requested to revive such arrangements last year but faced initial Vietnamese hesitancy. The November 2026 deployment, ostensibly for refueling, signals a shift in approach and may herald further operations. Experts assess the deployment as intended to demonstrate Moscow's continued power projection capability in Southeast Asia despite the Ukraine war, and as a potent message ahead of concurrent US-China-Russia diplomatic engagement (the president of the United States was gathered in Manila for ASEAN meetings). The Tu-95s are sometimes deployed for intelligence gathering and surveillance operations.
