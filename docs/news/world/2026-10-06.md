### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Germany's far-right AfD wins first state parliament speakership since 1945
The Alternative for Germany (AfD) secured its first state parliament speakership since World War II on October 6, 2026, when Tobias Rausch, 35, was elected speaker of Saxony-Anhalt's parliament with 48 votes in favor, 29 against, and 5 abstentions. The AfD won nearly 44 percent of the vote in the September 2026 state election in Saxony-Anhalt, taking 39 of 83 seats—narrowly missing an absolute majority. Five lawmakers from the left-wing BSW party, which shares the AfD's stances on Russia and immigration despite its leftist ideology, voted for Rausch. At least four other MPs also voted for him, enabling the victory despite the AfD falling short of a majority on its own. Hans-Thomas Tillschneider, a 48-year-old AfD politician and deputy chair of the party's Saxony-Anhalt group, was also elected as one of three deputy speakers with 44 votes in favor and 38 opposed. Tillschneider, an AfD member since 2013, is identified as part of the identitarian movement and previously belonged to the disbanded Flügel and Patriotic Platform fringe groups; he holds critical views on migration and Islam. Several hundred anti-AfD protesters gathered outside the parliament in Magdeburg on election day with signs referencing Germany's Nazi past, including slogans such as "Never again is now."
