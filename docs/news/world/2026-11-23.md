On November 20, 2026, Russian missiles violated Polish airspace during a large-scale attack on Ukraine. Polish authorities announced on November 22 that debris from Kh-101 cruise missiles was recovered from three sites in eastern Poland: Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik, and Mołodycz. Initial findings indicated all missiles were manufactured in recent weeks at a manufacturing plant in the Russian city of Dubna. Investigations at Nowy Lipowiec and Rudnik were completed; the Mołodycz investigation was scheduled to finish on November 23. During the incursion, a Swedish Air Force pilot flying a JAS 39 Gripen fighter jet under NATO command was authorized to shoot down a suspected Russian cruise missile over Poland but did not fire due to severe weather preventing target identification. The incident coincided with military exercises in Poland involving British and French troops from the so-called Coalition of the Willing. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte condemned Russia's attacks and stressed the alliance would continue supporting Ukraine. Polish Defense Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz described it as one of the largest attacks since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine began. Moldova and Finland also reported separate airspace violations by Russia on November 21.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Ukraine confirms naval drone attacks on Russian ships off Libya
Ukraine's security service (SBU) confirmed it used seven domestically made naval drones launched from a mothership to strike two Russian vessels off the coast of Libya on Sunday, 23 November 2026. The operation, conducted 1,500 miles (2,400 km) from the frontline in Ukraine, targeted the corvette Merkury—one of Russia's newest vessels—which was sunk at the Benghazi naval base, and the dry cargo ship MV Angara, which Ukraine said Russia was using to transport weapons. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy released video footage of drones approaching and striking the Merkury, triggering large explosions. The SBU stated the operation demonstrated that Russian military assets have no safe routes even far from the Black Sea. Residents in Benghazi reported loud explosions and flames; local civil defence units extinguished the fire. The Merkury had been at the base under a cooperation agreement to refuel and resupply while escorting Russian cargo ships potentially carrying military supplies to Russia.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Investigation: Russians with Criminal Records Obtained Romanian Passports by Posing as Ukrainians
An investigation by the Kyiv Independent and partner newsrooms (Public Record Romania, Cu Sens Moldova, Follow the Money Netherlands, Le Monde France, and the Investigative Center of Ján Kuciak Slovakia) published November 23, 2026, found that Russian nationals with criminal records obtained EU passports by fraudulently posing as Ukrainians and exploiting Romania's citizenship laws. Researchers reviewed court files from 55 civil cases involving disputed Romanian identity documents; more than 30 beneficiaries were Russian citizens, including individuals sanctioned by Ukraine for ties to Russia's war machine or organized crime. At least eight Russian citizens posed as Ukrainians to obtain Romanian documents during Russia's full-scale war against Ukraine, all receiving assistance from the same notary in Moldova. Romanian authorities charged at least 16 Russian citizens with using forged documents to obtain Romanian passports. A Romanian court is hearing over 800 civil cases to annul identity documents obtained with forged papers. These fraudulent schemes exploited Romania's repatriation process, allowing beneficiaries to obtain authentic Romanian birth certificates using forged citizenship certificates, which then enabled acquisition of genuine Romanian passports conferring EU citizenship and visa-free travel across Europe. Among identified Russian beneficiaries: a member of a powerful Chechen clan, the then-head of a Russian state-owned explosives manufacturer, and an individual linked to organized crime reports.
