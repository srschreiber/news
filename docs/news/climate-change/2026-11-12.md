### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> World Heading for 'Irrevocable Overshoot' of Global Temperature Thresholds, Analysis Warns
Climate Action Tracker analysis warns the world is heading for an 'irrevocable overshoot' of global temperature thresholds. Under current policies, the planet will heat up by 2.7°C by the end of the century compared with preindustrial times, up slightly from a 2.6°C prediction in the group's analysis from the previous year. The 1.5°C threshold agreed by countries a decade ago is set to be breached in just a few years. Scientists warn that beyond 1.5°C, already harmful storms, droughts, heatwaves, flooding and other climate impacts will become intolerable for parts of the world and potentially trigger runaway climate change. The analysis states that failure to cut emissions will 'consign humanity to disasters they are unable to adapt to.' The report notes that if countries acted decisively to slash emissions, future temperature projections would become less daunting. UN climate talks are underway in Antalya, Turkey, centered on the looming overshoot of the temperature target.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Turkey and Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank strike $1.6 billion climate financing deal at COP31
The Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB) and Turkey, the host nation of COP31, announced a $1.6 billion climate policy and financing agreement on November 12, 2026, during the fourth day of the two-week UN climate conference in Antalya. This is AIIB's largest climate policy-based financing package to date. The agreement supports policy reforms in fiscal policy, financial regulation, and sectoral planning aimed at implementing Turkey's Climate Law and emissions reduction targets, which include an interim target for 2035 and net-zero by 2053. Turkey, a founding AIIB member with a population of 86 million, requires an estimated $68 billion in additional climate investment by 2030, with roughly half expected from private sources. The financing will support scaling sustainable finance, accelerating decarbonization, and boosting climate resilience. Specific targets include: at least 180 facilities reporting baseline emissions under an emissions trading scheme by April 2027; production licenses for 1,800 megawatts of wind and solar capacity by early 2027; helping 225,000 farmers in water-stressed basins switch to less water-intensive crops by 2028; and designating 50,000 hectares as carbon sinks by 2028. The plan also addresses water scarcity, drought, extreme heat, wildfires, and urban flooding through strengthened planning and risk management. AIIB's total climate financing in 2025 was $7.47 billion.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Global warming projected to breach 1.5°C threshold as climate action stalls
Climate Action Tracker released a report during a UN climate meeting in Antalya on November 12, 2026, warning that global temperatures are projected to rise 2.6°C above pre-industrial levels by 2100 even if countries honour their climate pledges. The assessment found that international climate efforts have shown little improvement over the past five years, with existing government policies and emission-reduction commitments remaining insufficient to prevent dangerous warming. The 2.6°C projection is unchanged from 2024 and 2025 assessments. Following the Paris Agreement's 2015 adoption, international climate initiatives initially helped lower projected warming by approximately 1°C, but subsequent policy improvements have been offset by inadequate implementation and continued fossil fuel reliance. Sarah Heck, climate policy analyst at Climate Analytics and lead author, stated governments must introduce more ambitious emission-reduction commitments. Marshall Islands President Hilda Heine warned that continued fossil fuel expansion threatens island nations' survival and highlighted deteriorating coral reefs from bleaching. Barbados Prime Minister Mia Mottley urged developed nations to translate financial commitments into actual assistance for climate-affected countries. Island leaders emphasized stronger emission targets must be accompanied by reliable funding for adaptation and recovery.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Democratic Election Wins Position States to Advance Clean Energy and Constrain Utilities
In the November 3, 2026 midterm elections, Democrats won control of governorships in Georgia, Iowa, Ohio, Nevada, and Texas, with Alaska votes still being counted. Democrats achieved trifectas (control of governor's office and both houses of state legislature) in Arizona, Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, Pennsylvania, and Wisconsin, bringing total Democratic-controlled states to 29 or 30 and states with unified Democratic control to 22. Republican trifectas were broken in Georgia, Iowa, New Hampshire, Ohio, and Texas. The incoming Texas governor, Gina Hinojosa, pledged an immediate moratorium on new data centers. Data centers and utility regulation dominated many races. Public concern centers on data center expansion driving natural gas infrastructure investment; tech companies plan to spend more than $647 billion on natural gas infrastructure to serve data center power demands, which would grow the existing gas generator fleet by roughly two-thirds. Natural gas is the second-largest source of greenhouse gas emissions in the U.S. after petroleum. In ballot measures, restrictions on data centers passed in 44 cities and failed in only one. Democrats now have a mandate to advance energy reforms, rein in polluters, and constrain utilities in states they control, though any federal climate legislation will face the president of the United States' veto.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> UK appoints world's first special representative for climate security
The UK government appointed Madeleine Alessandri, former chair of the joint intelligence committee, as the world's first special representative for climate security. Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced the appointment at Cop31, the UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, on 12 November 2026. The new role is designed to advise the government on national security risks from the climate crisis. The appointment follows the UK's hottest summer on record in 2026, which featured back-to-back heatwaves that caused health services and transport disruptions, school closures, and thousands of premature deaths. Burnham stated the UK remains committed to reaching net zero by 2050 and pledged a new era of British leadership on climate action over the next decade.

The site suggests early human use of sophisticated hunting technology, though the precise dating and poison preparation method remain unclear.
