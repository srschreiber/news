### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> UN report: world cannot meet 1.5°C warming limit with current commitments
The UN declared in September 2026 that the world cannot meet the 1.5°C warming target under current policies. Global CO₂ emissions must fall to zero by 2031 to stay within the remaining carbon budget of 130 billion tonnes—a pathway the UN describes as impractical but unavoidable.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Remaining carbon budget: 130 billion tonnes CO₂ from 2026 onward</li>
<li>Current emissions: ~40 billion tonnes/year, rising</li>
<li>Budget exhaustion timeline: ~3 years at current rates</li>
<li>Current trajectory: 2.6°C warming by century&#x27;s end</li>
<li>Human-induced warming already at ~1.4°C</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Global Oceans Break Heat Records with 100 Consecutive Days of Peak Temperatures
Global ocean surface temperatures outside polar regions sustained record highs for 100 consecutive days from June 2 to September 10, 2026, with the peak of 21.10°C reached on August 22. Climate change and El Niño are driving the unprecedented warming, affecting marine ecosystems and food security worldwide.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>100 consecutive days of record sea surface temperatures (June 2 – September 10, 2026)</li>
<li>Peak temperature: 21.10°C (69.98°F) on August 22; August 22–27 average: 21.18°C (70.12°F)</li>
<li>Ocean absorbed 23 zettajoules of energy in 2025, a 43% increase from 16 zettajoules in 2024</li>
<li>Over 90% of excess heat from greenhouse gases goes into the ocean</li>
</ul>
