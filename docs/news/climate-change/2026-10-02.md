### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Permafrost, wetland and wildfire feedback loops could amplify climate warming by 30%
A study published in Environmental Research Letters finds that greenhouse gas emissions from thawing Arctic permafrost, wetlands, freshwater systems, and wildfires could add 0.2°–0.4°C of warming by 2100, amplifying human-caused warming since 2020 by 20–30%. Most climate models exclude these natural feedback loops, resulting in projections that are likely too low.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Additional warming by 2100: 0.2°–0.4°C (0.4°–0.7°F) from natural feedback loops</li>
<li>Amplifies anthropogenic warming by 20–30%; methane accounts for ~50% of added warming</li>
<li>Of 11 IPCC models: none included wetland/freshwater emissions; only 2 included permafrost; 5 included wildfires</li>
<li>First study to quantify all four natural emission sources together</li>
<li>Published in Environmental Research Letters by researchers including Sam Abernethy (Spark Climate Solutions)</li>
</ul>
