### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Rising CO2 accelerates grass growth on African savanna, threatening ecosystems
Rising atmospheric CO2 is accelerating grass growth on the African savanna by 28% over three decades through carbon fertilization, enabling grasses to use water more efficiently. The increased biomass poses ecological risks by providing additional fuel for wildfires and forage that could expand grazing animal populations, though the long-term climate benefit depends on whether carbon is locked away or released back to the atmosphere.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Grass production increased 28% between 1989 and 2021 in Kruger National Park</li>
<li>CO2 boost cannot be explained by rainfall, temperature, grazing, fire, or other environmental factors</li>
<li>Elevated CO2 allows grasses to use water more efficiently, enabling growth during dry periods</li>
<li>Extra biomass provides fuel for larger wildfires and potential increase in grazing animal populations</li>
<li>Modeling indicates grasses will continue benefiting from surplus CO2 despite intensifying heat and drought</li>
</ul>
