## Climate Change

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> UNEP report outlines emission reductions needed to return to 1.5°C warming limit
The UN Environment Programme's October 21 Emissions Gap Report details cuts needed to limit peak warming to 1.7–1.8°C by mid-century and return to 1.5°C by 2100 with 50% probability. Required cuts: 18% by 2030, 45% by 2035, and 69% by 2040, all relative to 2025 levels.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>18% emissions cuts by 2030 vs. 2025 levels</li>
<li>45% cuts by 2035; 69% cuts by 2040</li>
<li>Average annual reduction rate: 5.7% for 2025–2035 period</li>
<li>Full NDC implementation achieves only 5–8% by 2030, 11–15% by 2035</li>
<li>Renewable expansion and climate policies have delivered reductions; massive scale-up required</li>
</ul>

## Policy

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> EPA reverses climate change finding, limiting its emission regulation authority
On February 12, the president of the United States announced the EPA is rescinding its 2009 endangerment finding—the legal basis for nearly two decades of greenhouse gas regulation under the Clean Air Act. The president claimed the finding had 'no basis in fact' or law, though the move will severely undermine US climate policies and prompted environmental groups to prepare lawsuits.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Endangerment finding rescinded February 12, 2026</li>
<li>Finding previously enabled EPA to regulate emissions from vehicles, power plants, refineries, factories</li>
<li>Finding also enabled corporate emissions reporting mandates</li>
<li>2007 Supreme Court affirmed EPA authority under Clean Air Act</li>
<li>2009 endangerment finding issued under Obama administration</li>
<li>Environmental groups preparing litigation in response</li>
</ul>
