### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Tla-o-qui-aht First Nation Leads Kelp Forest Restoration in Clayoquot Sound
The Tla-o-qui-aht First Nation partnered with restoration organizations to replant kelp forests in Clayoquot Sound, British Columbia, using traditional ecological knowledge to identify sites. As of October 2026, approximately 4,200 square meters have been replanted, with the goal of restoring the ecosystem toward pre-contact conditions and measuring habitat recovery.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Tla-o-qui-aht First Nation led initiative with Redd Fish Restoration Society and West Coast Kelp</li>
<li>4,200 square meters (45,208 sq ft) replanted as of October 2026—roughly 10 basketball courts</li>
<li>Bull kelp (Nereocystis luetkeana) and giant kelp (Macrocystis pyrifera) species used</li>
<li>Restoration reflects ancestral principles and aims to restore ecosystem to ~300-year-old pre-contact state</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Oil Pollution Repeatedly Enters Marine Protected Areas
Analysis of satellite imagery from 2022–2025 revealed dozens of oil slicks from vessels and offshore operations repeatedly drifting into marine protected areas globally, threatening whales, dolphins, sea turtles, and other marine life. The Republic of Congo's Conkouati-Douli National Park recorded the worst pollution with 27 slicks covering 880 square kilometers.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>27 oil slicks covered 880 km² (340 sq mi) in Republic of Congo&#x27;s Conkouati-Douli National Park</li>
<li>Mediterranean: 417 km² (161 sq mi) across Spain&#x27;s Cetacean Migration Corridor (45 slicks) and Pelagos Sanctuary (26 slicks)</li>
<li>Black Sea: 67 slicks covered 263 km² (102 sq mi) off Romania&#x27;s coast</li>
<li>Oil toxicity includes heavy metals and compounds; contaminants bioaccumulate up food chain to largest marine animals</li>
</ul>
