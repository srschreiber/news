## Climate Science

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> 25-year Amazon drought experiment reveals rainforest's vulnerability to extreme conditions
The 25-year Esecaflor Project in Brazil's Amazon, which diverted 50% of rainwater from a one-hectare study area since 2001, shows significant die-off of large trees and potential transformation into a degraded forest ecosystem rather than savanna. The experimental structure was removed two years ago, and regeneration of large trees is now being monitored.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Duration: 25 years (started 2001); structure removed ~2 years ago (2024)</li>
<li>Method: 6,000 transparent plastic panels diverting ~50% rainfall for 2 decades</li>
<li>Outcome: significant large-tree die-off; forest shifted to new equilibrium (degraded forest, not savanna)</li>
<li>Longest-running, most extensive tropical forest drought study conducted</li>
<li>Monitoring: soil, air, tree growth, sap flow, roots; two 40-meter towers with NASA radar</li>
</ul>

## Climate/Conservation

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Sea Level Rise Could Displace Habitats of Over 42% of Land Vertebrates
A Science study of 34,120 species found climate-driven sea level rise could cause habitat loss for over 42% of terrestrial vertebrates by 2100. Between 14,325 and 15,324 species will lose habitat across IPCC scenarios, though only 5.9% are currently listed as at-risk.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>42% of terrestrial land vertebrates face habitat loss by 2100</li>
<li>14,325–15,324 species affected depending on climate scenario (5 IPCC scenarios examined)</li>
<li>Up to 63% birds, 52% mammals, 36% reptiles, 24% amphibians affected</li>
<li>Amazon River delta and Southeast Asia face most significant losses; 96% refuge areas outside protected areas</li>
</ul>
