### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> California Prepares for Convergence of El Niño Floods, Wildfires, and Extreme Storms
California is preparing for a severe winter combining record El Niño rainfall, wildfire risk, and extreme heat threats. The strongest El Niño on record is expected to bring multiple hazards simultaneously. The U.S. National Weather Service issued two September coastal flood advisories for the San Francisco Bay Area in October 2026—the first time it has ever issued a September coastal flood advisory for that region. Coastal flooding, beach erosion, and high tides have already occurred along the California coast. Models show a more than 90% chance that this El Niño will impact California. Disaster response agencies face budget cuts that have weakened their capacity to respond. Global impacts are projected: climate scientists have estimated that by February, more than 450,000 people worldwide could die from temperature spikes linked to the strong El Niño, with additional deaths from floods, droughts, wildfires, and rain. Tens of millions globally could face food insecurity, and global food prices could rise.
