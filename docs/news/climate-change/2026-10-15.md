The president of the United States administration cut federal NOAA funding dramatically after taking office in 2025, reducing grant awards by 74% to 229 by September 2, 2026 compared to an average of 891 in prior years. At least 26 existing grants were terminated, including $1.2 million for New Orleans climate-resilient water management and $2.5 million for Northwest tribal climate resilience projects. The administration used AI-flagged keywords like 'indigenous' and 'climate change' to target cancellations.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Arctic wildlife research declines as geopolitical competition rises
Arctic wildlife research funding is declining sharply as governments prioritize national security and rare mineral extraction over environmental monitoring. Canada shut down a 30-year eider duck monitoring station in Hudson Bay in 2024, leading to a population crash from 8,000 nesting pairs to a few hundred. Hundreds of circumpolar programs have been shuttered, creating a critical monitoring gap during rapid climate-driven Arctic ecosystem changes.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Canada Environment department closed Hudson Bay station tracking eiders for 30 years</li>
<li>Eider population crash: 8,000 nesting pairs to few hundred after closure</li>
<li>Federal research continues in only 3 of 14 Canadian Arctic migratory bird sanctuaries</li>
<li>Funding decline described as &#x27;unprecedented&#x27;; driven by geopolitical competition and rare earth mineral search</li>
</ul>
