### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Unlocking Extended Cycle Life in Lithium Metal Batteries
Researchers from three Chinese universities designed a gel polymer electrolyte with triphenyl phosphate using coaxial electrospinning paired with a pre-formed lithium fluoride-rich solid electrolyte interphase to extend lithium metal battery cycle life. The dual-confinement design suppresses corrosion, accelerates ion transport, and enables stable cycling at high current densities while addressing prior challenges where flame retardants triggered decomposition.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Published September 23, 2025 in Carbon Energy (DOI: 10.1002/cey2.70077)</li>
<li>Gel polymer electrolyte: 70 wt.% triphenyl phosphate via coaxial electrospinning</li>
<li>Dual-confinement design addresses lithium corrosion from flame retardants penetrating SEI</li>
<li>Suppresses dendrite growth, stabilizes interfacial chemistry, addresses electrolyte flammability</li>
<li>Enables stable cycling at high current densities</li>
</ul>

## Biomedical Engineering

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> In-Body Networking System for Wearable-Implantable Therapeutic Communication
Georgia Tech engineers developed Smart Wireless Autonomous Networking System (SWANS), enabling wireless communication between implantable and wearable medical devices using the body's natural ionic conductivity. Published in Science, the system achieves more than ten times the communication coverage of radiofrequency methods, transmitting through 30+ cm of tissue. Circuits use only passive micrometers-sized components consuming microwatt power, enabling year-plus device operation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Published Science, September 24, 2026; led by Alex Abramson</li>
<li>Uses body&#x27;s ionic conductivity instead of Bluetooth or NFC</li>
<li>Transmits through 30+ cm tissue; 10x coverage of radiofrequency methods</li>
<li>Passive circuits, micrometer-sized components, microwatt listening-mode power</li>
<li>Enables sensor-triggered therapeutic responses (medication release, nerve stimulation) across body without device alignment or proximity</li>
</ul>

## Neuroscience

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Study shows dogs segment speech using consonant patterns like humans
Hungarian researchers found that dogs' brains segment speech using consonant-pattern recognition, previously thought unique to humans. Dogs listening to continuous nonsense words showed 42% stronger neural responses to consonant-based regularities versus vowels and 55% stronger versus random syllables. The consonant bias likely develops from exposure to household human speech.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study by Attila Andics, Eötvös Loránd University</li>
<li>20 dogs measured via EEG; consonant response 42% stronger than vowel, 55% stronger than random</li>
<li>Consonant-based bias in speech segmentation previously thought unique to humans</li>
<li>Dogs develop consonant-preference from household human speech exposure</li>
<li>Consonant bias may vary by language; weaker/absent in Mandarin or Danish speakers</li>
</ul>
