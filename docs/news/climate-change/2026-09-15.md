## Climate-Resilience

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Indigenous lawyers invoke climate change as human rights violation at International Court of Justice
Human rights lawyer Julian Aguon secured a unanimous 2025 International Court of Justice ruling establishing that countries must legally limit greenhouse gas emissions. Arguing that climate change violates fundamental human rights—including the right to life, culture, and self-determination—Aguon gathered testimony from Indigenous communities across Melanesia and Micronesia documenting how climate impacts destroy homes, traditions, and livelihoods. The ruling provides legal tools for communities to pursue climate action or seek damages in national courts.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>ICJ ruling (2025): countries have legal obligation to limit greenhouse gas emissions</li>
<li>Climate change framed as violation of right to life, culture, and self-determination</li>
<li>Yakel village, Vanuatu: yam crop ceremonies not performed in years due to climate impacts</li>
<li>Lawyer: Julian Aguon, Indigenous Chamorro from Guam, founder of Blue Ocean Law</li>
</ul>
