### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> COP31 climate conference ends without fossil fuel consensus for first time since 2009
COP31, held in Antalya, Turkey in November 2026, concluded without a consensus cover decision for the first time since 2009. The conference, which ran two days beyond its scheduled end date, failed to reach agreement on an overarching statement due to deep divisions over whether fossil fuels and carbon capture should be mentioned, and disagreements on climate financing levels from developed to developing nations. Countries did adopt several decisions including a program to help workers and communities transition away from fossil fuels and a plan to assess climate adaptation progress. The European Union, several Latin American countries, and Pacific Island states rejected the proposed text. A central aim was to set voluntary global goals for economic electrification and increase funding for developing countries to adapt to climate impacts. This outcome follows the 2023 Dubai agreement where delegates first agreed that a "just and orderly" transition away from fossil fuels is needed to slow global warming. The Marshall Islands climate envoy stated the country would not accept "false solutions" to compromise on the agreement.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> UN climate talks collapse for first time in 17 years over fossil fuel language
The UN climate conference ended without an agreement for the first time in 17 years on November 23, 2026. Negotiations in Antalya, Turkey, collapsed over language on fossil fuels. The European Union, Latin America, and Pacific island nations objected to a proposal by petrostates—led by Saudi Arabia and Russia—to omit mention of fossil fuels from the final agreement. Irish environment minister Darragh O'Brien said it was "unconscionable that we would have a climate pact that makes no reference to fossil fuels." The rejected draft also included no firm commitments from wealthy countries to fund a climate reparations fund for poorer nations established in 2022. The United States did not send an official delegation to the conference; since the president of the United States returned to office, the U.S. has moved to pull out of the UN climate treaty. Under UN climate process rules, any agreement requires consensus approval, giving every country—including major fossil fuel producers—effective veto power. Former vice president Al Gore called for reforming the process "to stop polluters from blocking progress." Climate Analytics head Bill Hare noted that countries refusing to accept the document "drew a line in the sand" by rejecting a pact with no reference to phasing out fossil fuels.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Midwest Magnesium Recycler Eliminates Potent Greenhouse Gas SF6 Emissions
Advanced Magnesium Alloys Corp., located in Anderson, Indiana, and one of the world's largest magnesium recyclers, has eliminated its sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) emissions. The company was the United States' largest emitter of SF6, the most potent greenhouse gas. In 2023, the facility used 10 metric tons of SF6 as a cover gas to prevent molten magnesium from burning during recycling operations, releasing it directly into the atmosphere. Those 2023 emissions were equivalent to the annual greenhouse gas pollution of approximately 60,000 automobiles. In 2024, the company switched to a climate-friendly alternative, reducing SF6 emissions to zero. The facility operates in a former General Motors plant with approximately 40 employees.
