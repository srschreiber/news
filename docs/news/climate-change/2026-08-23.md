### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Study finds 15% of global warming driven by overlooked atmospheric pollutants not covered by climate treaties
A new peer-reviewed study published in Science identifies carbon monoxide and non-methane volatile organic compounds as responsible for approximately 15% of human-driven global warming through indirect atmospheric interactions. These pollutants are not included in international climate policy frameworks, despite their significant warming contribution through secondary effects on greenhouse gases.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Carbon monoxide and non-methane volatile organic compounds account for 15% of human-driven global warming</li>
<li>These pollutants do not directly warm the planet but trigger atmospheric reactions that enhance greenhouse gas effects</li>
<li>Currently absent from international climate treaties that form the basis of national emissions pledges</li>
<li>Study led by Ilissa Ocko, senior climate scientist at Spark Climate Solutions and former U.S. State Department climate advisor</li>
<li>Published Thursday in the journal Science</li>
</ul>
