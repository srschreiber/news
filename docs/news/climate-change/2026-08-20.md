### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> China's solar expansion linked to bird diversity decline across 2,344 counties
A Science study analyzing 2,344 Chinese counties from 2014–2023 found that solar power expansion policies correlate with bird diversity declines, with each standard deviation increase in policy intensity linked to a 2.10% drop in local bird diversity indices. Habitat fragmentation from large-scale photovoltaic installations replacing meadows and farmland drives avian population losses.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>2,344 counties analyzed across ten-year period (2014–2023)</li>
<li>2.10% bird diversity decline per one-standard-deviation increase in solar policy intensity</li>
<li>Habitat fragmentation from photovoltaic installations replacing meadows and farmland identified as driver</li>
<li>China has installed nearly as many solar panels as rest of world combined</li>
<li>Nature-friendly rules could create safer spaces for birds</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Florida removes climate change content from public school textbooks
Florida education officials requested textbook publishers remove or water down climate change content during summer 2024. McGraw Hill's Marine Science textbook was altered to change "climate change is killing at least 150,000 people per year, and another 1 degree Celsius of warming would double that" to "human health will also be affected by the increase in CO2 and another 1 degree Celsius will further increase the impact." In the same textbook, "climate change" was replaced with "changes in the environment." Cengage's environmental science textbook removed the word "polluting" before "coal burning power plants" and cut a reference to University of Washington students voting to pay higher fees for renewable energy. According to Glenn Branch of the National Center for Science Education, these changes were not required by state science standards. The state is simultaneously updating its science standards for the first time since 2008; existing standards mandate instruction on human-caused climate change in several high school electives including earth science and marine science.
