### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> First Nations Community Rejects Suncor Plan to Release Mining Wastewater into Drinking Water Source
Suncor proposed releasing treated tar sands mining wastewater into the Athabasca River, the primary drinking water source for Fort Chipewyan First Nations in Alberta. Chief Allan Adam rejected the plan, noting the treated wastewater is "deadly toxic." The federal government will make the final decision; this would be the first such release into the river.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Wastewater source: Suncor tar sands operations</li>
<li>Destination: Athabasca River → Lake Athabasca (primary drinking water)</li>
<li>Suncor rationale: treated wastewater &#x27;too corrosive&#x27; for refinery use</li>
<li>Alberta backs release without environmental impact assessment; federal Canada decides</li>
<li>Fort Chipewyan: 30% higher cancer rates; C$12M 10-year health study commencing September 2026</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Iowa lead azide detonation poses long-term health risks despite mitigation efforts, experts warn
The U.S. Army planned to detonate nearly 5 tons of unstable lead azide explosives in Middletown, Iowa on November 26. Despite mitigation measures including blast containment and water misting, experts warn the toxic dust cloud will travel over 100 miles southwest, posing long-term health risks to residents from lead exposure without safe levels.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Detonation date: November 26, 2026</li>
<li>Lead azide quantity: nearly 5 tons</li>
<li>Toxic dust plume projection: 100+ miles southwest</li>
<li>Lead exposure: no safe level; harms brain, nervous system, reproduction; especially dangerous to young children</li>
<li>Mitigation: blast walls, water misters; experts warn insufficient for long-term risk prevention</li>
</ul>
