Republican lawmakers are advancing legislation to grant fossil fuel companies immunity from lawsuits, similar to protections given to the gun industry. The effort is part of a broader push to pass high-priority Republican bills before newly elected Democrats take office. In April, Republicans introduced the Stop Climate Shakedowns bill, with companion Senate and House versions led by Ted Cruz and Harriet Hageman, designed to shield fossil fuel companies from legal accountability efforts, including climate-related litigation. Climate groups and hundreds of elected leaders have opposed the measure, with the Center for Climate Integrity arguing that recent election results demonstrate voters do not support such legislation. The bill faces a potential vote before the end of 2026.

## Space Exploration

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Voyager 1 Reaches One Light-Day Distance From Earth After 49 Years
NASA's Voyager 1 space probe, launched in September 1977, has reached a distance of one light-day (16.1 billion miles) from Earth after 49 years of travel. The probe travels at 37,800 miles per hour. At this distance, radio signals from Earth take 24 hours to reach Voyager 1 and another 24 hours to return; return signals are so weak that NASA's Deep Space Network requires a massive satellite dish to receive them. Voyager 1 is the farthest human-made object ever sent into space. After photographing Jupiter and Saturn, the probe crossed the heliopause in 2012 and entered interstellar space. The spacecraft carries a gold-plated copper disk with information about human culture.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> U.S. Fossil Fuel Emissions to Decline This Year Despite Rising Power Demand
U.S. fossil fuel emissions are projected to decline in 2026 despite rising power demand. Coal consumption is down 10 percent so far this year as plants operate fewer hours, according to an Energy Innovation analysis of federal data. Power demand is set to reach a record high, driven by data center buildout, but coal continues to lose market share to cheaper renewables and natural gas. Natural gas consumption is on track to remain relatively flat as rising power plant demand is offset by reduced building use from switching to heat pumps and mild weather. Gasoline and diesel consumption is declining slightly due to elevated prices from the Iran war. Analysts project a total emissions drop of roughly 70 million metric tons of carbon dioxide—equivalent to Peru's annual fossil fuel emissions. The president of the United States' administration has pursued pro-coal policies including scrapping emissions standards for coal generators, directing federal funds to coal plants, and delaying power station retirements, yet market forces favoring renewables and natural gas are overriding these efforts.
