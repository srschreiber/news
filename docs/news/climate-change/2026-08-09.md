### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> Study finds climate change doubled likelihood of Canadian wildfire conditions
A World Weather Attribution study released on August 10, 2026 determined that human-caused climate change doubled the likelihood of the extreme weather conditions that fueled Canada's summer wildfires. The research examined the tinderbox conditions in the Northwest Territories and northwestern Ontario, finding that such weather patterns are now twice as probable as in a pre-industrial climate.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Climate change doubled the frequency of fire-conducive weather conditions in affected Canadian regions</li>
<li>Such conditions now occur roughly every six years instead of once per lifetime in pre-industrial climate</li>
<li>The 2.5 degrees Celsius of warming since industrialization made these extreme fire-weather events more likely</li>
<li>Study by World Weather Attribution, conducted rapidly but not yet peer-reviewed</li>
</ul>
