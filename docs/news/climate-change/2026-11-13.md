World Weather Attribution analysis shows climate change made Indonesia's 2026 fire season 1.6°C hotter than baseline, tripling the likelihood of extreme heat and dry conditions. Fires burned 1.9 million hectares, exposed 12.5 million people to hazardous air, and caused 400,000+ respiratory infections; landscape degradation over three decades compounded the crisis.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>1.9 million hectares of peatland and rainforest burned</li>
<li>12.5 million people across Southeast Asia exposed to hazardous air</li>
<li>400,000+ respiratory infections in Indonesia</li>
<li>Peat cover declined 45% in Borneo, 63% in Sumatra (1990–2010)</li>
</ul>
