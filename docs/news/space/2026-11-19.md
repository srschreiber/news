### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> NASA plans nuclear-powered Mars spacecraft launch in late 2028 carrying three Ingenuity-based helicopters
NASA plans to launch the Skyfall mission to Mars in late 2028 aboard a nuclear-powered spacecraft carrying three Ingenuity-based helicopters. The mission will demonstrate nuclear electric propulsion, potentially reducing Mars transit time from 6–9 months to 3–4 months, though the timeline is considered extremely ambitious for a first interplanetary nuclear mission.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Skyfall mission planned for late 2028 launch</li>
<li>Carries three helicopters based on Ingenuity (which completed 72 Mars flights)</li>
<li>Nuclear electric propulsion (reactor generates electricity for thrusters)</li>
<li>Could reduce Mars transit from ~6–9 months to ~3–4 months</li>
<li>Reactor activated only after reaching orbit to reduce launch risks</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> NASA's Orion spacecraft design inadequate for updated Moon mission plans
NASA's Orion spacecraft design proves inadequate for the restructured Artemis program requiring Orion to dock with SpaceX's Starship in high Earth orbit and fly backward to the Moon—a configuration fundamentally different from its original design. This reversal creates propulsion system failures and forces structural recertification for substantially higher accelerations.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Orion must fly backward to Moon attached to Starship</li>
<li>Propellant will move toward wrong tank inlet during translunar injection</li>
<li>Accelerations increase from planned 0.1–0.15 g to 0.8–1 g</li>
<li>European Space Agency, Airbus modifying Service Module tanks</li>
<li>Orion structure must be recertified for reverse-direction loads</li>
</ul>
