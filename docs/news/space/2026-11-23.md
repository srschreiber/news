### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Pegasus lunar terrain vehicle passes NASA PDR, scheduled for 2028 moon launch
Lunar Outpost's Pegasus Lunar Terrain Vehicle passed NASA's Preliminary Design Review (PDR) on November 23, 2026, becoming the first LTV to clear this milestone. The vehicle, derived from an earlier design called Eagle, is scheduled to launch to the Moon in 2028. Pegasus underwent human-in-the-loop testing with suited astronauts from Johnson Space Center, evaluating design, usability, comfort, soil mechanics, traction, and mobility with spring mesh tires. The company built three prototypes for these tests. Following PDR approval, Lunar Outpost plans to test a drivable version in simulated lunar environments, with and without NASA astronauts. Flight-ready delivery to NASA is planned for late 2027. The rover is expected to map lunar surface terrain at approximately 3.7 miles (6 km) per day and will be remotely operated initially before human missions. It will support up to three moonwalks per Artemis mission. NASA's LTV requirements include carrying up to 176 pounds (80 kg) of lunar material and tools and traveling up to six miles (10 km) on a single charge. The Pegasus is backed by $220 million in NASA funding and built on lessons from the Mobile Autonomous Prospecting Platform (MAPP) rover sent to the Moon in 2025. Astrolab's competing design, the Crewed Lunar Vehicle 1 (CLV-1), delivered a mockup to Axiom Space for evaluation by Artemis astronauts.
