### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA Administrator Issues Directive to Loosen Planetary Protection Rules for Mars Missions
NASA administrator Jared Isaacman has issued a directive easing decades-old "planetary protection" regulations designed to prevent Earth microbes from contaminating Mars and other worlds. The policy shift argues that sterilization standards established in 1969 no longer reflect current scientific knowledge and technological capabilities, though critics worry it increases contamination risk to pristine environments.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>The 1969 rules required less than a one-in-10,000 chance of exposing Mars to Earth-origin microbial life; the new directive seeks to modernize these standards</li>
<li>NASA administrator Jared Isaacman cited improved sterilization technology and the need to lower mission costs and increase commercial capabilities</li>
<li>The policy shift was announced via an internal NASA message obtained by watchdog sites NASA Watch and Astrobiology.com</li>
<li>Original planetary protection rules were established in 1964 by the International Council for Science&#x27;s Committee on Space Research (COSPAR) amid Cold War space race concerns</li>
</ul>
