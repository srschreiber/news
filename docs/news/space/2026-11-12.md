### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> NASA Perseverance rover confirms organic compounds in ancient Martian rock samples
NASA's Perseverance rover confirmed complex organic carbon in two ancient Martian rocks located in the Bright Angel formation within Jezero crater, according to research published in Science Advances on November 12, 2026. The organic material was detected just beneath the rock surface using the SHERLOC (Scanning Habitable Environments with Raman & Luminescence for Organics & Chemicals) instrument, which employs Raman spectroscopy to identify minerals and organic compounds without damaging samples. The detected material is macromolecular carbon (MMC), consisting of large interconnected networks of carbon atoms. MMC can form through biological activity or non-biological chemical processes, so its presence alone cannot confirm past life on Mars. The surrounding geology indicates the material may have entered or formed during at least two separate periods of Mars' geological history, billions of years ago when water flowed through a river channel into Jezero crater and carried sediment that hardened into mudstone. The organic carbon was found alongside carbonate and sulfate minerals that developed as water altered the rocks. The study was co-led by Ashley Murphy, a postdoctoral researcher at the Planetary Science Institute, and Kyle Uckert, Deputy Principal Investigator of the SHERLOC instrument. This finding builds on a September 2025 NASA announcement that Perseverance had identified a potential biosignature in the same rocks.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Europe's Hera spacecraft inspects asteroid struck by NASA's 2022 planetary defense test
The European Space Agency's Hera spacecraft has begun close-range observations of the Didymos binary asteroid system, located 197 million kilometers from Earth. Hera captured its first close-up images in November 2026, taken from 60 kilometers away using its monochromatic Asteroid Framing Camera. The system consists of the larger asteroid Didymos (approximately 780 meters in diameter) and its moonlet Dimorphos (approximately 151 meters in diameter). Hera is now conducting observations from 25–30 kilometers away and will approach to approximately 20 kilometers in the coming month. The mission investigates the aftermath of NASA's DART (Double Asteroid Redirection Test) spacecraft, which deliberately struck Dimorphos in September 2022 at approximately 6.1 kilometers per second. The 580-kilogram DART spacecraft's impact shortened Dimorphos's orbital period around Didymos by approximately 33 minutes—a change greater than previously predicted—and nudged the moonlet into a slightly different path around the Sun. The impact ejected rubble and boulders, with a debris trail extending more than 10,000 kilometers into space for months. Hera carries 25 science instruments and will deploy two shoebox-sized CubeSats, Milani and Juventas, to conduct mineralogical analysis and ground-penetrating radar surveys of the asteroid's subsurface structure. Hera's investigations aim to improve scientific understanding of asteroid impacts and strengthen future planetary defense efforts. Detailed images of both asteroids were scheduled for release on November 25, 2026.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA's nuclear-powered Moon rover to launch in 2028 using Curiosity and Perseverance parts
NASA announced the Polar Rover for Observation, Mapping, and In-Situ Exploration (PROMISE), a nuclear-powered six-wheeled Moon rover set to launch at the end of 2028. The rover is being developed by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory using spare flight components and engineering test models from the Mars rovers Curiosity and Perseverance, reducing development cost and time. PROMISE will carry a drill to extract regolith from up to one meter below the lunar surface, spectrometers to analyze material for ice and resources, temperature and radiation sensors, visible light cameras, and a High Performance Spaceflight Computing system. The rover will be powered by a nuclear system enabling operation during lunar night and in permanently shadowed regions at the South Pole. Blue Origin will provide the launch rocket and Mark 1 lander for surface deployment. NASA's cost estimate for the mission is between $94 million and $140 million. The rover will scout for resources to sustain human presence and identify potential landing sites for future missions.
