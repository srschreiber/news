### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA images new lunar impact crater from rare event
NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter discovered crater McGetchin, the largest newly formed impact crater in the solar system at 728 feet wide and 141 feet deep, formed between April and May 2024. The impact, caused by a 3–6 story asteroid, represents a rare lunar event occurring roughly once per century.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Crater McGetchin: 728 feet wide, 141 feet deep</li>
<li>Formed April 11–May 22, 2024</li>
<li>Impactor estimated 3–6 stories tall</li>
<li>Such large impacts occur approximately once every 132 years</li>
<li>Three times larger than previous record holder from a decade earlier</li>
<li>Material ejected over 66 miles across lunar surface</li>
<li>Findings published September 16, 2026 in Science Advances</li>
</ul>
