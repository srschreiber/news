### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA Study: Space Station Astronauts Chronically Sleep-Deprived, Often Use Medication
NASA data shows that astronauts aboard the International Space Station frequently sleep fewer than six hours per night despite eight-and-a-half-hour allocations, with three-quarters using sleep medication.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>44% of ISS nights: sleep less than 6 hours despite 8.5 hours scheduled</li>
<li>ISS average: 6.09 hours/night; shuttle average: 5.96 hours/night</li>
<li>75% of astronauts reported using sleep medication in orbit</li>
<li>Zolpidem ~75% of medicated shuttle nights; extended-release added 12%</li>
<li>61 of 78 shuttle crew took sleep drugs on 52% of in-flight nights; 60% before spacewalks</li>
<li>Hypnotic use ~20x higher than general US population</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA discovers evidence of repeated water activity on Mars
NASA's Perseverance rover found evidence that rocks in Mars' Jezero Crater interacted with water on at least three separate occasions, including carbon-dioxide-rich groundwater activity, an ancient lake phase, and heated underground-water events. The findings preserve evidence relevant to searching for signs of ancient microbial life.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Three separate water-interaction episodes detected in Jezero Crater</li>
<li>SuperCam analyzed 185+ bedrock targets for chemistry and mineralogy</li>
<li>First episode: CO2-rich groundwater reacting with olivine created carbonate ridges</li>
<li>Later event produced calcium sulfate and fluorite mineral veins from heated water</li>
<li>Sequence determined but timing of events remains unknown</li>
</ul>
