### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> SpaceX tells NASA it will retire Crew Dragon after 2030, halting low-Earth orbit human spaceflight
SpaceX has informed NASA that it will no longer fly Crew Dragon missions to low-Earth orbit after 2030, effectively ending the company's role in NASA's commercial space station economy. The decision threatens plans for private space stations and represents a significant shift in NASA's two-decade strategy to foster a commercial human spaceflight sector.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>SpaceX agreed to support the International Space Station until 2030, then plans to retire Crew Dragon</li>
<li>SpaceX has declined orders from private space station companies (Axiom Space, Voyager Space, Vast Space) for Crew Dragon missions</li>
<li>NASA invested $3.1 billion in Crew Dragon development but only required SpaceX to fly six missions; SpaceX has flown 13 and exceeded obligations</li>
<li>SpaceX now derives majority revenue from Starlink, reducing reliance on NASA as a customer</li>
</ul>
