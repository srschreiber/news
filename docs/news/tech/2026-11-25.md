### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Cardiovascular Risk Detectable Decades Before Heart Attack or Stroke
Research from the University of Edinburgh examining over 100,000 people found cardiovascular risk detectable up to 30 years before heart attack or stroke. Systolic blood pressure was consistently higher—4 mmHg three decades prior, increasing to 7 mmHg closer to events—and biomarker changes were visible up to 10 years before symptoms.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study cohort: 100,000+ people (1985–2019) across US, Norway, UK</li>
<li>Cardiovascular risk detectable up to 30 years in advance</li>
<li>Systolic BP: 4 mmHg higher 3 decades before event, 7 mmHg closer to event</li>
<li>NT-proBNP biomarker changes detectable up to 10 years before events</li>
<li>Younger event-sufferers more likely to have diabetes and higher BMI in preceding decades</li>
</ul>

## AI Capabilities

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Frontier AI Systems Solving Physics Problems Once Taking Experts a Full Day
Frontier AI models have solved theoretical physics problems that previously required a full day of work from leading physicists. Suvrat Raju, a professor at the International Centre for Theoretical Sciences in Bengaluru, India, created the TheoPhysEval benchmark using unpublished problems contributed by physicists—real research findings never posted online to prevent AI models from having memorized answers. GPT-6 Astra solved 49 of 50 problems correctly (98% success rate), with the one missed problem described as a trick question. The model identified subtle errors in human-written solutions for problems it initially appeared to get wrong. Each correct answer cost less than $3 of compute on average and required only a single human prompt. The benchmark became saturated upon completion, with Raju stating the model had reached "the ceiling of well-defined calculations that people can do."

## Platform Reliability

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Microsoft Declares 'Code Red' After Massive Outlook Outage Affecting Millions
Microsoft internally declared 'Code Red' following a nearly 24-hour outage of Exchange Online and Outlook that disrupted email for millions of customers in November 2026. The outage was triggered by a networking bug that had been present on Microsoft 365 networking gateway servers since July without prior impact. Microsoft 365 Core president Jason Henderson characterized it as the most significant outage in the company's history for its customers. In response, Microsoft is implementing load shedding, geographic traffic partitioning, and faster recovery mechanisms, with a target of resolving future outages within one hour. The company will deploy AI agents to assist engineers in diagnosing issues and has established daily meetings with the Exchange Online engineering team and weekly reviews.
