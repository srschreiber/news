### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Alcohol Use Reduces Immunotherapy Efficacy Against Cancer
A University of North Carolina study published in Science Signaling found that regular alcohol use impairs anti-PD-1 immunotherapy effectiveness by approximately 50% in mouse models and increases tumor recurrence. Patients reporting eight or more drinks per week showed poorer survival after immune checkpoint inhibition.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Moderate-to-heavy ethanol (3–6 drinks/day equivalent) reduced immunotherapy efficacy ~50%</li>
<li>Ethanol reduced tumor-infiltrating T cells and shifted CD4+ phenotype</li>
<li>Patients with 8+ drinks/week had poorer immunotherapy survival</li>
<li>76.4% of cancer patients continue drinking despite clinical advice</li>
</ul>
