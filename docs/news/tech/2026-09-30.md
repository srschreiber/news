### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Pentagon Personnel Agency Suffers Data Breach Affecting 3 Million People
The U.S. Defense Manpower Data Center (DMDC) disclosed a nine-month unauthorized access to a file-sharing server discovered in July 2026, exposing personal information of 2.76 million living individuals and 294,000 deceased persons. The breach resulted from an unpatched vulnerability that allowed attackers to access sensitive military and civilian personnel records from October 2025 through mid-July 2026.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Unauthorized access to DMDC file-sharing server lasted approximately 9 months (October 2025–July 2026)</li>
<li>Exposed data includes Social Security numbers, names, dates of birth, contact details, and military occupational specialties for 2.76 million living individuals and 294,000 deceased individuals</li>
<li>Vulnerability was discovered on July 16, 2026, and patched immediately; system was restored</li>
<li>No evidence of misuse of accessed information at time of disclosure</li>
<li>No known cybercrime group has claimed responsibility for the attack</li>
<li>Breach duration: Oct 2025 to mid-July 2026 via unspecified file-sharing vulnerability</li>
<li>~2.8 million living individuals affected; ~300,000 deceased persons&#x27; records exposed</li>
<li>Data included SSNs, names, DOB, race, military service details—stored unencrypted</li>
<li>DMDC maintains 60+ million records for military and civilian staff, family members</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> 16-year-old researcher discovers Microsoft bug, gains admin database access
A 16-year-old researcher named Faav discovered an authentication vulnerability in Microsoft's Titan internal analytics platform that granted administrator database access without valid credentials. The vulnerability stemmed from Titan's API failing to verify login token signatures. Faav, assisted by an AI tool he built called Antares, accessed databases containing approximately 17.3 trillion rows. Microsoft patched the flaw and awarded a $5,000 bug bounty.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>16-year-old Faav discovered authentication bypass in Microsoft Titan analytics service</li>
<li>Vulnerability allowed admin access to ~17.3 trillion database rows</li>
<li>Faav built AI tool Antares to assist in research and discovery</li>
<li>Microsoft awarded $5,000 bounty; patched vulnerability by locking down API</li>
</ul>
