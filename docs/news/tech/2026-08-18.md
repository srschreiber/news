### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Study: AI-generated images often untraceable to training data as datasets grow
MIT CSAIL researchers identified "attribution decay" in large generative AI models: as datasets expand, individual training images become increasingly disconnected from generated outputs, making attribution impossible at scale. The finding has implications for lawsuits, licensing agreements, and regulatory responsibility frameworks.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Phenomenon termed &#x27;attribution decay&#x27; as datasets grow</li>
<li>At large scale, removing single images produces no change in model output</li>
<li>Tested on model trained on artwork from 744 public domain artists</li>
<li>If removing training data changes nothing in output, that data cannot be attributed to the output</li>
<li>Implications for artist attribution, licensing, and regulatory responsibility</li>
</ul>

Initial access occurs via Teams social engineering; the malware uses SharePoint and Teams TURN servers to maintain persistence while harvesting credentials and enabling lateral movement.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>SharePoint dead drop polls every 15 seconds; Teams TURN servers enable WebRTC relay-based interactive access</li>
<li>Harvests credentials via bogus lock screens, establishes reverse SOCKS5 tunnels to internal targets on ports 445, 3389, 5985, 1433</li>
