An Icelandic geothermal well accidentally struck magma at 900°C in 2009, creating the hottest production well ever measured and generating five to ten times the power of conventional geothermal wells. The well eventually failed due to corrosion and thermal shock in 2012, but demonstrated the potential for magma-enhanced geothermal systems.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Well reached 900°C magma at 2-kilometer depth at Krafla volcanic caldera</li>
<li>IDDP-1 produced superheated steam above 450°C at ~140 bar wellhead pressure</li>
<li>Potential output: 36 megawatts, five to ten times typical geothermal well output</li>
<li>Well destroyed by corrosion and thermal shock in July 2012</li>
</ul>
