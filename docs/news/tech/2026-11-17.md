## Funding

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Isomorphic Labs raises $5 billion Series C to advance AI drug discovery to clinical trials
Isomorphic Labs, the AI drug-discovery platform spun out of Google's DeepMind in 2021, raised $5 billion in Series C funding to advance drug candidates toward clinical trials. The funding was co-led by Greenoaks Capital and Sequoia Capital, with Andreessen Horowitz participating as a new investor. All existing backers participated, including Alphabet's venture divisions GV and Capital G, Thrive Capital, MGX, Temasek, and the UK Sovereign AI Fund. The company's AI drug-design engine uses simulation models to explore chemistry for drug design, building on DeepMind's AlphaFold protein-folding breakthrough. Isomorphic has reported positive preclinical results in oncology and immunology, and has partnerships with pharma companies including Eli Lilly, Novartis, and Johnson & Johnson. The Series C comes after a $2.1 billion Series B round in May 2026. Bloomberg reported the company was targeting a $50 billion valuation, though Isomorphic did not disclose the actual valuation. Founder and CEO Demis Hassabis, a Nobel Prize winner for AlphaFold work alongside John Jumper, stepped down as head of DeepMind to focus on drug discovery and moved into a chairman role at DeepMind.

## Robotics

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Genesis AI Secures $500 Million Series A to Scale Dexterous Robot Production
Genesis AI announced a $500 million Series A funding round led by Khosla Ventures, Premji Invest, and Eclipse. The funding will be used to scale production of dexterous humanoid robots. The company was co-founded in 2024 by CEO Zhou Xian and president Théophile Gervet, both former Carnegie Mellon University graduate students. Genesis previously raised a $105 million seed round in July 2025. The company's strategy centers on vertical integration: developing its own training data, generative AI model, and humanoid robot design in parallel. Genesis gathers training data using sensor-equipped gloves worn by over 100 workers worldwide, with the same glove technology integrated into the company's robots. The robots' hands are designed to mimic human hand proportions to enable manipulation tasks like sorting and wire handling. Genesis developed simulation capabilities using a physics algorithm co-authored by adviser Minchen Li. The company's flagship robot is called Eno. In a demonstration, the robot performed a complex workflow involving picking up boxes, scanning them, placing them in a larger box, folding, taping, and applying a label—though the robot struggled with peeling a small adhesive label in a second run-through.

## Food Technology

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Upside Foods Scales Cultivated Chicken Production to One Million Pounds Annually
Upside Foods announced on November 17, 2026, that it has achieved a 10,000-fold scale-up in cultivated chicken production, now capable of making over one million pounds of edible, lab-grown chicken annually without killing animals. The company has reached price parity with premium organic chicken, a major milestone after the first lab-grown hamburger cost $300,000 to produce in 2013. Upside Foods secured regulatory approvals from the FDA and USDA and is now available at several restaurants in California. The cultivated chicken matches the taste of conventionally raised chicken, as demonstrated in a taste test where a prominent internet personality was unable to distinguish between a sandwich made with Upside Foods chicken and one made from conventionally killed chicken. According to the company, cultivated meat production uses less water, land, and energy than conventional meat production, and reduces the risk of E. coli, Salmonella, and antibiotic resistance while lowering cholesterol and saturated fat.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Optical Computing Gains Momentum with AI Investment and Market Growth
The optical computing industry is experiencing renewed growth driven by artificial intelligence applications. Market valuation of optical computing companies totaled $2.22 billion as of November 2026, up from $0.4 billion in 2022. The sector has remained largely in research for over 70 years but has recently emerged as a fast-growing industry. Startups like Optalysys are developing technologies such as specially engineered waveguides to add computational capacity to networking paths, performing computations while data transits through data centers. The push to reduce energy consumption in AI inference is contributing to the sector's momentum.
