### <span class="imp imp-mid" title="Importance 5/10" aria-label="Importance 5 of 10">5</span> Trump Administration Removes Habitat Protection from Endangered Species Act Definition of 'Harm'
The Trump administration finalized a rule on July 10 removing habitat destruction from the regulatory definition of "harm" under the Endangered Species Act, effective September 14. The change weakens protections for endangered species by allowing farming, drilling, mining, logging, and development to proceed in critical habitats without violating the law, overturning a 30-year legal interpretation upheld by the Supreme Court.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Habitat destruction no longer counts as illegal &#x27;take&#x27; under the Endangered Species Act as of September 14, 2026</li>
<li>The rule removes decades-old regulatory language that treated severe habitat damage as &#x27;harm&#x27; to protected wildlife</li>
<li>Direct injury and killing remain prohibited, but habitat loss outside designated critical habitat areas now receives no ESA protection</li>
<li>The change applies to private projects with no federal funding or permits, limiting federal agency consultation requirements</li>
<li>The decision reverses the 1995 Supreme Court ruling in Babbitt v. Sweet Home that protected habitat as part of species protection</li>
</ul>
