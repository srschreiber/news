## Conservation

### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Trump administration weakens endangered species habitat protections, raising concerns for cross-border wildlife
The Trump administration rescinded a decades-old definition of "harm" under the U.S. Endangered Species Act on July 10, 2026, effective September 14, narrowing habitat protections for endangered species. Canadian experts warn the change threatens migratory animals that cross the U.S.–Canada border, including southern resident killer whales, grizzly bears, northern spotted owls, and migratory birds, prompting conservation groups to file a lawsuit in U.S. District Court.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Definition of &#x27;harm&#x27; rescinded July 10, 2026; takes effect September 14, 2026</li>
<li>Nearly a dozen conservation groups filed lawsuit in U.S. District Court challenging the repeal</li>
<li>Species at risk include southern resident killer whales, grizzly bears, northern spotted owls, and migratory birds</li>
<li>For decades, U.S. agencies interpreted &#x27;harm&#x27; to include significant habitat changes or degradation, upheld by Supreme Court in mid-1990s</li>
</ul>
