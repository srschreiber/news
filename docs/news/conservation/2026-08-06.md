### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Study finds primates as effective umbrella species for protecting thousands of other species
Research published in 2025 and reported on August 7, 2026 shows that protecting primate habitats can safeguard vast numbers of other wildlife and plants. A study of 16 primate species in China's Yunnan province found that designating just 30% of the region as a primate conservation priority could protect 86% of threatened mammals, amphibians, birds and reptiles, plus 72% of seed plants.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Protecting 30% of Yunnan for primates would protect 86% of 601 threatened and endemic vertebrate species and 72% of 4,010 seed plants</li>
<li>Primate habitat protection also safeguards over 50% of the province&#x27;s annual carbon storage</li>
<li>Conservation organizations like Rainforest Trust and Borneo Orangutan Survival Foundation advocate similar approaches, with orangutan habitat protection potentially securing 20,000 species across Borneo and Sumatra</li>
<li>Primates serve dual roles as seed dispersers and indicators of forest decline</li>
</ul>

The Bezos Earth Fund and Re:wild, co-founded by Leonardo DiCaprio, announced a $200 million initiative on August 6, 2026 to recover 100 critically endangered species across 30 countries through long-term, multi-year funding rather than short-term grants.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Partnership includes Bezos Earth Fund, Re:wild (co-founded by Leonardo DiCaprio), indigenous peoples, local communities and scientists</li>
<li>Largest single philanthropic initiative dedicated exclusively to species recovery to date</li>

### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> UK orchard conservation project uses molecular ecology to protect rare species
A three-year conservation initiative announced on August 6, 2026 brings together researchers from Aberystwyth University and conservation trusts to protect four highly specialized organisms dependent on traditional orchards using cutting-edge molecular methods and habitat restoration.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Project targets four species: noble chafer beetle, red-horned cardinal click beetle, orchard toothcrust fungus, and mistletoe marble moth</li>
<li>Three-year initiative funded by partnership of Aberystwyth University, Worcestershire Wildlife Trust, Gloucestershire Wildlife Trust, Herefordshire Wildlife Trust and People&#x27;s Trust for Endangered Species</li>
<li>Uses pheromone-based monitoring and genetic approaches to detect species at low population densities</li>
<li>Focuses on retaining veteran trees, dead wood and other microhabitat features essential for forest-dependent organisms</li>
</ul>
