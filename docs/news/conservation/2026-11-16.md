### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Wadden Sea may be added to UNESCO World Heritage in Danger list
The Wadden Sea, the world's largest unbroken system of intertidal sand and mud flats stretching along the coasts of the Netherlands, Germany, and Denmark, faces possible inclusion on UNESCO's World Heritage in Danger list. The site was awarded UNESCO World Heritage status in 2009. UNESCO is expected to send a joint inspection mission with the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) to assess the Wadden Sea in 2027, prompted by "continuing uncertainty surrounding the cumulative impacts of multiple pressures" and "indications of increasing ecosystem stress and ecological degradation." The threats include salt and gas extraction, fishing, tourism, and military exercises. The Wadden Sea is a vital staging post for millions of migratory birds and hosts thousands of species of marine creatures and plants. Hundreds of thousands of people live on islands and mainland towns and villages within the region.
