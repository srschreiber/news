### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Global Wildlife Populations Decline 73%; India Shows Conservation Success
The WWF Living Planet Report 2026 documents a 73% average decline in monitored wildlife populations globally between 1970 and 2022, with freshwater populations declining most steeply at 85%. India emerged as a conservation success, achieving population increases in tigers (3,682), greater one-horned rhinos (3,300+), and snow leopards (718).

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>73% global decline in monitored vertebrate species 1970–2022</li>
<li>Freshwater wildlife declined 85%; terrestrial 69%; marine 59%</li>
<li>Habitat loss and degradation identified as leading threat</li>
<li>India tigers: 3,682; Ganges river dolphins: 6,324 (assessed 2021–2023)</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> President Rolls Back Endangered Species Protections; Canadian Wildlife at Risk
On July 10, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Interior rescinded the decades-old definition of 'harm' under the Endangered Species Act, effective September 14, 2026. The change removes habitat-based protections that had been upheld by the Supreme Court. Canadian wildlife experts warn the change threatens cross-border species including migratory birds, spotted owls, grizzly bears, and orca whales.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Rescission effective September 14, 2026</li>
<li>Removes decades-old habitat degradation protections upheld by Supreme Court</li>
<li>Nearly dozen conservation groups filed lawsuits challenging repeal</li>
<li>Threatens migratory birds, northern spotted owls, grizzly bears, southern resident killer whales</li>
</ul>
