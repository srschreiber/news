### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> American Chestnut Trees Return After Nearly a Century of Functional Extinction
American chestnut trees, functionally extinct in the United States for nearly a century due to a parasitic blight, are being restored through new breeding and propagation techniques. The American Chestnut Foundation (TACF) has conducted research since the late 1960s at orchards in Virginia's Lesesne state forest, where healthy specimens now thrive, including a tree over 70 feet tall nicknamed 'Thompson' and a 15-meter-tall pure American chestnut named 'Gladys.' Historically, American chestnuts comprised an estimated one in four trees in Appalachian and eastern North American forests. The species was prized for its fast-growing, rot-resistant wood; sweet nuts that provided food for humans and wildlife; and tannins for leather production. New resistant strains have been created to combat the parasitic blight that nearly eliminated the species.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> President Rolls Back Endangered Species Protections; Canadian Wildlife at Risk
On July 10, 2026, the U.S. Department of the Interior rescinded the decades-old definition of 'harm' under the Endangered Species Act, effective September 14, 2026. The change removes habitat-based protections that had been upheld by the Supreme Court. Canadian wildlife experts warn the change threatens cross-border species including migratory birds, spotted owls, grizzly bears, and orca whales.
