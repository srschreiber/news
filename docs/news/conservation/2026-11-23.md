### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Falklands Oil Development Plans Threaten Unique Marine Ecosystem
The Falkland Islands government has licensed the Sea Lion oil development project, a major deepwater operation located approximately 130 miles north of the islands. The project, operated by Navitas Petroleum with Rockhopper Exploration, plans to drill 23 wells in its first two phases, with oil processed and stored aboard the production vessel Aoka Mizu, designed for a capacity of up to 55,000 barrels per day. Environmental experts warn the development threatens a globally significant marine ecosystem. The waters around the Falkland Islands and the Patagonian shelf support large colonies of albatrosses, penguins, and seabirds, as well as feeding grounds for South American sea lions, southern elephant seals, and southern right whales. Researchers emphasize that once subsea infrastructure is installed, it cannot be removed. The project's location falls within the Falkland Islands' disputed exclusive economic zone, which is claimed by Argentina. The sovereignty dispute over the islands and their vast oil reserves remains unresolved between Argentina and the UK, the current governing authority.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> UK Labour Government Considers Abolishing Environment Agency and Natural England
The UK Labour government is considering abolishing the Environment Agency and Natural England, England's two largest environmental regulators, as part of a broader review of arm's length bodies. Louise Haigh, described as the de facto deputy prime minister, announced on Monday 23 November 2026 at the CBI conference that these organisations are under review alongside other regulators including Ofwat and the Drinking Water Inspectorate. The government's stated rationale is to increase political control over development approvals and reduce bureaucratic delays, framed as part of a "rewiring the state" initiative. Ministers argue that environmental watchdogs create obstruction through multiple veto powers and statutory consultees that delay building approvals for housing and infrastructure. The proposed review will assess each body's functions to determine what should be devolved, dissolved, or delivered by government departments directly. Environmental groups have opposed the plans, with Tony Juniper, chair of Natural England, countering that the organisation has helped accelerate building by streamlining protections for species such as bats and newts.
