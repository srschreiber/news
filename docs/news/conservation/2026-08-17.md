### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Trump Administration Removes Habitat Protection from Endangered Species Act
The Trump administration finalized a rule on July 10, 2026, removing habitat destruction from the regulatory definition of "harm" under the Endangered Species Act, effective September 14. The change allows farming, drilling, mining, logging, and development to proceed in endangered species habitats without violating the law, significantly weakening decades of habitat protection policy.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Rule finalized July 10, 2026; takes effect September 14, 2026</li>
<li>Habitat destruction no longer counts as illegal &#x27;take&#x27; under the Endangered Species Act</li>
<li>Change reverses 1995 legal interpretation from Babbitt v. Sweet Home Supreme Court decision</li>
<li>Habitat loss is identified as one of the main drivers of extinction</li>
<li>Federal consultation requirements and critical habitat designations remain but do not cover all private projects or undesignated habitat areas</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Trump Administration Sued Over Endangered Species Habitat Protection Changes
The president of the United States' administration rescinded a decades-old definition of 'harm' under the Endangered Species Act, effective September 14, 2026, prompting lawsuits from nearly a dozen conservation groups. The change eliminated habitat degradation protections that the U.S. Supreme Court upheld in the mid-1990s.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Change to ESA &#x27;harm&#x27; definition effective September 14, 2026</li>
<li>Decades-old definition included habitat degradation; Supreme Court upheld it in mid-1990s</li>
<li>Administration stated change would reduce &#x27;burdens for landowners and communities&#x27;</li>
<li>Nearly a dozen conservation groups filed lawsuit challenging repeal</li>
<li>Species at risk crossing U.S.-Canada border include migratory birds, spotted owls, grizzly bears, killer whales</li>
</ul>
