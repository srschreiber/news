### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Canopy bridges in Amazon reduce roadkill and enable safe animal crossings
Eight canopy bridges were installed in Alta Floresta, Mato Grosso state, Brazil starting in 2024, connecting fragmented forest patches across a highway. Camera trap footage recorded over 15,000 safe crossings by arboreal animals including spider monkeys (Ateles chamek) and marmosets over 15 months following installation, with zero recorded roadkills during that period. The bridges are part of a conservation program involving Projeto Reconecta and the municipal government, along with local organizations and business owners motivated by concerns about wildlife-vehicle collisions and ecotourism potential. The initiative addresses the broader problem of roadkill in the region; a separate case documented a critically endangered buffy-headed marmoset (Callithrix flaviceps) killed on a highway in Espírito Santo state, highlighting ongoing wildlife mortality on Brazilian roads.
