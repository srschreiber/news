### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Bird Flu Causes Catastrophic Wildlife Die-Offs, Raising Extinction Risk Concerns
Bird flu (H5N1) has triggered catastrophic wildlife die-offs, prompting the IUCN to upgrade the southern elephant seal to vulnerable status in April 2026—the first species explicitly downgraded due to avian influenza. On South Georgia, more than 53,000 female seals died by 2025.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>H5N1 now conservation threat to at least 40 bird species (BirdLife International)</li>
<li>Southern elephant seal: first species upgraded to vulnerable explicitly due to avian influenza</li>
<li>South Georgia: 53,000+ female seals died by 2025; &gt;90% pup mortality in some colonies</li>
<li>Strain circulating northern hemisphere since 2021; now in 4 of 5 southern elephant seal populations</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Asymmetric cell division predicts human T cell vaccine response outcomes
A study shows that autophagy-regulated mitochondrial inheritance controls CD8+ T cell fate during division, with asymmetric inheritance patterns correlating with vaccine response effectiveness. Cells retaining old mitochondria show reduced memory potential, while those inheriting new mitochondria with higher turnover become long-lived responders.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Asymmetric mitochondrial inheritance drives distinct T cell fates during division</li>
<li>Autophagy-competent cells partition mitochondria asymmetrically; autophagy-deficient cells inherit symmetrically</li>
<li>One-carbon metabolism activated in cells retaining premitotic mitochondria</li>
<li>Findings suggest strategies to modulate T cell function, relevant to age-related immunity decline</li>
</ul>
