### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Cross-Fostering Strategy Revived Critically Endangered Chatham Island Black Robin Population
A landmark 1981–82 breeding intervention using cross-fostering successfully rescued the Chatham Island black robin from extinction, increasing the population from five birds to twelve in a single year. The population now numbers around 300 on Mangere and South East islands, representing one of conservation's most significant recovery successes.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Only 5 black robins remained at start of 1981-82 breeding season</li>
<li>Cross-fostering technique forced robins to produce multiple egg clutches instead of single annual nest</li>
<li>Population recovered to ~300 birds on Mangere and South East islands</li>
<li>Species had declined from 18 birds on Little Mangere Island in 1973 to 7 by 1976</li>
<li>Over 120,000 native trees planted to restore habitat before breeding intervention</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> Tergola Red Panda Trail Launched in Bhutan's Haa Valley
Bhutan has opened the Tergola Red Panda Trail, a community-led nature-based wildlife tourism initiative combining conservation with sustainable visitor experiences. The trail winds through pristine broadleaf and rhododendron forests in Haa's Tergola landscape, where a 2025 camera trap survey recorded 138 photographic captures of red pandas from a population of 42 individuals in the region.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>42 red pandas documented in Haa during national survey</li>
<li>2025 camera trap survey produced 138 photographic records</li>
<li>Trail integrates biodiversity conservation with community participation and sustainable tourism</li>
<li>Habitat includes bamboo understory, moss-laden trees, and intact forest ecosystem</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> Leopard Killings Rise in Pakistan Due to Habitat Loss and Weak Law Enforcement
Common leopards are being killed at increasing rates across Khyber-Pakhtunkhwa province in Pakistan as habitat degradation and declining prey force the predators into villages. A July 20 incident in Bajaur district exemplifies the trend, with wildlife authorities struggling to enforce protections despite the species being legally protected under the 2015 Wildlife and Biodiversity Act.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Latest killing occurred July 20, 2026 in Bajaur district&#x27;s Mamund area</li>
<li>Deforestation and prey decline pushing leopards into human settlements</li>
<li>Common leopard legally protected; killing punishable by up to 3 years imprisonment and Rs45,000 fine</li>
<li>Convictions remain rare despite legal protections</li>
<li>Incidents reported across Kohat, Swat, Kurram, Mansehra and Tirah Valley</li>
</ul>
