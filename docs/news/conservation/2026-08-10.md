### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Wildlife recoveries advancing across eastern United States despite urban pressures
A new synthesis documents significant wildlife comebacks in the eastern U.S., including the restoration of elk herds in Kentucky (roughly 10,000 animals), bison reintroductions in Illinois, and range expansions of moose, bears, and other species across New England. These recoveries demonstrate that restoration can succeed in densely settled regions but require sustained habitat preservation, funding, and management to balance conservation with human coexistence challenges.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Kentucky has restored approximately 10,000 elk across a 16-county zone—the largest herd east of the Mississippi River</li>
<li>Bison have been reintroduced to tallgrass prairie restoration at Nachusa Grasslands in Illinois</li>
<li>Forest regrowth in New England has enabled moose, bears, fishers, and otters to expand their ranges</li>
<li>Wildlife corridors and crossings are critical to reconnecting fragmented habitats and supporting species recovery</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-2" title="Importance 2/5" aria-label="Importance 2 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i><i></i></span> Dr. Eve Schlüter appointed first female director of Massachusetts Division of Fisheries and Wildlife
Dr. Everose "Eve" Schlüter has been named director of MassWildlife following a nationwide search, becoming the first woman to lead the agency. With nearly two decades of experience at the agency and a doctorate in conservation biology, Schlüter's priorities include advancing biodiversity conservation, strengthening climate resilience, and expanding access to outdoor recreation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Dr. Eve Schlüter appointed director on August 10, 2026—first woman to lead MassWildlife</li>
<li>She has served as acting director since January 2026 and brings nearly 20 years of agency experience</li>
<li>Previously served as deputy director overseeing fisheries, wildlife, endangered species, and habitat conservation</li>
<li>Led redevelopment of BioMap, a statewide conservation-planning resource created with The Nature Conservancy</li>
</ul>
