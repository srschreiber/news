### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Mass sardine die-off in South Africa threatens penguin survival and fisheries livelihoods
A mass sardine die-off began July 28, 2026 near Saldanha Bay and spread across 2,600 km of South African and Namibian coastline, caused by pilchard herpesvirus. The collapse threatens African penguins dependent on sardines and jeopardizes a fishery worth billions of rands and thousands of jobs.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Mass die-off began July 28; spread across 2,600 km including Henties Bay (Namibia) and Mbotyi (Eastern Cape)</li>
<li>Pilchard herpesvirus identified as cause; likely source imported frozen pilchards for canning or bait</li>
<li>African penguins feed exclusively on sardines and anchovies; face particular risk from population decline</li>
<li>Sardine fishery contributes billions of rands and thousands of jobs to South Africa&#x27;s economy</li>
</ul>
