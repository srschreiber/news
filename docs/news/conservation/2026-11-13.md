### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> UK Government Considers Weakening Environmental Legal Protections Under Aarhus Convention
The UK government is privately considering removing legal cost protections under the Aarhus Convention that currently cap legal expenses at £5,000 for individuals and £10,000 for organizations challenging environmentally damaging planning decisions. These protections enable community groups to afford judicial challenges to sewage pollution, new roads, and other developments; removing them could severely restrict public access to environmental justice.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Current cost caps: £5,000 individuals, £10,000 organizations</li>
<li>Proposed changes include removing caps entirely or doubling them at each stage</li>
<li>Protections enable challenges to sewage pollution, roads, datacentre developments</li>
<li>Environmental campaigner credits cap protection as essential to landmark legal action</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Fault-tolerant quantum computing with symplectic double codes
Quantinuum demonstrated the [[20, 2, 6]] C4-Helix quantum error correction code on its 98-qubit Helios processor, achieving a 3.5× reduction in qubit overhead compared to surface codes while improving logical error rates and gate fidelity. The complete fault-tolerant stack—spanning protected memory, logical computation, and logical entanglement—outperformed unencoded physical baselines without postselection.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Helios code: [[20, 2, 6]] pairs twisted toric code with block code</li>
<li>3.5× reduction in spatial qubit overhead vs. rotated surface codes</li>
<li>Logical memory error rate: 4.6 × 10^-5 per logical qubit per cycle</li>
<li>Logical Clifford error rate: 2.8 × 10^-4 per gate (4.28× improvement over physical)</li>
<li>GHZ fidelity: 99.925%–99.975% vs. 99.537%–99.791% physical baseline (+0.39%)</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Machine learning enables global eddy-resolving ocean forecasting
FuXi-Ocean, a deep learning model published in npj Climate and Atmospheric Science on May 30, 2026, delivers 6-hourly global ocean forecasts at 1/12° eddy-resolving resolution extending to 1500 m depth. Operating independently of numerical weather prediction, it addresses cumulative error amplification and maintains stable performance over extended autoregressive forecast windows.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>1/12° mesoscale-eddy-resolving resolution extending to 1500 m depth</li>
<li>6-hourly predictions with 22-hour forecast horizon</li>
<li>Operates independently of numerical weather prediction systems</li>
<li>2022 evaluation: improved performance over GOFS (WenHai) in 10-day forecasts</li>
</ul>
