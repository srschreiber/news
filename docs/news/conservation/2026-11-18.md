## Conservation

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> US offshore wind farms functioning as artificial reefs, reshaping marine ecosystems
US offshore wind turbines are functioning as artificial reefs, reshaping marine ecosystems along the coast. The phenomenon occurs when turbine structures attract bivalves and algae, which in turn attract crabs and small fish seeking food and shelter, eventually drawing larger species like black sea bass. At the Block Island Wind Farm, the United States' oldest offshore windfarm completed in 2016, a 2022 developer-funded study found that black sea bass populations increased tenfold near the windfarm compared with reference areas with similar habitat. Reef effects begin occurring rapidly after turbine installation, with mussels and other organisms beginning to colonize structures within approximately six months. The artificial reef effect has also been previously documented at other underwater structures including shipwrecks and oil rigs. The discovery is transforming understanding of how these wind installations affect ocean floor ecosystems and has implications for fishing communities, with some charter boat captains reporting increased fishing opportunities near the turbines. Researchers including Kevin Stokesbury, dean of the University of Massachusetts, Dartmouth's School for Marine Science & Technology, are actively studying what this transformation means for sea life and the fishing industry.

## Science

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Chang'e-4 and Chang'e-6 Reveal Postimpact Evolution of South Pole–Aitken Basin
China's Chang'e-6 mission retrieved the first samples from the lunar farside South Pole–Aitken basin, the Moon's largest impact basin. Analysis reveals the basin's three-layer differentiated structure formed by massive crust–mantle reorganization: 51% dunite, 44% norite, and 5% orthopyroxenite. Lunar regolith penetrating radar also revealed subsurface structure at the southwestern Apollo basin landing site showing distinct two-layer composition to 3 meters depth.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>South Pole–Aitken basin: 51% dunite, 44% norite, 5% orthopyroxenite composition</li>
<li>Basin melt sheet shows 20–30% highland crustal contribution to mantle source</li>
<li>Radar revealed two-layer regolith structure: ~1.7 m fine-grained weathered layer, lower coarse unweathered ejecta</li>
<li>Apollo basin site approximately 2.79 billion years old</li>
</ul>
