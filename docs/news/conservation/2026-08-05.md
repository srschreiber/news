### <span class="imp imp-5" title="Importance 5/5" aria-label="Importance 5 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i></span> Trump Administration Redefines 'Harm' Under Endangered Species Act, Narrowing Habitat Protections
The Trump administration finalized a rule on July 14, 2026, that redefines 'harm' under the Endangered Species Act to mean only direct physical injury to individual animals, eliminating 50 years of legal precedent that protected critical habitat. The change is expected to enable widespread habitat destruction and threaten hundreds of species with extinction, including the black-footed ferret, unless blocked by ongoing lawsuits.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Rule finalized July 14, 2026; set to take effect September 14, 2026</li>
<li>Redefines &#x27;harm&#x27; from habitat destruction + individual injury to only direct individual injury</li>
<li>Black-footed ferret population reduced from 18 individuals in 1986 to ~500 in wild; species faces extinction under new rule</li>
<li>50-year ESA track record prevented 99% of listed species from going extinct</li>
<li>Multiple lawsuits filed by conservation groups to block the rule</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Conservation Groups Sue Trump Administration Over Two Additional ESA-Weakening Rules
Conservation groups filed notices of intent to sue on July 21, 2026, challenging two rules finalized the same day that weaken the Endangered Species Act: one removes automatic protections for newly listed threatened species, and another gives outsize weight to economic considerations in protection decisions. Species affected include the Florida manatee, monarch butterflies, polar bears, and green sea turtles.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Two rules finalized July 21, 2026</li>
<li>First rule: newly listed threatened species lose automatic &#x27;take&#x27; protections (killing, trapping)</li>
<li>Second rule: economic considerations given greater weight in protection decisions</li>
<li>Species at risk include Florida manatee, Pygmy rabbit, Monarch butterflies, green sea turtles, polar bears, sage-grouse</li>
<li>Earthjustice filed notices on behalf of Sierra Club, Center for Biological Diversity, and other groups</li>
</ul>

Sources: [Defenders of Wildlife](https://defenders.org/newsroom/groups-sue-administration-over-protections-removal-threatened-species) <span class="src-badge src-research">Web Search</span>

### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Cambodia Establishes First Crocodile Genetic Conservation Area to Protect Siamese Crocodile Population
Cambodia announced new legal protections on August 5, 2026, for critical Siamese crocodile habitat in the Cardamom Mountains, establishing the country's first Crocodile Genetic Conservation Area. The 315-hectare protected wetland near O'Soam village safeguards the largest remaining wild population of this critically endangered species, which numbers only ~400 individuals in Cambodia and ~1,000 throughout Southeast Asia.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>315-hectare (780-acre) protected wetland designated August 5, 2026</li>
<li>Largest remaining wild population: ~400 Siamese crocodiles in Cambodia; ~1,000 in Southeast Asia</li>
<li>Protected area near O&#x27;Soam village in Cardamom Mountains; rediscovered there in early 2000s</li>
<li>17 crocodile eggs found in nest in 2025, confirming breeding success</li>
<li>Indigenous Chorng people&#x27;s cultural values and ranger participation critical to species survival</li>
</ul>
