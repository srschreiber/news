## Climate & Ecological Resilience

### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Utah breaks record for woodland and forest acres burned in 2026 wildfire season
Utah has burned a record 285,000 acres of woodland and forest as of early August 2026, halfway through the fire season. University of Utah research shows this represents an unprecedented concentration of tree loss, with three fires (Babylon, Cottonwood, and Widemouth 2) each exceeding 60,000 acres of vegetation burned—an event that has never occurred before in the 40-year record.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>285,000 acres of woodland and forest burned by August 5, 2026, the most on record since 1986</li>
<li>Three fires (Babylon &gt;100,000 acres, Cottonwood 97,000 acres, Widemouth 2 ~85,000 acres) each exceeded 60,000 acres of tree cover; only 10 such fires have occurred since 1986</li>
<li>27% of all forest and 20% of all woodland burned since 1986 occurred in just the last three years; 10% of high-elevation forest burned in the past decade alone</li>
<li>Economic and ecosystem impacts include reduced recreational value (outdoor recreation contributes ~$10 billion annually to Utah), flash flooding risk, and debris flows already affecting communities like Beaver</li>
</ul>
