### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Utah breaks record for forest and woodland acres burned in single year
University of Utah research shows that as of August 2026, Utah has already surpassed its record for wooded and forest areas burned in a single year, with 285,000 acres consumed. For the first time, the state has recorded three fires each burning more than 80,000 acres of vegetation, a phenomenon never documented since 1986.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>285,000 acres of woodland and forest burned in Utah by August 2026</li>
<li>Three fires (Babylon, Cottonwood, Widemouth 2) each exceeded 80,000 acres of tree coverage—unprecedented since 1986</li>
<li>10% of Utah&#x27;s high elevation forest area burned in the last decade</li>
<li>Seven of the ten largest fires since 1986 have occurred in the last 10 years</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Pacific Northwest experiences worst fire season in 30 years amid worsening climate conditions
The Pacific Northwest is facing its most severe wildfire season since the mid-1990s, driven by drought, extreme heat, and climate change that are extending fire seasons, increasing frequency, and making blazes more destructive. Forest ecology experts highlight how dense fuel accumulation and shifting climate patterns are creating a persistent "fire climate."

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Worst fire season in the Pacific Northwest in more than 30 years</li>
<li>Longer, more frequent, and more destructive wildfire seasons linked to climate change and drought</li>
<li>Historical Indigenous fire management practices contrasted with modern ecologically unsound forestry</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> Greece identifies six-week peak wildfire risk window, finds climate change extending fire season
Research by Meteo.gr of the National Observatory of Athens reveals that Greece's 23 largest wildfires over 19 years occurred between July 10 and August 20, but scientists warn that climate change is likely pushing dangerous fire conditions both earlier and later in the traditional fire prevention season.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Highest wildfire risk spans approximately six weeks, July 10 to August 20</li>
<li>All 23 of Greece&#x27;s largest wildfires (each exceeding 4,000 hectares) occurred within this window since 2007</li>
<li>Climate change and deteriorating weather conditions expected to shift fire season boundaries</li>
<li>Notable exceptions like September 2024 Corinth fire show season is already expanding</li>
</ul>
