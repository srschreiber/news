Utah's Cottonwood Fire, which started June 22 and charred over 97,000 acres, demonstrates that traditional forest management techniques like thinning and prescribed burns may no longer be effective as climate change drives unprecedented heat and drought. Researchers warn that extreme weather now overrides fuel management in determining wildfire severity, making the West's fire crisis increasingly difficult to control.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Cottonwood Fire charred 97,000+ acres and was only 95% contained by late August 4</li>
<li>Climate scientist research shows weather and climate override fuel effects in wildfire propagation</li>
<li>Severe Utah winter snow drought was 14 times more likely than pre-industrial baseline, per PNAS research published this month</li>
<li>Megafire embers can travel approximately one mile, jumping firebreaks hundreds of yards wide</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-3" title="Importance 3/5" aria-label="Importance 3 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i><i></i></span> French Wildfires Devastate Over 100,000 Acres, Threatening Vulnerable Toad Species
Catastrophic wildfires in France have burned over 100,000 acres near Bordeaux, killing millions of animals and posing severe risks to the Western spadefoot toad, a vulnerable species endemic to southern France, Spain, and Portugal. The fires, driven by climate change-induced drought, have transformed wildlife habitat into ash while driving displaced animals into urban areas where they face additional hazards.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>French wildfires burned 42,000+ hectares (100,000+ acres) near Bordeaux</li>
<li>Western spadefoot toad (Pelobates cultripes) listed as vulnerable on IUCN Red List, considered slow-moving and at high risk from ground-level fire exposure</li>
<li>Over 200,000 people fled homes in France; at least 4 firefighters died</li>
<li>Climate change increases atmospheric moisture demand, drying vegetation and creating wildfire conditions</li>
</ul>
