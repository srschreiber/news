### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Prescribed Fire Reduces Giant Sequoia Mortality by Over 50% During Wildfire
A new study from the University of Nevada, Reno demonstrates that prescribed fire can significantly improve giant sequoia survival during severe wildfires. Research at Mountain Home Demonstration State Forest found that prescribed burns cut tree mortality by more than half during the 2020 Castle Fire, offering a practical restoration technique for protecting these ancient trees after nearly two centuries of fire suppression.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Prescribed fire reduced giant sequoia mortality by more than 50% during the 2020 Castle Fire at Mountain Home Demonstration State Forest</li>
<li>Study co-authored by University of Nevada, Reno researchers including forest ecologist Sarah Bisbing</li>
<li>Results demonstrate that restoring fire regimes giant sequoias co-evolved with over millennia can counteract unprecedented modern wildfire mortality</li>
<li>Research conducted at the world&#x27;s largest giant sequoia grove, now serving as a living laboratory for wildfire resilience</li>
