### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Prescribed Fire Reduces Giant Sequoia Mortality by Over 50% During Wildfire
A new study from the University of Nevada, Reno demonstrates that prescribed burning in giant sequoia groves significantly improves tree survival during severe wildfires. Research at Mountain Home Demonstration State Forest found that prescribed fire cut tree mortality by more than half during the 2020 Castle Fire, offering a proven management approach to restore ecological resilience in these ancient forests after nearly two centuries of fire suppression.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Prescribed fire reduced giant sequoia mortality by more than 50% during the 2020 Castle Fire at Mountain Home Demonstration State Forest</li>
<li>Study co-authored by University of Nevada, Reno forest ecologists including Sarah Bisbing</li>
<li>Giant sequoias evolved with fire over millennia but face unprecedented mortality rates due to ~200 years of fire suppression and increasingly severe wildfires</li>
<li>Research conducted at the world&#x27;s largest giant sequoia grove provides real-world validation of fire-based restoration</li>
</ul>
