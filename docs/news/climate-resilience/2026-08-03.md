### <span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Prescribed Fire Reduces Giant Sequoia Mortality by Over 50% in California Study
A University of Nevada, Reno study published on August 3, 2026 found that prescribed fire and fuel-reduction treatments cut tree mortality by more than half in the world's largest giant sequoia grove during the 2020 Castle Fire. The research demonstrates that restoring fire to these ecosystems—after nearly 200 years of fire suppression—can help protect these ancient trees from increasingly severe wildfires.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Prescribed fire reduced giant sequoia mortality by &gt;50% during the 2020 Castle Fire at Mountain Home Demonstration State Forest</li>
<li>Study conducted at the world&#x27;s largest giant sequoia grove in California&#x27;s southern Sierra Nevada</li>
<li>Giant sequoias evolved with fire over millennia but faced suppression for ~200 years, leaving them vulnerable to modern severe wildfires</li>
<li>Research co-authored by University of Nevada, Reno forest ecologist Sarah Bisbing and colleagues</li>
</ul>
