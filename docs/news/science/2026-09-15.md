### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Ancient DNA reveals plague killed Siberian hunter-gatherers 5,500 years before Black Death
Ancient DNA analysis reveals Yersinia pestis killed Siberian hunter-gatherers approximately 5,500 years ago, predating the Black Death by millennia. DNA from the plague bacterium was detected in 18 of 46 individuals tested (40 percent), with an unusually high concentration of child and adolescent deaths suggesting severe lethality despite lacking later transmission-enabling mutations.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Plague detected in prehistoric Lake Baikal remains ~5,500 years ago</li>
<li>40 percent detection rate in 46 individuals tested</li>
<li>Concentrated deaths among children and young teenagers</li>
<li>Published in Nature, led by Ruairidh Macleod (Oxford)</li>
<li>Early strains may have carried severe immune-reaction genetic factors</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> UV imaging reveals possible ancient camouflage on 125-million-year-old crocodile fossil
Ultraviolet imaging of a 125-million-year-old crocodile fossil (Montsecosuchus depereti) from northeastern Spain revealed preserved soft tissues and an alternating band pattern on the tail, potentially representing the oldest known animal camouflage pattern in a crocodylomorph. The 50-centimeter specimen also showed evidence of lacking the high caudal fin present in modern crocodiles and possible sensory organs in specialized scales.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Fossil: Montsecosuchus depereti, 125 million years old, ~50 cm long</li>
<li>Location: Pedrera de Meià site, Lleida, Catalonia, Spain (Lower Cretaceous karstic lake)</li>
<li>UV imaging revealed preserved soft tissues, scales, and alternating band tail pattern</li>
<li>Possible sensory organs in scales around neck, limbs, trunk, tail</li>
<li>Specimen MGB-512 housed at Museum of Natural Sciences Barcelona</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Supermassive black hole winds found 100 times more powerful than previously measured
Using the XRISM X-ray satellite, astronomers observed quasar H1821+643 and discovered that supermassive black hole winds are approximately 100 times more powerful than previously estimated, with turbulence extending roughly 300,000 light-years beyond the host galaxy. The energy in this turbulence matches billions of supernova explosions.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>XRISM observations of quasar H1821+643 (constellation Draco, 3.4 billion light-years away)</li>
<li>Black hole winds ~100 times more powerful than previous estimates</li>
<li>Turbulence extends ~300,000 light-years beyond host galaxy</li>
<li>Energy comparable to billions of supernova explosions</li>
<li>Research led by Satoshi Yamada, Tohoku University Frontier Institute</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Scientists Identify Major Epigenetic Brain Changes Between Ages 50 and 75
Researchers identified major epigenetic and structural brain changes occurring between ages 50 and 75, including sharp decline in embryonically-derived microglia, reduced blood-brain barrier maintenance, and erosion of three-dimensional genome architecture. The findings, published in Science using single-cell methods on hippocampal tissue, suggest these changes may explain why aging is the strongest risk factor for Alzheimer's disease and neurodegeneration.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Age window: ages 50–75 shows sharp decline in embryonically-derived microglia</li>
<li>Microglia replaced by cells with pro-inflammatory molecular signatures</li>
<li>Substantial decline in blood-brain barrier maintenance populations</li>
<li>Broad erosion of 3D genome architecture across multiple brain cell types</li>
<li>Aging is strongest risk factor for Alzheimer&#x27;s disease</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Meta-Analysis of 139 Trials Finds Knee Braces Most Effective for Osteoarthritis Pain
A network meta-analysis of 139 clinical trials involving nearly 10,000 patients found knee braces most effective for knee osteoarthritis pain relief, stiffness reduction, and physical function improvement. Water-based therapy proved especially effective for pain; regular exercise provided consistent benefits. Ultrasound ranked near the bottom, while high-intensity laser and shock wave therapies showed promise in specific areas.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Meta-analysis: 139 trials, ~10,000 patients, 12 non-drug KOA treatments</li>
<li>Knee braces ranked highest for pain, stiffness, and physical function</li>
<li>Water-based therapy (hydrotherapy): especially effective for pain reduction</li>
<li>Regular exercise: consistent benefits for pain and physical function</li>
<li>Ultrasound: consistently ranked near bottom; limitations noted in trial design variation</li>
</ul>
