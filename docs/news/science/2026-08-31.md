### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Simulations suggest rocky planets could have formed within 100 million years after the Big Bang
University of Portsmouth simulations suggest rocky planets could have formed as early as 100 million years after the Big Bang—billions of years earlier than previously thought. Population III supernovae ejected heavy elements and water into gas clouds that collapsed into rotating disks around young stars, providing sufficient material for Earth-mass planetary formation and water acquisition.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Rocky planets may have formed 100 million years after Big Bang</li>
<li>Population III supernovae ejected over 100× Sun&#x27;s mass of heavy elements</li>
<li>Simulated disk contained water at levels comparable to early Solar System</li>
<li>Young star in simulation ~70% Sun&#x27;s mass with Earth-like planetary zone</li>
<li>Universe ~13.8 billion years old; 100 million years before first galaxies emerged</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Scientists uncover hidden dual superconducting states in ultrathin materials
Researchers at Hebrew University discovered that ultrathin superconductors niobium diselenide and tantalum disulfide each contain two distinct superconducting states that interact so strongly they appeared as a single unified state. The breakthrough, published in Physical Review Letters, could improve engineering of superconducting materials for quantum computers and advanced electronics.

<ul class="takeaways">
