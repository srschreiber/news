### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8.5/10" aria-label="Importance 8.5 of 10">8.5</span> CRISPR therapy cuts LDL cholesterol and triglycerides by half for a year
A Phase 1 trial of CTX310, a single-dose CRISPR-Cas9 gene-editing therapy, reduced LDL cholesterol by 52.5% and triglycerides by 47.8% at 12 months in 15 patients with medication-resistant lipid disorders. No serious adverse events were reported during one-year follow-up.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>CTX310: single-dose CRISPR therapy targeting ANGPTL3 gene in liver</li>
<li>LDL cholesterol reduction: 52.5% at 12 months (highest dose: 0.8 mg/kg)</li>
<li>Triglyceride reduction: 47.8% at 12 months</li>
<li>15 patient Phase 1 trial; no serious adverse events reported</li>
<li>Results presented ESC 2026; published simultaneously in New England Journal of Medicine</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Glucosamine linked to faster Alzheimer's progression in large health-record analysis
A health-record analysis found glucosamine use associated with a 25% higher likelihood of mild cognitive impairment progressing to dementia. The retrospective study of University of Florida Health records from 2012–2024 also found 25% higher mortality risk in established dementia patients.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Glucosamine use linked to 25% higher progression risk from MCI to dementia</li>
<li>Glucosamine use: 8% of MCI patients (2,750); 8% of ADRD patients (1,896)</li>
<li>25% higher mortality risk in ADRD group with glucosamine use (not in MCI group)</li>
<li>Study published Nature Metabolism; findings preliminary, require human clinical trials</li>
<li>~7 million Americans have Alzheimer&#x27;s disease; many take over-the-counter glucosamine</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Cat cancer genetics reveal insights for human cancer treatment
Scientists analyzed nearly 500 domestic cats across five countries and identified cancer-driving mutations strikingly similar to human tumors. The FBXW7 gene was the most common driver in feline breast cancers, present in over 50% of tumors examined.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Nearly 500 domestic cat tumors analyzed across five countries</li>
<li>FBXW7 gene mutations: most common driver in cat breast cancers (&gt;50% of samples)</li>
<li>FBXW7 mutations in human breast cancer associated with worse prognosis, paralleling cat findings</li>
<li>Similarities found in blood, bone, lung, skin, gastrointestinal, and central nervous system cancers</li>
<li>Freely available genetic resource created for other feline cancer genetics researchers</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Ancient eukaryote fossils link oxygen availability to complex life emergence
Scientists discovered over 12,000 eukaryote fossils dated to 1.7 billion years ago in Australian mudstone, revealing that early complex cells flourished only in oxygen-rich environments. The findings support the hypothesis that oxygen availability drove the emergence of complex life on Earth.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Fossil count: 12,000+ eukaryotes; oldest specimens 1.75 billion years old</li>
<li>Fossils from 1.7 billion year-old ancient inland sea in northern Australia</li>
<li>Early eukaryotes found only in oxygen-rich environments (coastal mudflats, open sea)</li>
<li>Oxygen-free settings contained only simpler prokaryotic microbes</li>
<li>Published in Nature</li>
</ul>
