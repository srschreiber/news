Rice University researchers developed a process doubling PFAS molecule size to enable destruction of these persistent pollutants. A layered double hydroxide material captures PFAS over 1,000 times better than other materials, breaking down chemicals hundreds to thousands of times faster while remaining reusable.

<ul class="takeaways">

## Environment

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Namibia Approves First Commercial Seabed Phosphate Mining Project
Namibia's environmental commissioner granted approval for the Sandpiper Project, the world's first commercial seabed-dredging operation for phosphate, located 120 kilometres off Walvis Bay. The project faces fierce legal and political opposition citing unprecedented marine risks, impacts on Namibia's 298,000-person fishing industry, and concerns that seabed excavation could release toxic substances into marine food chains.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First commercial seabed phosphate-dredging operation approved globally</li>
<li>3 million tonnes of phosphate concentrate planned annually over 20-year licence</li>
<li>Located 120 km off Walvis Bay in Atlantic Ocean; 1.7 sq km dredging area per year</li>
<li>Namibian Marine Phosphate 85% owned by Oman-based Mawarid Mining</li>
<li>Opposition leader filed appeal August 10; fishing associations threatened legal challenge</li>
<li>Marine scientists warn of heavy metal and toxic substance release risks</li>
</ul>
