### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Marine life crucial to regulating atmospheric CO₂ levels
Marine organisms regulate atmospheric CO₂ through the biological carbon pump, sequestering approximately 10 billion tonnes annually—over one quarter of human fossil-fuel emissions. Without marine life, CO₂ levels would rise 50% in pre-industrial scenarios, with mangroves and seagrasses sequestering carbon at rates up to four times higher than terrestrial forests.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Marine organisms sequester ~10 billion tonnes CO₂ annually (&gt;¼ of fossil-fuel emissions)</li>
<li>Without marine life, CO₂ would increase 50% (pre-industrial scenario), causing 1.6°C warming</li>
<li>Mangroves store average 1,000 tonnes carbon per hectare; sequester 4× faster than terrestrial forests</li>
<li>Biological carbon pump more important than solubility pump for modern ocean CO₂ sequestration</li>
</ul>

## Space Discovery

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Astronomers detect potential primordial black hole named Phoebe
Astronomers detected a possible primordial black hole named Phoebe through gravitational microlensing in the Large Magellanic Cloud in December 2019, estimated at 13 billion years old if confirmed. The object has a mass roughly three times the Moon's; alternative explanations include a rogue exoplanet or a wandering planet detected via microlensing beyond our galaxy.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Detection via gravitational microlensing on December 18, 2019 in Large Magellanic Cloud star</li>
<li>Microlensing event lasted ~1 hour; smooth, symmetrical brightening pattern</li>
<li>Phoebe mass ~3× Moon&#x27;s mass; considerably less massive than any solar system planet</li>
<li>If primordial black hole: ~13 billion years old, formed shortly after Big Bang</li>
<li>Alternative hypotheses: rogue exoplanet or first extragalactic planet detected via microlensing; identity remains uncertain</li>
</ul>
