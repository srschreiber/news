### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Scientists discover immune 'off switch' that stops inflammation
Researchers at University College London identified epoxy-oxylipins, natural immune molecules that brake inflammation by preventing excessive accumulation of intermediate monocytes. A clinical trial of drug GSK2256294 showed it increased these molecules, accelerated pain resolution, and reduced inflammatory white blood cells in both preventive and therapeutic settings.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Epoxy-oxylipins are fat-derived molecules that naturally suppress excessive immune activation</li>
<li>Trial enrolled 48 healthy volunteers receiving placebo or sEH-blocking drug GSK2256294</li>
<li>Drug increased epoxy-oxylipin levels and sharply reduced intermediate monocytes in blood and tissue</li>
<li>Findings suggest therapeutic targets for arthritis, heart disease, and diabetes</li>
</ul>

## Geology

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Water Fueled Volcanoes 3 Billion Years Ago; Ancient Australian Rocks Reveal Mantle Dynamics
Ancient rocks from Western Australia's Pilbara Craton, over 3 billion years old, reveal that water was reaching Earth's mantle and fueling volcanic activity long before modern plate tectonics developed. Scientists propose "dripduction"—a process where water-rich crustal sections sagged into the hotter mantle, releasing water that generated magma—as an early mechanism for water cycling on young Earth.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Water cycling between surface and interior occurred during Archean Eon, predating modern plate tectonics</li>
<li>&quot;Dripduction&quot; process: dense water-rich crust collapsed into mantle, releasing water to generate magma</li>
<li>Study led by Adelaide University geochemist Dr. Eric Vandenburg, published in Nature Communications</li>
<li>Early Earth was too hot for modern plate tectonic behavior, requiring alternative water-transport mechanisms</li>
</ul>
