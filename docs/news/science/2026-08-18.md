Einstein's cosmological constant, abandoned after Hubble's discovery of cosmic expansion, was rehabilitated in 1998 when astronomers found the universe's expansion is accelerating. The constant now underpins modern cosmological models by explaining this acceleration as a repulsive gravitational influence built into spacetime.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Magnetar observation provides possible evidence of quantum vacuum birefringence
Observations of magnetar 1E 1547.0-5408 using NASA's IXPE and other telescopes have detected the strongest evidence yet of vacuum birefringence, a quantum effect predicted by Heisenberg 90 years ago. The magnetar's trillion-fold stronger-than-Earth magnetic field showed X-ray polarization of 40–80% with coherent variations across its 2.1-second rotation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Magnetar 1E 1547.0-5408 shows X-ray polarization of 40–80%</li>
<li>Quantum effect predicted ~90 years ago by Heisenberg</li>
<li>Used IXPE, NICER, and Parkes radio telescope (Murriyang)</li>
<li>Published in Nature; if confirmed, first direct evidence of vacuum birefringence</li>
<li>Would demonstrate virtual particles influence light in extreme magnetic fields</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Hubble Reveals Dwarf Galaxy Merger in Early Milky Way History
NASA's Hubble Space Telescope confirmed that the dwarf galaxy "Low-Energy-Kraken-Heracles" merged with the young Milky Way approximately 11.8 billion years ago, pushing back known galactic growth by 1.8 billion years. The discovery, based on analysis of 39 globular clusters, demonstrates the early Milky Way incorporated external galaxies beyond the previously known Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus merger.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Dwarf galaxy merger 11.8 billion years ago (2 billion years post-Big Bang)</li>
<li>Merger pushed back Milky Way growth history by 1.8 billion years</li>
<li>Analysis of 39 dense globular clusters within inner 20,000 light-years</li>
<li>Published in Nature Astronomy on August 17, 2026</li>
<li>Confirms early Milky Way evolution involved multiple external galaxy mergers</li>
</ul>

<li>Currently at weakest point in at least 1,000 years</li>

<li>Collapse risks: abrupt European cooling, accelerated U.S. sea-level rise, monsoon displacement</li>
</ul>
