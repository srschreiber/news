The magnetar's trillion-fold stronger-than-Earth magnetic field showed X-ray polarization of 40–80% with coherent variations across its 2.1-second rotation.

<ul class="takeaways">

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Atlantic circulation collapse could trigger global warming surge
New research warns that weakening Atlantic currents could accelerate planetary warming while cooling northern Europe. The AMOC acts as a heat valve—strong circulation dissipates warmth to the atmosphere, but weakening forces heat to accumulate in the ocean instead, potentially raising global temperatures by the equivalent of 10 years of current emissions.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>AMOC weakening increases overall planetary temperature, contradicting prior models assuming only regional heat shifts</li>
<li>Northern Europe would cool 5–15°C (9–27°F) if AMOC collapses</li>
<li>Past weak AMOC periods produced warming equivalent to 10 years of current human emissions</li>
<li>AMOC has experienced hundreds of weak periods over the last 2.7 million years</li>
<li>AMOC weakening at four locations in western North Atlantic over past 20 years</li>
<li>Projected 50% decline by 2100, approaching potential collapse</li>
<li>Currently at weakest point in at least 1,000 years</li>
<li>Melting Greenland ice reduces surface salinity, weakening sinking mechanism</li>
<li>Collapse risks: abrupt European cooling, accelerated U.S. sea-level rise, monsoon displacement</li>
</ul>

<li>Analysis of 39 dense globular clusters within inner 20,000 light-years</li>
<li>Published in Nature Astronomy on August 17, 2026</li>
<li>Confirms early Milky Way evolution involved multiple external galaxy mergers</li>
</ul>
