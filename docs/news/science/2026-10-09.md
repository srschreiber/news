### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Nitrogen fixation 'off switch' discovery could enable self-fertilizing crops
Aarhus University researchers identified a molecular switch in root proteins that controls nitrogen-fixing bacterial symbiosis. By modifying two amino acids in Symbiosis Determinant 1, they converted defensive receptors into symbiosis-initiating ones, with early success demonstrated in barley.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Root protein receptor switch: Symbiosis Determinant 1 region</li>
<li>Modifying 2 amino acids converts defensive to symbiosis receptor</li>
<li>Early success in barley; potential for wheat, maize, and major crops</li>
<li>Synthetic fertilizer ~2% of global energy consumption; generates significant CO₂</li>
</ul>
