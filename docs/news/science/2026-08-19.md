<li>Public comment period through September 21, 2026</li>
<li>Roadless rule currently covers ~30% of national forest land (~59 million acres total)</li>

## Climate & Conservation

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Three-quarters of Indo-Pacific coral reefs at risk of drowning from accelerating sea-level rise
NTU Singapore researchers found that 76% of Indo-Pacific coral reef sites lack capacity to keep pace with rising sea levels. Most corals cannot sustain growth if sea level rise exceeds 5.3 mm/year—a threshold potentially surpassed by the 2060s—risking reef submergence and ecosystem collapse across 288 ancient reef cores from 92 sites.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>76% of Indo-Pacific reef sites surveyed cannot sustain sea-level rise</li>
<li>Study analyzed 288 ancient reef cores from 92 sites over 11,700 years</li>
<li>Coral sustainability threshold: 5.3 mm/year sea level rise</li>
<li>Current global sea level rise: ~4.5 mm/year as of 2023 (NASA)</li>
<li>5.3 mm/year threshold could be surpassed by 2060s under continued emissions</li>
<li>Reef drowning reduces sunlight, threatens fish habitats, increases coastal wave energy</li>
</ul>

## Space Exploration

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter captures Falcon 9 impact crater details
NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter imaged a fresh crater formed on August 5, 2026 when a SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage struck the Moon near Einstein Crater at 5,400 mph. The ~40-foot rocket stage, in orbit since January 2025, created a crater approximately 60 feet wide with impact force equivalent to three tons of TNT.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Falcon 9 upper stage impacted August 5, 2026 at ~5,400 mph</li>
<li>Rocket stage: ~40 feet long, ~4,900 kg, launched January 15, 2025</li>
<li>Crater: ~60 feet (18 m) diameter, &lt;10 feet (3 m) deep</li>
<li>Impact force equivalent to ~3 tons of TNT</li>
<li>LRO captured images August 11–12 at ~97 km altitude</li>
<li>Second known uncontrolled human-made object crash on Moon</li>
</ul>
