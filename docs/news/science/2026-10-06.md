### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> CERN Captures Clearest Evidence of Quark-Gluon Plasma as Primordial Liquid
CERN's Large Hadron Collider has captured the clearest evidence yet that quark-gluon plasma (QGP)—the scorching-hot matter that filled the newborn universe—behaves as a true liquid. MIT physicists leading a CERN team developed a new method to detect quarks generating wakes as they travel through the plasma, much like a duck creating ripples on water. The observations provide the first direct evidence that quark-gluon plasma responds to fast-moving particles as a unified fluid, producing waves, splashes, and swirling motion rather than behaving as a collection of independently scattering particles. The plasma is dense enough to slow down speeding quarks and respond collectively as a fluid. The research was conducted using the Compact Muon Solenoid (CMS), one of the general-purpose particle detectors at CERN, and involved members of the CMS Collaboration. By measuring wake characteristics—size, travel speed, extent, and fade duration—scientists may determine important properties of quark-gluon plasma and gain insight into how the plasma behaved during the universe's first microseconds. Quark-gluon plasma existed in the early universe at trillions of degrees for only a few millionths of a second before cooling rapidly and allowing quarks and gluons to combine into protons, neutrons, and other particles.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Stanford scientists restore cartilage in mice by blocking age-related protein
Stanford Medicine researchers discovered that blocking the protein 15-PGDH, which accumulates with age, restores cartilage in aging mice and prevents osteoarthritis. In old mice, the treatment reversed naturally occurring cartilage loss and protected animals from developing arthritis after ACL-like knee injuries. Human cartilage tissue samples from knee replacement surgery patients responded to the same inhibitor by producing new, functional cartilage. The mechanism differs from typical tissue regeneration: existing cartilage cells (chondrocytes) shifted their gene activity patterns toward a more youthful state rather than relying on stem cells. 15-PGDH is classified as a gerozyme—an enzyme that increases with age and contributes to tissue function decline. Previous work by the same group showed blocking 15-PGDH increased muscle mass and endurance in old mice, while increasing it in young animals caused muscle shrinkage. The protein has been linked to bone, nerve, and blood cell regeneration. Osteoarthritis affects roughly one in five U.S. adults and costs approximately $65 billion annually in direct healthcare expenses. Currently, no drug can reliably slow or reverse osteoarthritis; treatments focus on symptom management. Researchers suggest the approach could eventually lead to oral medication or injections that regenerate cartilage and reduce demand for knee or hip replacement surgery.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> El Niño and tropical Atlantic warming can provide early warning of Amazon heat extremes
Research published in the journal Earth's Future on October 6, 2026, found that El Niño and warming in the tropical Atlantic Ocean can provide early warning of extreme heat events across the Amazon Basin as much as seven months in advance. The study, conducted by researchers from the Australian Research Council Center of Excellence for the Weather of the 21st Century and Brazil's National Institute of Space Research, analyzed monthly climate data from 1950 to 2023 across six Amazon regions. The clearest long-range signal emerged for the March-May period, when extreme Amazon heat was associated with ocean conditions observed up to seven months earlier, according to UNSW Associate Professor Andrea Taschetto, a study co-author. Researchers used machine learning to examine compound hot-dry events, which can increase wildfire risks, damage ecosystems, and threaten human health, food security, and water supplies. However, researchers cautioned that the seven-month relationship applies specifically to extreme heat prediction, not to reliable forecasts of combined heat and drought conditions. Predictability of compound events varied considerably by season and region, with useful signals generally limited to shorter periods.
