### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Three supermassive black holes discovered in single galaxy J0148-4214
An international team led by the Max Planck Institute discovered three actively accreting supermassive black holes in galaxy J0148-4214, located 12.5 billion light years away. This is the first confirmed detection of multiple supermassive black holes in a single galaxy, suggesting efficient black hole mergers in the early universe.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First confirmed detection of three supermassive black holes in one galaxy</li>
<li>Black hole masses range from millions to ~80 million solar masses</li>
<li>Two central black holes separated by ~620 light years; third ~5,500 light years away</li>
<li>Galaxy J0148-4214 appears as it was 1.2 billion years after the Big Bang</li>
<li>Discovery made using James Webb Space Telescope in December 2024</li>
<li>Findings suggest black hole mergers contributed to rapid growth in early universe</li>
</ul>
