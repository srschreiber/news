### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Evidence Suggests Life on Earth May Have Originated Twice
Scientists at Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf found evidence that free-living cells may have originated independently twice on Earth approximately 4 billion years ago, with bacteria and archaea making separate transitions from vent-dependent chemistry to free-living existence. The last universal common ancestor possessed enzymes for only half of the necessary metabolic reactions; the other half were catalyzed by environmental metals.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Evidence suggests bacteria and archaea originated independently ~4 billion years ago</li>
<li>Study examined 420 chemical reactions in metabolic network</li>
<li>LUCA possessed enzymes for only ~50% of metabolic reactions; metals catalyzed the rest</li>
<li>Enzymes between bacteria and archaea lack same conservation as genetic code</li>
</ul>

## Space

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Three supermassive black holes discovered in single galaxy J0148-4214
An international team led by the Max Planck Institute discovered three actively accreting supermassive black holes in galaxy J0148-4214, located 12.5 billion light years away. This is the first confirmed detection of multiple supermassive black holes in a single galaxy, suggesting efficient black hole mergers in the early universe.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First confirmed detection of three supermassive black holes in one galaxy</li>
<li>Black hole masses range from millions to ~80 million solar masses</li>
<li>Two central black holes separated by ~620 light years; third ~5,500 light years away</li>
<li>Galaxy J0148-4214 appears as it was 1.2 billion years after the Big Bang</li>
<li>Discovery made using James Webb Space Telescope in December 2024</li>
<li>Findings suggest black hole mergers contributed to rapid growth in early universe</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Starlink Satellites Repurposed to Map Upper Atmosphere Density
Researchers at Kyoto University used orbital data from approximately 1,200 Starlink satellites to create the first two-dimensional snapshot of thermospheric density at 500 kilometers altitude using tomographic analysis. The results show strong consistency with European Space Agency SWARM satellite observations and could help improve satellite tracking and collision-risk reduction.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Data from ~1,200 Starlink satellites used for analysis</li>
<li>First two-dimensional thermospheric density map at ~500 km altitude</li>
<li>First tomographic analysis of upper atmosphere</li>
<li>Density patterns consistent with ESA SWARM satellite observations</li>
</ul>

The 26-kilogram vest is designed to protect vulnerable organs and may be included on future moon missions, though it was not carried on the crewed Artemis II due to space constraints.

<li>May be included on future moon missions but not on Artemis II (April 2026)</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Ozempic-like drugs may reduce addiction cravings by targeting brain's 'craving center'
GLP-1 drugs like Ozempic and Wegovy, originally developed for diabetes and weight loss, appear to reduce cravings for alcohol and addictive substances by acting on the lateral septum, a brain region connecting memory and reward. Human studies confirm reduced alcohol consumption; animal studies show reduced use of cocaine, amphetamines, opiates, and nicotine.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>GLP-1 drugs reduce cravings for alcohol, cocaine, amphetamines, opiates, and nicotine</li>
<li>Mechanism operates through lateral septum, a brain region densely packed with GLP-1 receptors</li>
<li>Lateral septum acts as neural control point between thinking about rewards and pursuing them</li>
<li>Could open new therapeutic approaches to addiction treatment</li>
</ul>
