## Health

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Higher REM sleep associated with lower risk of 83 diseases
A study of 95,559 people found that greater REM sleep was associated with significantly lower risk for 83 diseases including dementia, heart failure, and Parkinson's disease over a median 8.9-year follow-up. The research used wrist accelerometers and deep-learning algorithms to measure sleep stages, though it cannot establish causation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Greater REM sleep (47.6 min per IQR) linked to lower risk across 83 conditions</li>
<li>Dementia risk reduced 46% (HR 0.54); Parkinson&#x27;s 80% lower (HR 0.20); heart failure 26% lower (HR 0.74)</li>
<li>Sleeping &lt;5 hours increased risk for 37 conditions; optimal window 6–8 hours</li>
<li>Greater sleep irregularity and wakefulness after sleep onset linked to higher anxiety and substance use disorder risk</li>
</ul>

## Science

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> JWST observes early galaxies already seeding universe with heavy elements
The James Webb Space Telescope detected LAP1-B, a galaxy formed 800 million years after the Big Bang with metal abundance 1/240th that of the Sun—the most chemically primitive galaxy observed. The findings suggest heavy elements were being synthesized and dispersed in the early universe, seeding it with carbon and oxygen necessary for life.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>LAP1-B observed 800 million years after Big Bang; metal concentration ~1/240th of Sun (record low)</li>
<li>High carbon-to-oxygen ratio and dominant dark matter halo suggest progenitor to fossil galaxies near Milky Way</li>
<li>30 hours of observations using Webb&#x27;s spectrometers with 100× gravitational lensing magnification from MACS J0416</li>
<li>Early heavy-element synthesis indicates universe seeded with life-forming elements during first galaxies</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Astronomers Capture Unprecedented Direct Observation of Gas Giant Formation
Using the James Webb Space Telescope's NIRISS in Aperture Masking Interferometry mode, astronomers captured unprecedented observations of gas giant formation in the PDS 70 system, 370 light-years away and 5 million years old. Separate ALMA observations revealed for the first time dynamic spiral structures in a protoplanetary disk around young star IM Lupi, providing direct evidence of early planetary formation.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Two actively forming planets (PDS 70 b and c) within protoplanetary disk</li>
<li>JWST NIRISS used in Aperture Masking Interferometry mode</li>
<li>ALMA captured dynamic spiral structures in IM Lupi disk</li>
<li>Spiral structures theorized crucial to planet birth; now directly observed</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Uranus moon Ariel may harbor hidden subsurface ocean over 100 miles deep
Ariel, a moon of Uranus with a diameter of 720 miles, may have once harbored a subsurface ocean deeper than 100 miles beneath its frozen exterior, according to research analyzing its surface features and tidal stresses. The finding suggests the Uranian system could contain multiple ocean worlds.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>May have harbored subsurface ocean &gt;100 miles deep</li>
<li>Past orbital eccentricity estimated ~0.04 (40x current), amplifying tidal forces</li>
<li>Combined with Miranda evidence, suggests multiple ocean worlds in Uranian system</li>
<li>Research led by Caleb Strom, University of North Dakota</li>
</ul>
