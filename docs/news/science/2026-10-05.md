## Neuroscience

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 9/10" aria-label="Importance 9 of 10">9</span> Optogenetics breakthrough earns physiology Nobel Prize
The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, and Georg Nagel for developing optogenetics, a technique using light to control nerve cell activity. The field originated from research on light-seeking algae and enables precise observation of what specialized neurons do without physical brain damage.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>2026 Nobel Prize recipients: Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, Georg Nagel</li>
<li>Optogenetics: uses light to control nerve cell activity</li>
<li>Originated from research on Chlamydomonas algae attracted to light</li>
<li>Hijacks ion channels to activate/deactivate neurons via light stimulation</li>
<li>Overcomes limitations of genetic deletion and physical brain damage methods</li>
<li>Allows activation and shutdown of neurons marked by individual gene activity</li>
<li>Three recipients: Deisseroth (Stanford), Hegemann (Humboldt), Nagel (Würzburg)</li>
<li>Optogenetics uses light to control individual neurons within milliseconds</li>
<li>Potential applications: blindness restoration, depression, Parkinson&#x27;s, epilepsy, dementia</li>
<li>Prize worth 12 million Swedish kronor (~€1M or $1.2M)</li>
<li>Awards presentation December 10, 2026 in Stockholm</li>
<li>Discovered channelrhodopsin: light-sensitive protein from algae</li>
<li>Enables neuron control within milliseconds using blue light pulses</li>
<li>Maps neural circuits governing behaviors, memories, feelings, disease states</li>
<li>Deisseroth HHMI Investigator since 2014; 36th current/alumni HHMI Nobel laureate</li>
<li>Optogenetics now used worldwide; transformed neuroscience research</li>
</ul>

Memorial Sloan Kettering researchers identified that excessive MEK signaling drives T cell exhaustion in cancer immunotherapy. Blocking MEK with FDA-approved inhibitors helped T cells conserve energy and remain active longer in tumor microenvironments, suggesting potential for human trials to enhance immunotherapy effectiveness and prevent treatment fade.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>FDA-approved MEK inhibitors could potentially be tested in human trials</li>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Florida Keys coral disease mortality increases fourfold during heat waves
University of Georgia research shows disease-afflicted corals in the Florida Keys face approximately four times higher mortality risk during heat stress. The 2023 heatwave brought the highest sea temperatures in 150+ years, and staghorn and elkhorn corals have been reduced to functional extinction with 98–100% mortality rates.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Four times higher mortality when disease-afflicted corals exposed to heat stress</li>
<li>2023 heatwave: highest recorded sea temperatures in Florida coral reefs in 150+ years</li>
<li>Heat stress lasted 2–3 months, up to 4x higher than any previously recorded year</li>
<li>Staghorn and elkhorn corals functionally extinct, too few to reproduce</li>
<li>Mortality rates: 98–100% in Florida Keys and Dry Tortugas; ~38% further north</li>
<li>Survey: 52,000+ coral colonies across 391 sites; published in Science</li>
</ul>

## Genetics

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Discovery of Mobile BC200 Gene in Human Brain Cells
Cornell University scientists discovered BC200, a human brain gene evolved from ancient mobile DNA (transposon) that retained the ability to move through the genome—a combination never before observed in a human gene. BC200 is expressed mainly in neurons and appears to regulate protein translation, with potential implications for inheritance through germ cells.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>BC200 evolved from transposon but retained mobility and functional cellular role (rare)</li>
<li>Expressed mainly in neurons; appears to regulate mRNA-to-protein translation</li>
<li>Also appears at low levels in germ cells (sperm/eggs), suggesting inheritance potential</li>
<li>Found embedded in molluscum contagiosum virus (MCV), indicating cross-species jumping</li>
<li>~50% of human genome is transposon-derived DNA; most elements no longer active</li>
<li>Published in Science, September 24, 2026</li>
</ul>

## Space

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA Selects GIMLI Project to Explore Lunar Cave Beneath Marius Hill Pit
NASA selected the GIMLI (Geophysical Instruments for Marius Lunar pit Investigation) project to investigate whether the Marius Hill Pit on the Moon opens into a vast underground cave or lava tube. Led by Planetary Science Institute's Than Putzig, GIMLI will use ground-penetrating radar, seismic sensors, gravity measurements, and cameras to probe subsurface voids and estimate cave size, funded through NASA's PRISM program with equipment delivered via CLPS commercial landers and rovers.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Project lead: Than Putzig (Planetary Science Institute)</li>
<li>Instruments: ground-penetrating radar, seismic sensors, gravity measurements, cameras</li>
<li>Partners: Honeybee Robotics (equipment), Norwegian Space Agency</li>
<li>First use of active-source seismic methods on lunar surface since Apollo</li>
<li>Target: determine if caves/lava tubes exist beneath Marius Hills volcanic region</li>
<li>Caves could provide radiation shielding and temperature protection for future astronauts</li>
<li>Funded through PRISM program with delivery via CLPS initiative</li>
</ul>
