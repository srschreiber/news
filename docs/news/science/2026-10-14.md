### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Scientists solve Moon's asymmetry mystery using farside samples
Analysis of Chang'e-6 farside lunar samples suggests uneven Earth-driven heating caused ancient magma flows creating the Moon's two dramatically different hemispheres. Findings show depleted mantle material on the farside similar to Apollo low-Ti basalts, indicating mantle composition is not substantially different despite surface and crustal contrasts.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Earth&#x27;s gravitational pull drove heat-driven circulation in ancient lunar magma ocean</li>
<li>Uneven heating created contrast: near side (dark volcanic plains, KREEP-rich) vs far side (cratered highlands, thick crust)</li>
<li>Chang&#x27;e-6 basalts show chemical signatures matching Apollo low-Ti basalts</li>
<li>Mantle composition does not differ substantially between hemispheres despite surface differences</li>
<li>Research resolves long-standing scientific debate about lunar asymmetry origins</li>
</ul>
