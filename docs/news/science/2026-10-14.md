### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Ancient climate cycles drove flowering plant diversity explosion
Research in Nature shows Earth's two warmest periods in the past 140 million years triggered both mass extinctions and explosions in flowering plant diversity. Angiosperms created ecological niches enabling tens more species per hectare, with over a million modern insect species depending on them as pollinators or food sources.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Two warmest periods in 140 million years triggered both extinctions and flowering plant diversity explosions</li>
<li>Study used 10 years of computational processing; found positive correlation between river sediment and marine diversification</li>
<li>Flowering plants created ecological niches enabling tens more species per hectare than without angiosperms</li>
<li>Over 1 million modern insect species depend on angiosperms as pollinators, leaf-eaters, or nectar feeders</li>
