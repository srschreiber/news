### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Claude AI discovers novel enzyme system with CRISPR-like properties
Anthropic's Claude AI has autonomously discovered a previously uncharacterized enzyme system associated with DNA repeats that resembles CRISPR in structure and potential function. The discovery, made by AI agents searching genomic databases, identifies a reverse transcriptase system with accessory proteins and non-coding DNA sequences that may perform programmable DNA operations.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Claude discovered a reverse transcriptase-based enzyme system with associated RNA-repeat arrays and accessory proteins of unknown function</li>
<li>The system was identified after 21 hours of searching by approximately 950 Claude agents using 210 million tokens across a massive DNA sequence database</li>
<li>Feng Zhang, MIT CRISPR pioneer, confirmed the finding is &#x27;genuinely intriguing&#x27; and merits further investigation</li>
<li>The discovery demonstrates AI agents can autonomously identify novel biological systems with minimal human direction beyond initial prompting</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Alzheimer's risk gene APOE4 effects partially reversible in experiments
Mount Sinai researchers found that the Alzheimer's risk gene APOE4 damages brain blood vessels by transforming stabilizing cells into scar-producing cells, worsening protein buildup. Critically, blocking a specific signaling pathway reversed this damage in experiments, identifying a potential therapeutic target for neurodegenerative diseases.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>APOE4 transforms pericytes into myofibroblast-like cells causing vascular fibrosis</li>
<li>TGF-β signaling blockade partially reversed vascular damage in cell culture and aged mice</li>
<li>Findings published in Cell (September 24, 2026) and Cell Stem Cell</li>
<li>Vascular degeneration is an active, targetable process rather than disease consequence</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> MIND diet linked to slower brain aging equivalent to 2.5 years
A 20-year study of 1,647 adults found that strict adherence to the MIND diet slowed brain shrinkage and grey matter loss, with effects equivalent to delaying brain aging by approximately 2.5 years. Berries and poultry correlated with healthier brain changes, while sweets and fried foods linked to greater tissue loss.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>MIND diet adherence linked to 2.5 years of delayed brain aging</li>
<li>Berries and poultry associated with healthier brain changes</li>
<li>Sweets and fried fast foods associated with greater tissue loss</li>
<li>Study tracked 1,647 middle-aged and older adults from 1999 to 2019</li>
</ul>
