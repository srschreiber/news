### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Earliest known quasar pair challenges black hole growth theory
Astronomers discovered the earliest known compact quasar pair approximately 900 million years after the Big Bang, raising questions about rapid early supermassive black hole growth. The double system was observed with the Hubble Space Telescope during the era known as cosmic noon.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Discovery challenges models of early supermassive black hole formation and rapid growth</li>
<li>Observed during &#x27;cosmic noon&#x27; (peak of star and black hole formation)</li>
<li>Researchers used cosmological simulations spanning 10 million to 4 billion years post-Big Bang</li>
</ul>

Astronomers developed an imaging technique using a digital twin of James Webb's Near Infrared Camera to remove stellar glare, making exoplanets 2–15 times easier to detect. The method has already uncovered a previously hidden exoplanet in existing JWST observations.

<ul class="takeaways">

<li>Published in Nature Nov 4, 2026; already found hidden exoplanet in existing JWST data</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Global carbon emissions may have peaked in 2026

Confirmation requires years, and the drop is primarily attributed to the Iran war and energy crisis rather than climate action.

<li>Oil prices: ~$60/barrel (early 2026) → $100/barrel (mid-2026)</li>

## Archaeology

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Ancient Homo naledi Burial Site Contains All-Female Remains, Uncovered Via Protein Analysis
Protein analysis of teeth from at least 20 Homo naledi individuals from a South African cave burial site reveals no male-specific markers, suggesting an all-female burial dated to over 240,000 years ago. If confirmed as intentional, this would represent the earliest known single-sex burial by a human relative other than Homo sapiens.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Homo naledi burial site: Dinaledi Chamber, Rising Star Cave, South Africa</li>
<li>Age: 240,000+ years old</li>
<li>Analyzed 23 teeth from 20+ individuals; no Amelogenin-Y protein (male marker) detected</li>
<li>Protein recovered via acid etching of tooth enamel; ancient origin verified via amino acid analysis</li>
<li>Homo naledi lived 335,000–241,000 years ago</li>
</ul>
