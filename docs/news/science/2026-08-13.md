### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Rapid warming may trigger Atlantic circulation collapse at 2°C warming
Research published in Communications Earth & Environment found that warming pace, not just magnitude, determines Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation collapse, with rapid warming triggering it at 2°C. AMOC failure would increase atmospheric CO₂ by 47–83 ppm and cause pronounced regional temperature shifts.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Rapid warming triggers AMOC collapse at 2°C</li>
<li>AMOC failure increases CO₂ by 47–83 ppm</li>
<li>Arctic cools ~7°C; Antarctic warms ~6°C from collapse</li>
<li>Study led by Da Nian, Johan Rockström et al.</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> India faces rising uncompensable heat stress particularly during monsoon season
India is experiencing expanding uncompensable heat stress—a condition where the body cannot cool through sweating—spreading from summer into monsoon season due to climate change. Currently affecting 8% of land in summer and 1% during monsoon, projections show 60% and 53% respectively under 2°C warming, with over 25,000 heat-related deaths recorded between 1992 and 2020.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Currently 8% of India experiences summer UHS, 1% during monsoon</li>
<li>At 2°C warming: 60% summer UHS, 53% monsoon UHS projected</li>
<li>At 4°C warming: monsoon UHS may exceed summer UHS in some regions</li>
<li>Over 25,000 heat-related deaths recorded 1992–2020</li>
<li>Monsoon UHS driven by moderate temperatures combined with high humidity</li>
</ul>
