A multi-university study of 3,500 wildfires across western North America found that rapid wildfires burn so severely they prevent forest recovery to historic composition. Fire speed predicts how completely forests are destroyed and how difficult regeneration becomes, potentially shifting landscapes from conifer forests to shrublands or grasslands.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Analyzed 3,500 wildfires, 33,000+ daily spread events (2012–2023)</li>
<li>Rapid fires kill nearly all trees; slow fires leave surviving seed sources</li>
<li>Severe fire removes seed sources; conifer regeneration fails</li>
<li>Landscapes may transition to shrublands/grasslands better suited to warmer climate</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Rocky planet building blocks may have formed 100 million years after Big Bang
University of Portsmouth astrophysicist Dr. Daniel Whalen's simulations suggest rocky planet building blocks formed just 100 million years after the Big Bang, before the first galaxies existed. Supernova explosions from Population III stars scattered heavy elements into gas clouds where planetary material accumulated with substantial water content.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Rocky planet building blocks formed 100M years after Big Bang</li>
<li>Pair-instability supernovae scattered 100+ solar masses of heavy elements</li>
<li>Disk around 0.7 solar-mass star accumulated Earth-masses of planetesimals</li>
<li>Disk contained water comparable to early Solar System amounts</li>
</ul>
