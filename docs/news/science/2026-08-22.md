### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> JWST finds early galaxies contain far more mass than previously estimated
The James Webb Space Telescope revealed that massive early galaxies contain three to four times more mass than previously thought, driven by a hidden population of small, faint stars. This discovery challenges long-standing assumptions about star formation and suggests low-mass stars were far more common in the early universe than models predicted.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Early galaxies 3–4× more massive than previously estimated</li>
<li>Hidden population of small, faint stars discovered</li>
<li>Early universe had much greater share of low-mass stars than smaller galaxies like Milky Way</li>
<li>Finding published in Nature Astronomy, August 22, 2026</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Honeybee queens transfer pesticide contamination to eggs as survival mechanism
Honeybee queens exposed to pesticides transfer contamination into their eggs as a survival mechanism, protecting themselves while endangering offspring and potentially destabilizing colonies. Research from UC Davis reveals worker bee filtration has limits; severe contamination can create a tipping point for colony collapse.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Queens transfer pesticide burden to eggs to reduce own chemical load</li>
<li>Worker bee filtration capacity can be overwhelmed by accumulated pesticides</li>
<li>Severely contaminated eggs may fail to develop properly</li>
<li>Study used nanocolonies with one queen and 60 worker bees each</li>
</ul>
