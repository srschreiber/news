### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Physicists discover hidden gluon structure inside protons
Physicists analyzing RHIC collision data discovered evidence that baryon number in protons is carried by a Y-shaped gluon junction rather than solely by three quarks, challenging decades-old textbook assumptions. The finding may explain why baryon number is conserved universally and suggests gluons play a more fundamental role in nuclear physics than previously recognized.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Gluon junction proposed theoretically in 1970s; baryon-carrying role predicted by Dmitri Kharzeev in 1996</li>
<li>RHIC ceased operations early 2026</li>
<li>Gluon junction stopped more readily than valence quarks in high-energy collisions</li>
<li>Discovery may explain universal baryon number conservation since the Big Bang</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Black hole shreds massive star in energetic tidal disruption event
Astronomers observed a black hole violently destroying a massive star in a tidal disruption event (AT2024wpp, nicknamed "the Whippet"), releasing approximately 400 billion times the Sun's energy output. The event, classified as a Luminous Fast Blue Optical Transient, was detected by multiple observatories and unexpectedly showed fast-moving helium months later, suggesting some stellar structure survived.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Event released ~400 billion times Sun&#x27;s energy output</li>
<li>Detected by Zwicky Transient Facility, Liverpool Telescope, NASA Swift satellite</li>
<li>Classified as Luminous Fast Blue Optical Transient (LFBOT)</li>
<li>Fast-moving helium detected months after initial event, suggesting partial survival</li>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Gene-edited beagles produce no detectable major dog allergen in early tests
Researchers have successfully created two gene-edited beagles that produce no detectable levels of Can f 1, the primary dog allergen affecting roughly 15% of the global population. Using CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing and cloning techniques, the company produced puppies with a frameshift mutation in the allergen gene; early tests show normal development and no allergic response in a person with dog allergies.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Two female beagles (Alfie and Bailey) born September 2024 produce no detectable Can f 1 allergen</li>
<li>CRISPR-Cas9 caused frameshift mutation in Can f 1 gene via one-base-pair insertion</li>
<li>Whole-genome sequencing found no off-target edits at 413 predicted Cas9 sites</li>
<li>Saliva and dander samples showed no allergen; skin-prick test negative in allergic volunteer</li>
<li>FDA approval and long-term health monitoring still required before commercialization</li>
</ul>
