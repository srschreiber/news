### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Quantum computer operates in space for the first time
Scientists at the University of Vienna achieved a global first by operating a quantum computer in space aboard a Falcon 9 rocket in low Earth orbit. Despite laser-adhesive degradation reducing output power from 20 to 4 milliwatts, the successful operation demonstrates feasibility of on-satellite quantum data processing to bypass downlink bandwidth limitations.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Launched June 2025, operated in low Earth orbit for first time</li>
<li>Laser adhesive degraded in vacuum: output fell from 20 to 4 milliwatts</li>
<li>Key challenge: downlink bandwidth limits force Earth transmission of all raw satellite data</li>
<li>Quantum processing on satellites could offer alternative to ground processing</li>
</ul>

## Climate

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Drought halts photosynthesis, causing forests to emit more CO₂ than they absorb
During droughts, trees halt photosynthesis to conserve water, causing forests globally to shift from carbon sinks to net emitters. Research across 137 U.S. forest sites shows that trees continue photosynthesizing months after growth ceases, meaning less carbon is permanently stored than climate models predict, a concern as droughts intensify.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Trees close stomata during drought, stopping CO₂ capture: ~400 water molecules released per CO₂ molecule absorbed</li>
<li>Forests globally capture ~25% of human CO₂ emissions but lose this role under extreme drought</li>
<li>36% of annual carbon uptake in eastern U.S. forests occurs after growth ends in late summer</li>
<li>Photosynthesis decoupling from wood growth reduces permanent carbon storage</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Immune activation outside the brain drives Alzheimer's neurodegeneration pathway
Washington University scientists discovered that T cells driving Alzheimer's neurodegeneration are activated in lymph nodes before entering the brain. Blocking the signals preparing these T cells in lymph nodes dramatically reduced neurodegeneration and preserved cognitive abilities in mice, potentially offering a more accessible treatment target than blood-brain barrier therapies.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>T cells contributing to Alzheimer&#x27;s activated in lymph nodes outside brain</li>
<li>Blocking lymph-node signals reduced neurodegeneration and preserved cognition in mice</li>
<li>Published September 3, 2026 in Nature Neuroscience</li>
<li>Offers more accessible treatment target than therapies requiring blood-brain barrier passage</li>
</ul>
