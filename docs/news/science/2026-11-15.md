### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Zero Emissions Commitment: How prior warming affects future climate response
A Met Office Hadley Centre study published in Nature Climate Change found that zero emissions commitment—continued warming after CO₂ emissions stop—depends on prior warming levels. ZEC remains near zero up to 2°C but becomes positive at higher warming, reaching 0.4–0.6°C after 150 years at 3–5°C of prior warming.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study uses UK Earth System Model (UKESM1.2)</li>
<li>Zero emissions commitment near zero up to 2°C prior warming</li>
<li>ZEC becomes positive above 2°C prior warming</li>
<li>At 3–5°C prior warming: 0.4–0.6°C additional warming over ~150 years</li>
<li>Mechanism: sustained energy imbalance and weakening carbon sinks</li>
<li>Implies warming reversal becomes harder at higher peak temperatures</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Physicists Use Laser Light to Unlock Crystal's Hidden Electrical Properties
Auburn University physicists discovered how to use laser light pulses to organize polar nanoregions in a strontium titanate crystal, with the alignment lasting approximately one trillionth of a second. The breakthrough resolved a decades-long question about whether these electrical regions form through atomic movement or pre-exist and align during cooling.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Laser bursts organize polar nanoregions in modified strontium titanate crystal</li>
<li>Alignment lasts femtosecond timescale (one trillionth of a second)</li>
<li>Study resolved whether regions form through atomic movement or pre-exist</li>
<li>Discovery could enable new approaches to ultrafast computing and information storage</li>
</ul>
