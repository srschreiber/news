## Climate

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Satellites measure 12.5 trillion tons of ice lost from Greenland and Antarctica since 1979
Satellite data spanning 1979–2026 shows Greenland and Antarctica lost 12.5 trillion tons of ice, raising sea levels by 3.1 cm, with annual loss accelerating from 40 billion tons (1979–1990) to sixfold higher rates in Antarctica recently. Ocean warming drives approximately five-sixths of the loss via basal melting rather than atmospheric warming alone.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>12.5 trillion tons of ice lost from Greenland and Antarctica (1979–2026)</li>
<li>Annual ice loss increased from ~40 billion tons (1979–1990) to much higher rates</li>
<li>Antarctic ice loss increased sixfold in recent decades</li>
<li>Cumulative loss raised global sea levels by 3.1 cm</li>
<li>~5/6 of ice loss driven by warm oceanic currents, not atmospheric warming</li>
<li>Jakobshavn glacier retreated at rates up to 50 meters per day</li>
<li>Sharp acceleration evident from 2010s onward; early 1990s relatively stable</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Ancient DNA reveals plague's 400-year recurrence in Europe after the Black Death
Genetic analysis of 26 Yersinia pestis genomes from 11 archaeological sites across Estonia, Russia, England, the Netherlands, and Switzerland revealed plague repeatedly returned to Europe over 400 years following the Black Death. A major genetic expansion and branching into three lineages occurred around 1450–1500, likely influenced by the Great Renaissance Drought.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>26 ancient plague genomes reconstructed from 14th–18th centuries</li>
<li>Samples from 11 sites across Estonia, Russia, England, Netherlands, and Switzerland</li>
<li>Plague repeatedly resurfaced over 400+ years after Black Death (1347–1353)</li>
<li>Major genetic expansion into three lineages around 1450–1500</li>
<li>Estonia experienced repeated introductions beginning late 14th century</li>
<li>Plague spread via trade, migration, and war</li>
</ul>

## Weather Prediction

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Hurricane Hunters identify four warning signs of tropical cyclone intensification
Scientists at the University of Miami and NOAA identified four warning signs indicating when a tilted tropical cyclone is becoming primed to strengthen, based on nearly three decades of Hurricane Hunter observations. The findings could enable forecasters to give coastal communities additional lead time for evacuation decisions.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Nearly three decades of NOAA Hurricane Hunter data analyzed</li>
<li>Four characteristics: compact tightly organized circulation, favorable tilt relative to wind shear, stronger rising air and rainfall near lower center, warm water with abundant moisture and weak middle-level winds</li>
<li>Tropical cyclones must become vertically organized before substantial intensification</li>
<li>Strong upper-level winds prevent strengthening until circulation centers realign</li>
</ul>
