### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Antarctica ice loss will drive sea-level rise for low-lying coastal areas regardless of snowfall
Researchers confirmed that Antarctic ice sheet collapse is locked in by current climate dynamics and cannot be offset by increased snowfall. Containing 58 meters of sea-level-rise equivalent, the ice sheet exhibits "robust hysteresis"—certain regions face irreversible mass loss regardless of temperature changes. Self-reinforcing mechanisms like surface melt-elevation feedback will drive continued ice loss, threatening hundreds of millions in coastal regions.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Antarctic ice sheet contains 58 meters of sea-level-rise equivalent</li>
<li>Robust hysteresis locks in irreversible mass loss in ice sheet regions</li>
<li>Self-reinforcing surface melt-elevation feedback magnifies warming</li>
<li>Sea level rise projected at 1 foot by 2050 regardless of carbon emission cuts</li>
<li>Damaging coastal flooding predicted 10x more frequently in 2050 vs. today</li>
</ul>
