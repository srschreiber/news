### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> CAR T-cell cancer therapy achieves remission in severe rheumatoid arthritis
CAR T-cell therapy, borrowed from cancer treatment, dramatically reduced disease activity in six patients with severe rheumatoid arthritis resistant to other drugs, with three achieving medication-free remission. The therapy genetically modifies immune cells to eliminate disease-driving B cells even in hard-to-reach locations like bone marrow and joint tissue.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Six patients with treatment-refractory rheumatoid arthritis</li>
<li>Three achieved medication-free remission by end of observation period</li>
<li>Therapy targets and eliminates B cells that produce harmful antibodies</li>
<li>CAR T cells cleared inflammatory foci in deep tissue locations</li>
<li>Study published Nature Medicine, August 2026</li>
<li>Conducted by Charité - Universitätsmedizin Berlin researchers</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Scientists discover massive heat anomaly in Mars's interior, partially molten southern region
Scientists discovered a massive heat anomaly within Mars using gravity data from three spacecraft, revealing the southern interior is 200–400°C warmer than the north and potentially partially molten. The finding explains unusual magnetic signatures and seismic anomalies, offering insights into Mars's ancient water history and habitability.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Southern interior 200–400°C warmer than northern hemisphere</li>
<li>Southern region potentially partially molten</li>
<li>Data from Mars Global Surveyor, Mars Odyssey, and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter</li>
<li>Study by Alexander Berne et al., published Nature, August 27, 2026</li>
<li>Explains unusual magnetic signatures and InSight seismic wave energy loss</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> 1.4-million-year-old footprints reveal large Paranthropus boisei individuals
Fossil footprints from Kenya dated to 1.4 million years ago reveal eight Paranthropus boisei traveling together, with body sizes up to 1.8 meters tall and 75 kilograms—substantially larger than previously estimated. The grouping, likely mostly adult males, suggests complex social structures and a rare snapshot of extinct hominin behavior.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Eight Paranthropus boisei individuals, dated 1.4 million years ago</li>
<li>Body sizes up to 1.8 meters tall, ~75 kilograms—larger than skeletal estimates suggested</li>
<li>Group likely contained mostly adult males, no obvious females or children</li>
<li>Footprints preserve rare &#x27;snapshot of fossil behavior&#x27;</li>
<li>Led by Kevin Hatala (Chatham University, Max Planck Institute)</li>
</ul>
