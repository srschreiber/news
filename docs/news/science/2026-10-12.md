### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Robert Hall and Michael Woodford win Nobel Prize in Economics for stabilization models
Robert Hall (Stanford) and Michael Woodford (Columbia) were awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Economics for their work on microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilization through monetary and fiscal policy. Their New Keynesian models remain the basis for modern stabilization policy implementation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Hall: PhD MIT 1967; research on expectations and household consumption (late 1970s)</li>
<li>Woodford: PhD MIT 1983; &#x27;Interest and Prices&#x27; (2003) comprehensive macroeconomic model</li>
<li>Work rebuilt macroeconomic models on microeconomic foundations rather than statistical links</li>
<li>New Keynesian models based on their research now underpin economic stabilization policy</li>
</ul>

</ul>

## Quantum Computing

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Hybrid quantum computers advance light-matter interaction modeling
University of Turku researchers extended polariton theory using symmetry principles to model light-matter interactions with arbitrary numbers of emitters and excitations, reducing computational complexity from O(N^X) to O(X). The advance enables investigation of systems exceeding one million components, previously impossible.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Computational load reduced from O(N^X) to O(X) using symmetry principles derived from Tavis-Cummings model</li>
<li>Now enables calculation on systems exceeding 1 million components (previously impossible)</li>
<li>Overcame previous limitation restricted to few excitations due to exponential computational complexity</li>
<li>Applications for polariton-based devices in optoelectronics and energy storage</li>
</ul>
