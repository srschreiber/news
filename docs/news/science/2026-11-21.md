### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Mercury-Bound X-Ray Telescope Opens Eyes After Eight Years in Space
The Mercury Imaging X-ray Spectrometer (MIXS), a UK-built telescope aboard ESA and JAXA's BepiColombo mission, captured its first cosmic observations in September 2026 after eight years and 10 billion kilometers of space travel. MIXS detected X-rays from the Crab Nebula, achieving angular resolution exceeding pre-flight expectations. BepiColombo entered Mercury orbit on November 21.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>MIXS aboard BepiColombo: eight years, 10 billion km space travel</li>
<li>September 2026: detected Crab Nebula X-rays, 55 spacecraft orientation changes</li>
<li>Angular resolution exceeded 2015 baseline after lengthy journey</li>
<li>BepiColombo entered Mercury orbit November 21, 2026</li>
<li>MIXS confirms instrument functionality for Mercury surface composition study</li>
</ul>
