### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> New geodesy method reveals significant ocean warming gaps between measurements and climate models
Researchers at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University developed a satellite laser ranging method measuring ocean thermal expansion over 30 years (1993–2022), published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. Global mean sea level rose approximately 90 millimeters at an average rate of 3.3 mm per year, with ocean mass from melting land ice identified as the dominant driver.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Global mean sea level rise: ~90 millimeters over 30 years (1993–2022)</li>
<li>Average rate: 3.3 mm per year with acceleration in recent decades</li>
<li>Ocean mass from melting land ice (Greenland, mountain glaciers) is dominant driver, not thermal expansion alone</li>
<li>First direct 30-year record of barystatic sea level using satellite laser ranging</li>
<li>Method improves spatial resolution by incorporating ocean-land boundary information</li>
</ul>

## Physics

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Scientists discover first type I superconductor breaking time-reversal symmetry
An international team led by IISER Bhopal discovered ytterbium diantimonide (YbSb₂), the first type I superconductor breaking time-reversal symmetry—a property previously seen only in type II systems. Muon spin spectroscopy detected spontaneous internal magnetic fields below the superconducting transition, indicating unconventional spin-triplet pairing. The material could host Majorana surface modes useful for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First type I superconductor with time-reversal symmetry breaking</li>
<li>YbSb₂ exhibits zero resistance at ~−272°C (above absolute zero)</li>
<li>Unconventional spin-triplet pairing with interband Cooper pairs</li>
<li>Fully gapped with higher energy barrier than conventional superconductors</li>
<li>Published Physical Review Letters, September 23, 2026</li>
</ul>
