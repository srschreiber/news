## Health

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> New experimental drug targets mesothelioma by disabling tumor antioxidant defense
Scientists developed an experimental drug that kills mesothelioma cancer cells by disabling PRX3, an antioxidant enzyme tumors depend on for survival. A phase one trial found the drug controlled disease progression in 67% of patients with relapsed mesothelioma and produced longer survival than standard treatments in critically ill patients.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Drug controlled disease progression in 67% of relapsed mesothelioma patients</li>
<li>Some tumors shrank; generally well tolerated</li>
<li>Works by blocking PRX3 (peroxiredoxin 3) in mitochondria</li>
<li>Mesothelioma: rare asbestos-linked cancer; ~30,000 diagnosed annually worldwide</li>
<li>Median survival without treatment ~12 months; five-year survival ~10%</li>
<li>Published in Nature Communications; sponsored phase one trial by RS Oncology, LLC</li>
</ul>

## Space Exploration

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA New Horizons Detects Evidence of Liquid Nitrogen Flow on Pluto
NASA's New Horizons spacecraft detected evidence that liquid nitrogen may be flowing onto Pluto's surface through cracks in ice, marking the first evidence of current liquid on the dwarf planet. Dark features resembling patterns from water flow on Earth glaciers appear on the northern edge of Sputnik Planitia, suggesting Pluto is geologically more active than its frozen appearance indicates.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>First evidence of liquid currently on Pluto&#x27;s surface</li>
<li>Liquid nitrogen may flow through cracks in Sputnik Planitia glacier</li>
<li>Sputnik Planitia larger than Texas and Oklahoma combined; mostly frozen nitrogen</li>
<li>Dark features resemble patterns observed on Greenland ice sheet</li>
<li>Surface of Sputnik Planitia estimated less than one million years old</li>
<li>Research published in peer-reviewed Planetary Science Journal; led by Dr. Alan Stern, New Horizons principal investigator</li>
</ul>
