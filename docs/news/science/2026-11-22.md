### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> JWST Discovers Possible Billion-Solar-Mass Black Hole Outpacing Galaxy Growth in Early Universe
The James Webb Space Telescope discovered three actively feeding supermassive black holes in a single galaxy seen 1.2 billion years after the Big Bang. Two central black holes sit approximately 620 light-years apart and are expected to merge within the next few hundred million years; the smallest black hole accretes at a rate exceeding the Eddington limit, allowing faster growth than standard models predict.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Three supermassive black holes found in one galaxy at redshift 5.02 (~12.5 billion light-years away)</li>
<li>Black hole masses: ~80 million, 2 million, 630,000 solar masses</li>
<li>Galaxy&#x27;s stars total ~1.3 billion solar masses</li>
<li>Two central black holes ~620 light-years apart; expected to merge in few hundred million years</li>
<li>Smallest black hole accretes above Eddington limit, enabling faster growth</li>
<li>82% chance of detection by LISA gravitational wave observatory at merger</li>
</ul>

## Space Science

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Record-Resolution Milky Way Simulation Using Three Supercomputers
Researchers led by Keiya Hirashima created the first Milky Way simulation tracking more than 100 billion individual stars across 10,000 years of evolution by pairing artificial intelligence with advanced numerical simulation techniques. The model includes 100 times more stars than previous simulations and was generated 100 times faster by using a deep learning surrogate model to predict gas spread after supernovae.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Tracked 100+ billion individual stars; 10,000-year evolution span</li>
<li>100 times more stars than previous sophisticated simulations</li>
<li>Generated 100 times faster than conventional physics-based methods</li>
<li>Deep learning model predicts post-supernova gas spread, eliminating major computational bottleneck</li>
<li>Strategy potentially applicable to Earth climate and weather research</li>
</ul>
