### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Ancient Moon Rocks Rewrite Solar System Bombardment History
Scientists from Curtin University analyzed ancient rocks from the Moon's far side and found evidence challenging the decades-old theory of a single catastrophic bombardment period. Instead, the data shows asteroid impacts declined gradually over billions of years. The analysis identified impact events spanning approximately 4.33 billion to 1.13 billion years ago—a record covering more than three billion years. The Moon's far side rocks preserve a cleaner record of early impacts than the near side because they were less affected by later geological events. Dr. Fred Jourdan, a research co-author from Curtin University's School of Earth and Planetary Sciences and John de Laeter Centre, stated that lunar rocks act as a time capsule preserving Solar System history erased from Earth by erosion, plate tectonics, and other processes. The findings have implications for understanding Earth's earliest history and the conditions that influenced the origins of life.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> New quasar discovery breaks distance record, constrains black hole formation models
Astronomers led by Julien Wolf at the Max Planck Institute for Astronomy discovered the most distant quasar to date using ESA's Euclid space telescope. The quasar, designated J1504+6252 and nicknamed 'Dún Aonghasa' by the team, has a redshift of z = 8.13, placing it 630 million years after the Big Bang—40 million years earlier than the previous record holder and the first quasar with redshift greater than eight. Initial observations came from the Euclid Wide Survey, with confirmation provided by the W.M. Keck Observatory, Hubble Space Telescope, Large Binocular Telescope, and James Webb Space Telescope. The quasar contains a supermassive black hole that already existed at this early cosmic epoch. The discovery supports the scenario in which supermassive black holes form through direct collapse of large gas clouds and subsequently accumulate additional matter, constraining early black hole formation models.

## Climate Science

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Global warming halted Earth's already-begun ice age, ancient Arctic plants reveal
Scientists at Columbia Climate School analyzed ancient plants preserved beneath the Barnes Ice Cap in the Canadian Arctic and found that Earth's next ice age began approximately 2,000 years ago but was halted by global warming. The ice sheet started expanding again around that time, marking the possible onset of a new glacial period. However, by the 1960s, rising summer temperatures reversed this trend, causing the ice to retreat instead of continue growing. The research, published in Science, was conducted by geochemists Nicolás Young and Joerg Schaefer and colleagues. The Barnes Ice Cap is a surviving remnant of the Laurentide Ice Sheet, located on central Baffin Island in Nunavut, Canada, covering an area roughly the size of Delaware. The findings indicate that global warming interrupted a natural climate cycle that has shaped Earth for millions of years, with Young stating the warming 'jolted the system' by stopping an ice age that was already underway.
