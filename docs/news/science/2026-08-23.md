A DNA repair mechanism allows these deep-sea sharks to preserve vision for centuries despite parasites and extreme darkness in their Arctic habitat.

## Astrophysics

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Stars survive repeated near-miss encounters with supermassive black holes
Astronomers identified stars that survive repeated close encounters with supermassive black holes, each time producing bursts of light that fade progressively. These repeating partial tidal disruption events occur when a star's rapid rotation allows it to lose mass without being destroyed, returning months or years later for additional passes.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Stars survive multiple near-miss encounters with black holes</li>
<li>Repeating partial tidal disruption events (rpTDEs) occur months or years apart</li>
<li>Fading light bursts correlate with each successive encounter</li>
<li>Star&#x27;s rapid pre-existing rotation enables survival and orbit entry</li>
</ul>

## Energy

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Computer models identify catalysts for low-carbon ammonia production
MIT researchers developed computational modeling to identify catalyst materials for electrochemical ammonia production, potentially replacing the century-old fossil fuel-dependent Haber-Bosch process. Ammonia production currently consumes up to 2% of global energy and generates 1.5% of greenhouse gas emissions.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Ammonia production: 2% of global energy consumption, 1.5% of emissions</li>
<li>Haber-Bosch process relies on fossil fuels for over 100 years</li>
<li>Computational approach accelerates catalyst discovery vs. trial-and-error testing</li>
<li>Electrochemical methods exist but lack economic scale competitiveness</li>
</ul>
