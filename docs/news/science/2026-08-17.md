### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Researchers identify nerve network that fuels triple-negative breast cancer
Researchers at the University of Oklahoma discovered that triple-negative breast cancer tumors recruit macrophages that release BDNF, a protein drawing nerves into tumors and supporting cancer progression. In mouse models, blocking BDNF signaling prevented nerve infiltration and significantly reduced tumor growth, with patient data showing higher macrophage and BDNF levels associated with poorer survival.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Macrophages release BDNF protein that draws nerves into tumors</li>
<li>Blocking BDNF signaling reduced tumor growth in mice</li>
<li>Higher macrophage and BDNF levels linked to poorer survival in patients</li>
<li>Published in Cell Death &amp; Differentiation</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> James Webb's 'little red dots' may be compact galactic centers, not primordial mysteries
Astronomers studying galaxy Saguaro found evidence that James Webb's mysterious 'little red dots' may be bright compact galactic centers surrounded by fainter structures rather than a separate population. The findings suggest these objects represent active supermassive black holes and that their apparent abundance partly results from observational bias at extreme distances.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Saguaro galaxy located at redshift 2, appearing as it did 3.3 billion years after Big Bang</li>
<li>Little red dots may represent active galactic nuclei rather than distinct class of objects</li>
<li>Their numbers decline sharply at lower redshifts</li>
<li>Published in The Astrophysical Journal on July 29, 2026</li>
