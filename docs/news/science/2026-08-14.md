## Conservation

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Prehistoric Pacific had 20 times more sharks than modern coral reefs due to fishing
University of California researchers reconstructed prehistoric shark populations using fossilized shark scales in reef sediments from Panama, finding the Pacific coast supported approximately 20 times more sharks than the Caribbean coast 3,000–7,000 years ago. The analysis indicates fishing has reduced shark abundance by approximately 95 percent on modern coral reefs compared to pre-industrial baselines.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Shark populations 20× higher on Pacific coast than Caribbean coast 3,000–7,000 years ago</li>
<li>Collected 3,497 shark denticles from fossilized reef sediment samples</li>
<li>Fishing reduced shark abundance by approximately 95% on coral reefs</li>
<li>Modern conservation targets based on current populations dramatically underestimate historical ecosystem structure</li>
</ul>
