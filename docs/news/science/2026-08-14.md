### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Organic molecules found in 66-million-year-old dinosaur bones overturn fossilization assumptions
Researchers at the University of Liverpool discovered original collagen proteins preserved in a 66-million-year-old dinosaur fossil, overturning the assumption that fossilization destroys organic molecules. The finding, published in Analytical Chemistry, opens new avenues for studying ancient biology through molecular analysis.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Collagen protein detected in 66-million-year-old Edmontosaurus hip bone using mass spectrometry</li>
<li>Discovery challenges long-held belief that fossils contain no original organic molecules</li>
<li>22-kilogram sacrum excavated from Hell Creek Formation, South Dakota</li>
<li>Potential to reveal previously unknown evolutionary connections between dinosaur species</li>

The finding challenges the assumption that the brain's immune system remains separate from the body's defenses throughout life and could reshape understanding of neurological diseases.

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Stress Hormone CRH Aids Brain Repair After Injury
Researchers at the Max Planck Institute of Psychiatry discovered that oligodendrocyte progenitor cells rapidly release the stress hormone CRH near brain damage to aid repair. Approximately one-third of these cells activate CRH production within hours of injury, helping control myelin regeneration and nerve insulation repair.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>About one-third of OPCs release stress hormone CRH near brain wounds within hours</li>
<li>CRH response shuts down after roughly three days following injury</li>
<li>OPCs use CRH to control maturation into myelin-producing oligodendrocytes</li>
<li>Discovery may explain how early-life stress contributes to psychiatric disorders</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> UK satellite to observe cosmic dark ages from lunar far side
CosmoCube, a UK-developed satellite the size of a small suitcase, will orbit the moon's far side to detect radio signals from the cosmic dark ages—the first 150 million years after the Big Bang before stars formed. The mission aims to map early galaxy formation and investigate dark matter's role using the 21-centimeter hydrogen line, with a planned launch within five years.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>CosmoCube approximately suitcase-sized</li>
<li>Will detect 21-centimeter radio signals from neutral hydrogen</li>
<li>Moon&#x27;s far side shields from Earth&#x27;s ionosphere and radio interference</li>
<li>40 minutes unobstructed observation per two-hour orbit</li>
<li>Planned two-year mission targeting ~1,000 hours of observations</li>
<li>Funded by UK Space Agency, led by University of Cambridge</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> Prehistoric Pacific had 20 times more sharks than modern coral reefs due to fishing
University of California researchers reconstructed prehistoric shark populations using fossilized shark scales in reef sediments from Panama, finding the Pacific coast supported approximately 20 times more sharks than the Caribbean coast 3,000–7,000 years ago. The analysis indicates fishing has reduced shark abundance by approximately 95 percent on modern coral reefs compared to pre-industrial baselines.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Shark populations 20× higher on Pacific coast than Caribbean coast 3,000–7,000 years ago</li>
<li>Collected 3,497 shark denticles from fossilized reef sediment samples</li>
<li>Fishing reduced shark abundance by approximately 95% on coral reefs</li>
<li>Modern conservation targets based on current populations dramatically underestimate historical ecosystem structure</li>
</ul>

A study of 21,462 women found that estrogen-only hormone therapy use later in life was associated with 39% lower odds of dementia diagnosis and 35% lower odds of Alzheimer's brain changes at autopsy. The association suggests protective effects but does not establish causation.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>39% lower dementia diagnosis odds; 35% lower Alzheimer&#x27;s pathology odds at autopsy</li>
<li>18% of hormone therapy users showed no Alzheimer&#x27;s signs versus 10% of non-users</li>
<li>Study published August 12, 2026, in Neurology journal; 21,462 participants followed 3–5 years</li>
<li>Results specific to estrogen-only therapy; combined estrogen-progestin therapy may raise dementia risk</li>
</ul>
