### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7/10" aria-label="Importance 7 of 10">7</span> NASA's Voyager 1 reaches one light-day distance from Earth

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Milestone date: Nov 18, 2026, 5:16 a.m. ET</li>
<li>Distance: one light-day (~16 billion miles or ~26 billion km)</li>
<li>Speed: ~36,000 mph (~61,000 km/h); will take ~18,000 years to travel one light-year</li>
<li>Launch: Sept 5, 1977; in flight ~49 years; plutonium-powered, relays data daily</li>
<li>Communication: skeleton team sends commands ~weekly; Oct 2026 brief communication loss, cause undetermined</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 8/10" aria-label="Importance 8 of 10">8</span> Stanford researchers discover brain activity patterns predicting Alzheimer's years early
Stanford researchers discovered that subtle brain activity patterns can predict Alzheimer's disease risk years before memory problems or protein buildup occurs. A scoring system based on differences between Alzheimer's patients and 'super agers' predicted future cognitive decline more effectively than established biomarkers including genetic risk and amyloid-beta levels.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Study published Nov 4, 2026, in Science Advances</li>
<li>Brain activity patterns predicted cognitive decline more effectively than demographics, genetics, amyloid-beta, tau biomarkers</li>
<li>Cohort: adults 55–70 assessed; follow-up periods extended to 12 years</li>
<li>Participants with Alzheimer&#x27;s-like patterns more likely to experience decline, accumulate amyloid-beta, receive Alzheimer&#x27;s diagnosis</li>
<li>Super ager patterns associated with maintained cognitive abilities and avoided outcomes</li>
<li>Unlike conventional biomarkers, brain patterns may be modifiable via cognitive training and transcranial magnetic stimulation</li>
</ul>

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> Astronomers Discover Potential Sixth Dwarf Planet 2026 ED138 Beyond Pluto
Astronomers discovered 2026 ED138, a potentially massive dwarf planet beyond Pluto estimated at roughly 1,000 kilometers across—the largest trans-Neptunian object found in approximately 20 years. If confirmed by the International Astronomical Union, it would become the sixth recognized dwarf planet.

<ul class="takeaways">
<li>Discovery announced Nov 14, 2026; found Oct 13, 2026 by Matthew Holman (Harvard-Smithsonian)</li>
<li>Size: ~1,000 km across (largest trans-Neptunian object found in ~20 years)</li>
<li>Location: ~46 AU from Sun; beyond Pluto</li>
<li>If confirmed: sixth dwarf planet (joining Pluto, Ceres, Eris, Haumea, Makemake)</li>
<li>Detected via Simonyi Survey Telescope at NSF-DOE Vera C. Rubin Observatory; verified via 20 additional detections</li>
</ul>
