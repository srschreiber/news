## AI Achievement

### <span class="imp imp-high" title="Importance 7.5/10" aria-label="Importance 7.5 of 10">7.5</span> AI and Human Collaboration Solves Famous Mathematical Problem
Levent Alpoge, a mathematician at Anthropic, announced on November 21, 2026, that he used the company's Claude AI tool to solve the Riemann hypothesis, one of mathematics' most famous open problems dating to 1859. The hypothesis concerns the distribution of prime numbers and underpins broader mathematical work. Alpoge stated that the collaboration with Claude generated more than 250,000 pages of mathematical material, noting that Claude "far outpaced" him near the finish line. The proposed proof requires independent verification by other mathematicians before acceptance. Martin Bridson, president of the Clay Mathematics Institute (which offers $1 million for solutions to each of seven Millennium Prize Problems), called the announcement "of historic significance" but cautioned that "the journey from today's announcement to a place of real understanding will be long and difficult." The breakthrough is part of a broader pattern where AI has increasingly solved famous mathematical problems that occupied mathematicians for decades. The MIT News article from November 20, 2026, reports on a panel discussion titled "AI and Society: The Future of Math" where leading mathematicians discussed implications of AI's rapid mathematical productivity for human understanding, employment, and the intellectual pursuit of mathematics—a period some have termed the "mathocalypse."
