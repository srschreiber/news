# Sam's News — science — Week 2026-W32

## TL;DR
- 🔥🔥🔥◯◯ AI systems cracked major open math problems this week — OpenAI's unreleased Astra model solved 10+ Erdős problems, and an Anthropic mathematician used Claude Mythos 5 to disprove the 87-year-old Jacobian conjecture in 3D.
- 🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Jeff Dean and top Google AI researchers departed after 27 years to launch Discovery Loop, an AI-for-scientific-discovery startup.
- 🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Alzheimer's research advanced on two fronts: a hidden brain immune-cell shift starting around age 50 was identified as a likely driver, alongside two new Vanderbilt TAOK-inhibiting compounds as research tools.
- 🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Gene-edited hypoallergenic beagles were created by removing the allergen-producing gene, and PVC plastic was chemically converted into high-quality lubricant oil (Nature).
- 🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Blocking glycine transporter SLC6A20 reversed autism-related symptoms in adult mice and human organoids.
- 🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Arctic fires and geophysics made headlines: human activity roughly doubled Arctic tundra fire frequency, and Earth's molten iron core was found to have reversed direction beneath the Pacific.
- 🔥🔥◯◯◯ Spokane wildfires (65,000 evacuated) and H5N1 bird flu spreading into Australian wildlife underscored ongoing climate/disease pressures.

## Biggest stories

### AI cracks decades-old math problems, reshapes research
🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Two frontier models delivered rare mathematical breakthroughs this week: OpenAI's unreleased Astra solved at least 10 long-standing Erdős problems (Aug 3), and days later an Anthropic researcher used Claude Mythos 5 to find a counterexample disproving the 87-year-old Jacobian conjecture in three dimensions (Aug 6). Together with Jeff Dean's exit from Google after 27 years to found Discovery Loop, an AI-for-science startup, the period marks a visible acceleration of AI's role in original scientific discovery.
Sources: [2026-08-03](../../news/science/2026-08-03.md), [2026-08-06](../../news/science/2026-08-06.md)

### Alzheimer's and neurodegeneration research advances on multiple fronts
🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Across the week, several studies converged on Alzheimer's/neurodegeneration mechanisms: aging protein EPS8 was linked to toxic protein clumping in ALS/Huntington's (Aug 3); two new Vanderbilt compounds enabled selective control of TAOK proteins as Alzheimer's research tools (Aug 3); and researchers found that around age 50 the brain's memory center swaps longtime immune cells for inflammatory ones, a potential trigger for disease onset (Aug 7). A separate long-term study also tied smoking, high blood pressure, heart disease and high lipids to nearly half of dementia cases.
Sources: [2026-08-03](../../news/science/2026-08-03.md), [2026-08-06](../../news/science/2026-08-06.md), [2026-08-07](../../news/science/2026-08-07.md)

### Genetic engineering delivers practical fixes: allergy-free dogs, autism reversal in mice
🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Scientists produced gene-edited beagle puppies lacking the allergen-producing gene (Aug 5), and separately showed that blocking the glycine transporter SLC6A20 restored brain signaling and reversed social, communication and repetitive-behavior symptoms in autism mouse models and human organoids (Aug 7).
Sources: [2026-08-05](../../news/science/2026-08-05.md), [2026-08-07](../../news/science/2026-08-07.md)

### Climate stress mounts: Arctic fires, sinking coasts, wildfire evacuations
🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Satellite analysis found human activity has more than doubled Arctic tundra fire frequency, with Canadian wildfire smoke reaching Europe (Aug 5). Related findings this week: Spokane wildfires forced 65,000 evacuations (Aug 4); coastal cities are sinking (avg. 6mm/yr) nearly three times faster than seas rise; U.S. wetlands are drowning faster than they can migrate (Aug 3); and Canada continued adapting policy to a worsening wildfire crisis (Aug 8).
Sources: [2026-08-03](../../news/science/2026-08-03.md), [2026-08-04](../../news/science/2026-08-04.md), [2026-08-05](../../news/science/2026-08-05.md), [2026-08-08](../../news/science/2026-08-08.md)

### Earth's core reversal and PVC-to-lubricant chemistry
🔥🔥🔥◯◯ Satellite data showed Earth's molten iron core abruptly reversed direction beneath the Pacific (Aug 7), while Virginia Tech chemists published in Nature a process converting PVC plastic into polyalphaolefin, a key lubricant/engine-oil component (Aug 5) — both notable for upending established assumptions in geophysics and materials chemistry.
Sources: [2026-08-05](../../news/science/2026-08-05.md), [2026-08-07](../../news/science/2026-08-07.md)

## Themes & trends
- **AI-driven science is now routine news**: three separate items (Astra/Erdős, Claude/Jacobian, Discovery Loop launch) show frontier AI models and their creators pivoting explicitly toward scientific discovery, not just chatbots.
- **Neurodegeneration research cluster**: nearly every day featured an Alzheimer's, ALS, Huntington's, or autism mechanism study — a sustained wave of basic-science findings rather than one big breakthrough.
- **Climate change compounding effects**: coverage moved beyond simple warming to compounding, hard-to-reverse phenomena (subsidence outpacing sea-level rise, fires reaching new baselines, wetlands unable to keep pace).
- **Ancient/rare specimens reinterpreted**: recurring stories reassessed old assumptions using new techniques — Shroud of Turin DNA, dodo intelligence, rediscovered Megalodon fossils, Ötzi's microbiome, 518-million-year-old spider-fang fossil — a steady drumbeat of "history revised by modern methods."
- **Quantum/energy engineering incremental gains**: sunlight-powered quantum entanglement (reported twice, Aug 6 and Aug 8), flying-focus laser plasma accelerators, and fusion-reactor wall shielding point to steady applied-physics progress rather than single breakthroughs.
