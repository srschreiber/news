## Biggest stories

### Claude Opus 5 launch: cost-efficiency play against Fable 5
<span class="imp imp-4" title="Importance 4/5" aria-label="Importance 4 of 5"><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i class="on"></i><i></i></span> Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 at unchanged pricing ($5/M input, $25/M output tokens vs Opus 4.8), positioning it as roughly half the cost of rival Fable 5 ($10/$50 per million tokens). Benchmark claims varied by report: more than double Opus 4.8's Frontier-Bench v0.1 score (43.3% vs 18.7%), ahead of Fable 5 (33.7%); 3x the next-best rival on ARC-AGI 3; beats Fable 5 on OSWorld 2.0 at one-third the cost; within 0.5% of Fable 5 on CursorBench 3.2 at half the price; tops the Artificial Analysis Index at 61 points. Features include a new "effort dial" (low/medium/high) to trade cost for capability, a fast mode (~2.5x speed at double price), a 1M-token context window, May 2026 knowledge cutoff, and training without offensive cyber capabilities (cutting safety interventions ~85% vs Fable 5). Now the default model on Claude Max and the strongest tier on Claude Pro.

## Themes & trends
- **Price-performance war:** Anthropic's Opus 5 messaging centers entirely on matching or beating Fable 5 at a fraction of the cost, signaling competitive pressure to commoditize frontier-model pricing rather than chase raw capability alone.
- **Safety vs. capability tension:** Opus 5 was trained without offensive cyber capabilities to cut safety interventions, yet within a day researchers found a sandbox-escape vulnerability — highlighting a recurring gap between stated safety design and real-world robustness.
- **Access expansion:** Alongside the flagship model push, Anthropic broadened access through free education/certification programs for underserved regions.
